## Concept
The question is "Why does NaOCl in a canal need to be refreshed?" The reel answers it in three visual steps:
1. **Mechanism.** The worked equation NaOCl + H₂O ⇌ NaOH + HOCl sits in the bottom band from about 2.3 s. Inside the
   magnifier, NaOCl reacts with pulp tissue in Estrela et al.'s three reactions. At each reaction a glowing particle
   flies from the lens down to the product it used up. A persistent chip under that product counts it: **OH⁻ ↓**,
   then **OH⁻ ↓↓**, then **free chlorine ↓**.
2. **Consequence.** A small unit-free toy-model graph shows free chlorine near the tissue. In "left alone" it runs out:
   the canal greys, the lens empties, and the tissue stops shrinking during a short hold at zero. In "refreshed often"
   it comes back in a sawtooth (partial exchange) and the tissue keeps shrinking.
3. **Payoff.** A bracket joins the two chips and points into the boxed answer **"Tissue & dentin use it up →
   refresh often."** Fine print underneath says that agitation and concentration matter too.

Colour code, used the same way throughout the illustration, magnifier, captions, graph and equation:
chartreuse = NaOCl / HOCl / free available chlorine · cyan = NaOH / OH⁻ · coral = tissue (fat, amino acid, protein)
· lavender = Na⁺ / salt · white = products and neutral.

**Toy model (graph, tint, lens dots, tissue size).** dC/dt = −k₁·C·S − k₀·C and dS/dt = −k₂·C·S, with k₁ = 1.6,
k₀ = 0.08, k₂ = 0.55 in arbitrary time units (0–5 across the axis). C = free chlorine relative to fresh and S = tissue
remaining. "Refreshed" is a partial exchange at τ = 1, 2, 3, 4: C ← C + 0.85·(1 − C). Values: left alone, C = 0.24,
0.07, 0.02 at τ = 1, 2, 3, and S stalls at 0.68. Refreshed, C after each reset is ≈ 0.89–0.92, and S at τ = 5 is
0.19. The parameters are arbitrary and chosen only to show the qualitative behaviour. **No time unit or exchange
interval is implied.** The displayed C is eased over ~0.15 s so nothing on screen jumps.

## Corrections and refinements to the brief
1. **"NaOH + fatty acids → soap + glycerol"** is chemically loose. Free fatty acids + NaOH give soap + water. Glycerol is
   released only when **fats (triglycerides)** are saponified. The reel shows *fat + NaOH → soap + glycerol* and draws a
   triglyceride.
2. **"Each reaction consumes free available chlorine"** is not accurate. Saponification and amino-acid neutralization use
   **hydroxide** (alkalinity, so pH falls, per Estrela). Chloramination and oxidation use **free available chlorine**.
   The chips show exactly this (OH⁻ ↓↓ vs free chlorine ↓).
3. **Moorer & Wesselink 1982 and temperature:** the PubMed record (PMID 6964523) has no abstract. Its MeSH terms are
   Hydrogen-Ion Concentration, Rheology, Surface Properties, Necrosis, Rabbits and Sodium Hypochlorite, with **no
   temperature term**. This supports the decision not to repeat the brief's claim that the paper covered temperature.
   It is cited (under the graph) only for chlorine consumption and frequent exchange, via the 2022 review. The scope
   suggested by the MeSH terms (pH, agitation/flow, surface) matches the "agitation & concentration" fine print, which
   is itself sourced to Stojicic 2010.
4. **HOCl vs OCl⁻:** at pH 11–12, OCl⁻ is almost all of the free chlorine, and the hydrolysis lies far to the left.
   The equation is drawn with a long reverse harpoon and the note "free chlorine is mostly OCl⁻". The chip under
   HOCl says **free chlorine ↓**, not "HOCl ↓", because as HOCl is consumed it is re-formed from OCl⁻.
5. **Dentin also consumes chlorine** (Macedo 2010; Tejada 2019; Boutsioukis 2022). The answer now says "Tissue & dentin
   use it up".
6. **Time scale:** the earlier 0–5 min axis implied chlorine is gone in ~2–3 min. That contradicts Ragnarsson 2015, so
   the axis is now unit-free.
7. **Needle depth:** the earlier needle stopped ~56% of the way down the canal while irrigant refreshed all the way to the
   apex, which contradicts Boutsioukis 2010. The needle now reaches ~1 mm short of the terminus.

## Design rationale
- **Typography:** Source Serif 4 is used for the question, captions, chemistry, the answer and the handle byline (italic
  24 px, 75% grey, right-aligned on the question's second line). EB Garamond italic is used for the *Assumed* line.
  A small custom renderer draws the subscripts, superscripts, arrows (→, ⇌ and the left-lying ⇌) and ↓ arrows. The
  vendored font subsets lack these glyphs, and fallbacks would look mismatched.
- **Persistence over transience:** the "what was used" information lives in chips that stay to the end and become the
  bracket into the answer. They replace the old ~1 s tag line.
- **One focal point at a time:** the reveals are sequenced (tooth → needle/fill → equation → magnifier). The empty graph
  frame waits at 30% and brightens only when its data starts. Captions crossfade without overlap. The lens hands off
  from tissue to molecules with a zoom-in, never a double exposure. The end beats are sawtooth → bracket → short rest
  → answer.
- **Shown, not told:** the canal tint greys when chlorine is depleted and eases back to chartreuse on each refresh. The
  lens dots vanish and return in sync with the graph. During the hold, the tissue stops moving.
- **Loop:** frame 0 is the question, the handle and a 20% ghost of the tooth. The last 0.5 s fades back to exactly that.
- **Safe areas:** readable content sits within x 78–1010 and y 270–1580. Nothing readable is in the right-hand button
  column (x > 960, y > 1100). The answer box spans x ≤ 956 and y 1480–1543, with its fine print at 1572.

## Known weaknesses
- Atom letters in the lens are 18–21 px (decorative; the meaning is in the 30 px+ captions). Fine print (citations,
  *toy model*, *not to scale*, equation note) is 22–24 px.
- The chips use ↓ to mean "decreases", like "pH ↓". In formal chemical notation ↓ after a formula means precipitate.
  The chips sit apart from the formulae to avoid that reading, but a chemist might still notice it.
- The reel is 27.5 s, longer than the brief's 20–24 s, because readSecs holds were prioritised.
- The particle path from the lens to the chips briefly crosses the caption text (0.6 s).
