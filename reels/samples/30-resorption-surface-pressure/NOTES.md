# 30 · Resorption 2/5 · External surface + pressure resorption — RISOGRAPH DUOTONE PRINT

## Concept
Two kinds of external resorption that stop when their cause stops. Printed as three two-color riso "sheets"
(fluorescent pink + teal, overprint = dark third tone, halftone tints, grain, slight misregistration) pulled off a stack.
A CAUSE switch -> CLASTS dial on sheets 1 and 2 shows the same thing for both kinds: cause off, clasts stop (with a short lag).

## On-screen claims and sources
All sources were checked by the research and fact-check stages (RESEARCH.md); the review round added caption-only sources (below).
1. Small localized injury -> small, shallow resorption of cementum; self-limiting. Abbott PV, Lin S. Dent Traumatol 2022;38:267-285 (PMID 35605161), full text section 6.1.
2. No infection -> does not persist; repaired by new cementum ("cementoid-like tissue ... about 4 weeks": Lin S et al. Dent Traumatol 2022;38:253-266, PMID 35559593, full text; "a few weeks": Abbott & Lin 2022). No timed human study, so the clock has no numerals. Source line: "Lin; Abbott & Lin, Dent Traumatol 2022" (companion Part 1 / Part 2).
3. Pressure is the stimulus that keeps resorption going (orthodontic treatment, impacted tooth, tumor). Fuss Z, Tsesis I, Lin S. Dent Traumatol 2003;19:175-82 (PMID 12848710), abstract.
4. Pressure removed -> resorption stops, cemental repair follows; resorption is irreversible (root length does not return). Patel S, Saberi N, Pimental T, Teng PH. Int Endod J 2022;55 Suppl 4:892-921 (PMID 35229320), full text (relevant sections) + abstract.
5. "No root canal treatment. Pulp is not the cause." Patel et al. 2022, Table 1 and text (no signs of endodontic disease; managed by eliminating the etiological factor); Abbott & Lin 2022 ("Orthodontic resorption itself does not cause pulp pathosis"). The literal "endodontic therapy is not indicated" is the AAE Communique 2021 (web article, not cited on screen).
6. Surface resorption: usually not evident on a radiograph. Abbott & Lin 2022, section 6.1.
7. Orthodontic: shortened, blunted, rounded apex. Abbott & Lin 2022 section 6.6; Patel et al. 2022.
8. "Remove the cause." sits under the PRESSURE panel only: removal of the pressure is the treatment of choice (Fuss 2003, abstract; Patel 2022). Surface resorption: "no indication for any management" (Abbott & Lin 2022, 6.1), so nothing is printed under that panel.

## Caption-only statements and their sources (all PubMed-verified, abstracts unless stated)
- Arrest is not instant: resorptive activity "in some teeth" up to 10 days after force removal (Winter 2009, Eur J Orthod, PMID 19465737, intruded premolars); "root resorption continued for 4 weeks after orthodontic force stopped" (Cheng 2009, AJODO, PMID 19732664, premolars, micro-CT).
- Not always: 7 of 32 lesions progressed in 27 followed patients (Falahat 2008, Angle Orthod, PMID 18298199).
- Advanced pressure defects can reach the pulp: 60% of 58 resorbed lateral incisors graded "severe with pulpal involvement" (Ericson & Kurol 2000, Angle Orthod, PMID 11138644; CT, 107 referred children).
- Pulp testing during orthodontics: EPT threshold rises immediately, negative responses peak at 2 months, thresholds still raised at the end of active treatment; "thermal testing may be more reliable" (Alomari 2011, Int Endod J, PMID 21366625). Systematic review of 13 observational studies, peak at about 2-3 months, low certainty (Nangia 2025, Int Orthod, PMID 39536681).
- Typical orthodontic shortening: pooled 0.79 mm on CBCT (0.86 mm at end of treatment), low-quality studies (Samandara 2019, Eur J Orthod, PMID 29771300).
- Naming differences: Fuss 2003 (pressure resorption, used here), Abbott & Lin 2022 (pressure and orthodontic as separate types), Patel 2022 (all under external surface resorption).

## Schematic / not literal
- Cells, their size, number and timing; vessel-to-surface recruitment; the starburst "injury".
- Clast size on the whole-tooth sheet and the amount of root lost (about 19% of length) are exaggerated for visibility (typical mean is under 1 mm; caption says so).
- The stop is drawn as a wind-down of under a second; in reality the lag is days to weeks and some lesions progress - caption only.
- The intrusion arrow is one example of orthodontic force. The impacted tooth "removed" is drawn as sliding out of the picture.
- Radiographs are icons: pink pulp = living pulp, not radiographic density (a canal is radiolucent on a real film).
- "schematic · not to scale" is on every sheet inside the safe area.

## Deliberately left off screen (see RESEARCH.md "Do not claim")
No healing time in numbers, no stats (Ericson & Kurol, Lund go in the caption or nowhere), no "never visible",
no "pulp never involved", no "pulp tests normal" on screen (unreliable during active orthodontics; caption explains), no apex regrowth, no bacteria.

## Design rationale
Pink = activity (clasts, force, cause ON, living pulp); teal = tissue and type; overprint dark = new cementum, nuclei, emphasis.
Small text is teal only so misregistration never blurs it. Files: index.html, riso.js (print pipeline), reel.js (content).
