# 22 — How an electronic apex locator works (the electrical science) — evidence brief

Research stage only (nothing built). Literature located and checked through PubMed (abstracts, and PMC full text where
stated), Crossref / J-STAGE bibliographic records, one scanned 1942 primary paper, and one manufacturer instruction
manual. Every PubMed-indexed source below carries its PMID and DOI link. "Verified from" says exactly what was read.

Legend for "verified from": **abstract** = PubMed abstract read; **full text** = PMC / publisher full text read;
**secondary full text** = the statement was read in the full text of a *different* paper that cites the primary;
**manufacturer IFU** = J. Morita ROOT ZX II operation instructions (text extracted and read);
**record only** = bibliographic record confirmed, content not readable; **NOT VERIFIED** = leave off screen.

> **Fact-check status (adversarial review, 2026-11-22).** Every PMID, citation, DOI and quoted number in the tables was
> re-checked against PubMed (metadata + abstract) and, where stated, PMC full text (Custer 1918, Rambo 2010, Nasiri &
> Wrbas 2022, Gehlot 2016, Alothmani & Siddiqui 2023, Tampelini 2017). Bibliographic details were all correct. The
> substantive changes are: the accuracy range (item 7 / A7) was a hand-picked subset and has been widened; "~6.5 kΩ",
> the Rambo ratio numbers and "the only way out" were taken off the on-screen list; a mis-attribution about the
> Root ZX II (D7) and an omitted contrary finding (A12) were fixed. See the **Fact-check log** at the end of this file.
> Rows A14-A25 and the Oishi 2002 / Kim 2000 sources were added by the fact-checker.

## (b) Recommended on-screen facts (5-8, safe wording)

*(List revised at fact-check — see the log at the end. Items 1, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 and 8 were re-worded; nothing below should
be put on screen in the first-draft wording.)*

1. **"The patient is the circuit."** File → canal fluid → foramen → periodontal ligament → lip clip. Dentin and cementum
   insulate; the foramen is the way out. **Not "the only way out"**: the abstract does not say "only", and lateral
   canals, cracks, perforations and dentin itself conduct (L3; correction 7). Keep the "schematic" tag on this scene.
   *(Nekoofar 2006, Int Endod J — abstract; lip electrode: Nasiri & Wrbas 2022 — full text)*
2. **"Canal = resistor + capacitor"** (must carry the word **"schematic"**). *(Nekoofar 2006; Meredith & Gulabivala 1997)*
3. **"Two AC signals. The device reads their ratio."** plus, with the device named, **"Root ZX: 0.4 kHz + 8 kHz"**.
   The ratio principle is Kobayashi & Suda, J Endod 1994 (abstract). The two frequencies are NOT in that abstract: they
   are verified for the Root ZX in Oishi et al., J Endod 2002 (abstract, same Tokyo group) and in three later full
   texts (C6). So never print "0.4 kHz + 8 kHz" as a bare fact under a "Kobayashi & Suda 1994" source line — add
   "Root ZX" (or "e.g.") on screen and list Oishi 2002 in NOTES/meta.json for the numbers.
4. **"The ratio stays nearly flat, then drops sharply in the last ~1 mm"** (steepest in the final 0.5 mm), with the
   on-screen tag "in vivo, 21 canals". **No number on screen** (the "0.7 → 0.45" option was removed at fact-check: it
   is a research prototype's 8 kHz / 250 Hz amplitude ratio, not the Root ZX's own quotient, and the "foramen" position
   was set by a Root ZX — see C7/C8). The numbers may go in the caption/NOTES with all those qualifiers.
   *(Rambo 2010, Biomed Eng Online — full text)*
5. **"A ratio largely ignores what is in the canal"** — meaning conductive fluids (blood, saline, NaOCl); wording
   "largely", not "cancels". Do NOT show a dry canal as "ratio holds": the Root ZX was more often unstable in
   low-conductivity canals in vivo (Venturi & Breschi 2005) and the manufacturer says to moisten an extremely dry canal
   (C11, L5). The abstract behind the claim does not state its study design, so no "in vivo"/"in vitro" tag from it.
   *(Kobayashi & Suda 1994: "only negligibly influenced by the electrolyte" — abstract)*
6. **"It senses the foramen region. The scale is not millimeters."** ("finds the foramen" was softened to match D1:
   whether the 0.5 mark is the constriction is disputed, and Nekoofar 2006 itself speaks of the *minor* apical foramen.)
   "Not millimeters" is the manufacturer's statement for one device (ROOT ZX II instructions — not a PubMed source);
   record it that way in NOTES. *(manufacturer instructions, ROOT ZX II; Ounsi & Naaman, Int Endod J 1999 — in vitro,
   abstract; Rambo 2010 — full text)*
7. **"In vivo: about 70-100% of readings within ±0.5 mm"** with the qualifier "Root ZX, small studies — not pooled"
   (16 abstracts, 1996-2023, most with 11-40 teeth; landmark foramen or constriction; exact range 72-100%; A7).
   **The first-draft "75-96% (5 small studies)" was removed**: it is the range of five hand-picked 1996-2005 papers,
   while other in vivo Root ZX studies report 72% (Stöber 2011) and 100% (Vieyra 2010; De-Deus 2023). If the builder
   prefers a non-numeric line, "most readings land within ±0.5 mm (small in vivo studies)" is also supported.
   *(lowest: Stöber et al., J Endod 2011; highest: Vieyra et al., Int Endod J 2010 and De-Deus et al., J Endod 2023;
   classic: Shabahang 1996, Dunlap 1998, Pagavino 1998, Welk 2003, Tselnik 2005 — all abstracts)*
8. **History strip (silkscreen):** "1918 Custer: electrical method · 1962 Sunada: DC resistance · AC impedance ·
   1994 Kobayashi & Suda: ratio method". **"(~6.5 kΩ)" was removed from the strip at fact-check** (secondary-only
   number, H3). If Suzuki is shown: "1942 Suzuki" only (no claim text), because his paper is an iontophoresis study and
   the "constant resistance" reading of it is secondary. Do not write "first" next to 1994: a two-signal
   "relative value" device (Endex) is already described in 1990 (H5).

Single best source line for the mechanism scenes: `Kobayashi & Suda, J Endod 1994 · Rambo et al., Biomed Eng Online 2010`.
Payoff qualifier worth keeping on screen: **"confirm with a radiograph"** (Martins 2014 SR; manufacturer IFU).

Word budget: items 1-7 can be said in about 55 must-read words; put pacemakers, limitations and the constriction-versus-
foramen nuance in the caption.

- Sourced, usable: canal series resistance in vivo 7.5-9 kΩ (NaOCl), 9-12 kΩ (water), 22-92 kΩ (dry) *(Meredith &

   the number on screen** (the first draft allowed it "with medium confidence"); in the caption it may appear only as
   "about 6.5 kΩ, as quoted in later papers". **"40 µA" is not verified — do not show.**

   fact-check; Nasiri & Wrbas 2022 also call it the first proposal). Add it to the strip as "1918 Custer: electrical
   method" — it was a working DC/milliammeter technique on single-rooted teeth, not just an idea; avoid the bare word
   "first" on screen (priority claims cannot be proven from PubMed).
4. **"quotient value at the constriction"** — a Root ZX quotient of **0.67 could not be verified**; another paper citing
   the same primary gives **0.72 at 0.5 mm short**. Do not print either. A verified in vivo alternative is in C8.
5. **"why two frequencies cancel the effect of electrolytes"** — overstated. Primary wording: "only negligibly
   influenced". Irrigant type still shifts precision in a meta-analysis (Tsesis 2015) and overflowing fluid still
   short-circuits the reading (IFU). Use "largely compensates".
6. **"accuracy from systematic reviews (within ±0.5 mm percentages; Martins 2014, Tsesis 2015)"** — neither abstract
   reports a pooled ±0.5 mm percentage. Martins 2014 is a qualitative EAL-versus-radiograph review (21 articles);
   Tsesis 2015 pools mean file-tip-to-constriction distances (10 studies, 1105 measurements). The ±0.5 mm percentages
   come from individual in vivo studies. **Corrected at fact-check:** A1-A5 give 75-96%, but that is a five-paper
   subset; with eleven further in vivo Root ZX-family abstracts (A14-A24) the range is 72-100% (A7). Use "about
   70-100%, small studies, not pooled".
7. **"dentin = insulator tube"** — fine as a schematic, but dentin is not a perfect insulator (Suzuki 1942, per my reading of the paper's Japanese summary, found the current through the root wall of freshly
   extracted human teeth to be several times that through the foramen — this reading of a Japanese scan was NOT
   re-verified at fact-check and must not be cited in the caption; Krizaj 2004 models dentin conductance, abstract
   verified). Keep "schematic", and say "the way out", not "the only way out".
8. **"the 0.5 mark is not a distance in mm"** — correct, and confirmed in the manufacturer's own instructions. But the
   same instructions say the 0.5 reading means the tip "is in the apical constriction", which independent studies dispute
   (see D1). Say "not millimeters"; do not say "0.5 = constriction".
9. **"multi-frequency ratio" as the end of the story** — more frequencies did not beat two: the five-frequency Endo
   Analyzer located the minor diameter in 34.4% vs 90.7% for the two-frequency Root ZX (Welk 2003), and a review found no
   accuracy difference between 3rd-6th "generations" (Nasiri & Wrbas 2022). Avoid implying a ladder of ever-better
   devices.
10. **Pacemakers** — Garofalo 2002 is in vitro with one pacemaker and one of five EALs *did* interfere; Wilson 2006 is in
    vivo (27 patients, no interference). Add the dissenting in vivo study (Moraes 2016) and the fact that the
    manufacturer label still prohibits use. Keep it in the caption, phrased conservatively.

## (d) Controversies and caveats (what must not be overstated)

- **D1. Constriction or foramen?** Manufacturer: 0.5 reading = constriction, APEX = major foramen. Supporting in vivo
  data: Root ZX within ±0.5 mm of the minor diameter/constriction in 75-97% (Dunlap 1998, Welk 2003, Tselnik 2005; and,
  added at fact-check, Haffner 2005 78%, Wrbas 2007 75%, Kim 2008 84%, Swapna 2015 96.6% — A14-A16, A22). Against: Ounsi &
  Naaman 1999 (use only the APEX reading), Hoer & Attin 2004 (constriction found in only 51-64%; minor-to-major zone in
  81-82%), Rambo 2010 (only the 0 mm reading is electrically reliable). Many clinicians therefore advance to APEX and
  withdraw 0.5-1 mm (described in Rambo 2010). The reel should say the device senses the **foramen region** and that the
  scale is not millimeters, and stop there.
- **D2. The apical constriction is not always present or where expected** (Dummer 1984 ranges; primary teeth and resorbed
  apices lack one — Vitali 2022, Int Endod J, PMID 35808837: 69.31% within ±0.5 mm in primary teeth, very low certainty).
- **D3. The equivalent circuit.** One resistor plus one capacitor is a teaching model. Published models use series and
  parallel R-C networks and constant-phase elements; the frequency dependence comes partly from the file/electrolyte
  interface, not only from "the tooth as a capacitor".
- **D4. Accuracy numbers** come mostly from small studies (11-40 teeth; two larger series of 482 and 693 canals come
  from one group), mostly teeth scheduled for extraction, with different reference landmarks and tolerances. Published
  in vivo ±0.5 mm figures for the Root ZX family run from 72% to 100% with hand files (A7) and down to 50% with rotary
  NiTi files (A25); an in vitro model even gave 17-53% (Alothmani & Siddiqui 2023, full text), and a literature
  summary quoted in that paper gives 50-100% across study designs. Any single percentage is therefore a choice; systematic reviews rate the evidence base as short and at considerable risk of
  bias (Martins 2014) or very low certainty (Kaur 2024). ElAyouti et al. 2022 (Int Endod J, PMID 35347726) criticize the
  heterogeneity of working-length study methods. Report a range, name the device, never "9x% accurate" unqualified.
- **D5. "Generation" taxonomy** is marketing-adjacent and disputed (Nekoofar 2006). Later devices are not shown to be
  more accurate (A10).
- **D6. Pacemakers/ICDs.** Labels prohibit; most bench and clinical studies show no clinically relevant interference at
  clinical distances; one small in vivo study recorded noise and pauses under provoked conditions. Not a settled "safe".
- **D7. Brand dependence.** Almost all verified mechanism and accuracy data concern the Root ZX family. Other devices
  are described with different signal processing — e.g. the Propex Pixi "uses multiple frequencies, in addition to
  calculating the root mean square (RMS) values of the electric signals", and the Elements unit compares separate
  resistance and capacitance measurements with a database (both Gehlot 2016, full text re-read); Vasconcelos 2014
  (abstract) calls Root ZX and Propex II "different operation systems". Manufacturers do not publish full algorithms.
  Describe "the ratio method", not "all apex locators".
  **Corrected at fact-check:** the first draft said the *Root ZX II* is described as using "mean square roots of the
  impedances" (Tampelini 2017, PMC5553019). That sentence does exist in Tampelini's introduction, but it assigns the
  simple impedance ratio to the Propex II and the RMS calculation to the Root ZX II — the reverse of how the Propex
  family and the Root ZX are described elsewhere (Gehlot 2016; Kobayashi & Suda 1994; Oishi 2002). It looks like a
  transposition in a single secondary source. Do not repeat it; do not say or imply that the Root ZX II departs from
  the ratio method.
