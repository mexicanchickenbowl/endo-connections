## How the chapter itself summarizes these factors
Heading: "Summary of Factors Influencing Periapical Healing Following Nonsurgical Root Canal Treatment".
Lead sentence (short quote): "The following factors are considered as having a major impact on periapical health
subsequent to root canal treatment". Seven bullets follow, in this order — **the same seven, in the same order, as the
resident's list.** The chapter then lists factors with "minimal impact": age, gender, tooth morphologic type, and the
specific treatment protocol and technique. It closes: "all factors that have a strong influence on periapical health
after root canal treatment are associated in some way with a persistent root canal infection."

## What the chapter says about the culture test
- It was once a condition for obturation in some centers: obturation "would only be acceptable after a negative
  culture test was obtained from the canal".
- "This practice has fallen out of clinical favor because of the perceived predictability and good prognosis of root
  canal treatment without microbiologic sampling." Sampling is described as lengthy, difficult and often inaccurate,
  needing laboratory support, with a low benefit-to-cost ratio.
- Still prognostic: "A preobturation negative culture result may increase treatment success twofold (Fig. 17.19)".
  One large study (chapter ref. 201, Seltzer, Bender & Turkenkopf 1963) "helped contribute to the demise of the
  canal-culture test; however, even this study showed a 10% difference in success in favor of the negative culture
  test when periapical disease was present."
- "Regardless of the technique used for obtaining a sample for a canal culture, the presence of a negative culture
  seems to have a positive impact on treatment outcome."
- Elsewhere: the culture test "has fallen out of favor in contemporary practice".
So "a prognostic factor, though not routine practice" is a fair on-screen summary.

## Cross-check with the primary papers (PubMed; abstracts only — full texts were not read)
1. **Ng Y-L, Mann V, Gulabivala K.** A prospective study of the factors affecting outcomes of nonsurgical root canal
   treatment: part 1: periapical health. *Int Endod J* 2011;44(7):583-609. PMID 21366626.
   [doi:10.1111/j.1365-2591.2011.01872.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2591.2011.01872.x). According to the PubMed
   abstract: prospective, postgraduate students, 2–4 years of follow-up; complete periapical healing in 83% of roots
   after primary and 80% after secondary treatment; **eleven** prognostic factors: no preoperative periapical lesion
   (P = 0.003); smaller lesion (P ≤ 0.001); no preoperative sinus tract (P = 0.001); patency at the canal terminus
   (P = 0.001); "extension of canal cleaning as close as possible to its apical terminus" (P = 0.001); EDTA as a
   penultimate wash in retreatment (P = 0.002); not using 2% chlorhexidine as an adjunct to NaOCl (P = 0.01);
   "absence of tooth/root perforation (P = 0.06)"; no interappointment flare-up (P = 0.002); no root-filling
   extrusion (P ≤ 0.001); satisfactory coronal restoration (P ≤ 0.001).
   - **Agrees with the chapter on:** lesion presence and size (1), patency (2), apical extent of cleaning (3),
     perforation (5), coronal restoration (7).
   - **Differences:** the 2011 abstract lists four factors the chapter's seven-item summary does not (sinus tract,
     irrigant findings, flare-up, root-filling extrusion). The chapter discusses them in the body text. The abstract
     does **not** list the culture test (4) or radiographic root-filling quality (6); those two rest on the pooled
     analyses (Figs. 17.19 and 17.23). Perforation is reported with P = 0.06 in the abstract (borderline), while the
     chapter summary states "reduces the odds of success by 30%". The abstract says "tooth/root perforation"; the
     chapter summary says "iatrogenic perforation". The "twofold" (patency) and "30%" (perforation) figures are the
     chapter's; they are not in the abstract.
2. **Ng Y-L, Mann V, Rahbaran S, Lewsey J, Gulabivala K.** Outcome of primary root canal treatment: systematic review
   of the literature — Part 2. Influence of clinical factors. *Int Endod J* 2008;41(1):6-31. PMID 17931388.
   [doi:10.1111/j.1365-2591.2007.01323.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2591.2007.01323.x). According to the PubMed
   abstract: 63 studies; four conditions significantly improved outcome: "pre-operative absence of periapical
   radiolucency, root filling with no voids, root filling extending to 2 mm within the radiographic apex and
   satisfactory coronal restoration".
   - **Agrees with the chapter on:** factors 1, 3 (as root-filling extent, 0–2 mm within the radiographic apex),
     6 (no voids) and 7. This is chapter ref. 153, the source of the flush/short/long categories.
   - The abstract does not mention culture results, patency or perforation.
3. **Sjögren U, Figdor D, Persson S, Sundqvist G.** Influence of infection at the time of root filling on the outcome
   of endodontic treatment of teeth with apical periodontitis. *Int Endod J* 1997;30(5):297-306. PMID 9477818.
   [doi:10.1046/j.1365-2591.1997.00092.x](https://doi.org/10.1046/j.1365-2591.1997.00092.x). According to the PubMed
   abstract: 55 single-rooted teeth with apical periodontitis, one-visit treatment, 5-year follow-up; complete
   periapical healing in 94% with a negative culture at root filling vs 68% with a positive culture (significant).
   It is one of the 15 studies in the chapter's Fig. 17.19. **Agrees** with factor 4.
4. **Seltzer S, Bender IB, Turkenkopf S.** Factors affecting successful repair after root canal therapy. *J Am Dent
   Assoc* 1963;67:651-662. PMID 14054365.
   [doi:10.14219/jada.archive.1963.0349](https://doi.org/10.14219/jada.archive.1963.0349). Chapter ref. 201 (the
   "large study" that contributed to the demise of the culture test). No abstract on PubMed; existence and citation
   verified only.
