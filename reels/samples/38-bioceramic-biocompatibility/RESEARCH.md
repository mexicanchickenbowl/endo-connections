## (c) Corrections to the brief

1. **Albrektsson & Johansson 2001 does not give the "ectopic bone" criterion** (at least not in its abstract). Its definition of osteoinduction is broad: recruitment of immature cells and their stimulation into preosteoblasts, "regularly seen in any type of bone healing". The strict heterotopic/ectopic-site test comes from the biomaterials literature (Urist 1965; Barradas et al. 2011; Habibovic & de Groot, J Tissue Eng Regen Med 2007;1:25-32, PMID 18038389, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/term.5) — background review of osteoinductive biomaterials; **its abstract does not state the heterotopic-site criterion**, so cite Barradas 2011 for the criterion). Cite both, and say which definition the stamp refers to. Citation detail: Eur Spine J 2001;**10 Suppl 2**:S96-101.
2. **The decisive paper is missing from the brief**: Moretton et al. 2000 implanted MTA subcutaneously and intraosseously in rats and concluded in so many words that MTA is "not osteoinductive" but is "osteoconductive". It answers the reel's question directly and should be the on-screen source for beat 3.
3. **"Giraud et al. 2019 Dent Mater on calcium silicate sealers and PDL cells" is wrong.** Giraud T et al. Dent Mater 2019;35:24-35 (PMID 30269862, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dental.2018.09.008)) is a narrative review of **pulp-capping** materials and the inflammation/regeneration balance (resin-containing TheraCal vs resin-free Biodentine). The sealer/PDL-cell papers from the same group are Camps et al. J Endod 2015;41:1469-73 and Jeanneau, Giraud et al. J Endod 2019;45:1016-23 (row 31).
4. **Zanini et al. 2012 did not use dental pulp stem cells.** It used an immortalised **murine** pulp cell line (OD-21). The abstract does not name RUNX2/DSPP etc. ("several genes"). The PubMed MeSH indexing of the paper lists Core Binding Factor Alpha 1 Subunit (Runx2), Sp7 (osterix), osteocalcin and collagen type I, which suggests those were among the genes assayed — but indexing terms are not results and give no direction of change. Do not attribute a specific gene list to it on screen.
5. **Peng et al. 2011** used human dental pulp **cells** exposed to tricalcium silicate **extract** (pure Ca3SiO5, not a commercial cement). Markers verified: DSPP, DMP-1, osteocalcin, ALP, collagen I. RUNX2 and BMP-2 are **not** in this abstract. BMP-2 up-regulation is verified for **PDL cells + MTA** (Maeda 2010), not for pulp stem cells.
6. **"Silicon ion release up-regulating markers"** — the verified endodontic mechanism paper (Maeda 2010) implicates **calcium** via the calcium-sensing receptor (CaCl2 reproduced it, MgCl2 did not). A specific role for Si ions from endodontic cements was not verified in this search (a second-pass search on silicon/silicate ions + pulp/PDL cells + calcium silicate returned 54 records; the ones opened were general cytocompatibility/differentiation studies of commercial cements that do not isolate a silicon effect). What *is* verified is that Si from these cements is taken up into adjacent dentin in vitro (Han & Okiji 2013, row 22). Keep Si off screen or label it "proposed"; show Ca²⁺ as the signal.
7. **Sanz systematic reviews**: three relevant ones, all of in vitro studies — Materials 2019 (DPSCs, 37 studies), Dent Mater 2021 (signalling pathways, 13 studies), Int Endod J 2021 (PDL stem cells, cements and sealers, 20 studies). None is evidence of in vivo osteoinduction.
8. **Fresh vs set cytotoxicity is not a clean story.** Torabinejad 1995 tested both and ranked MTA favourably in both states; Balto 2004 found no PDL-fibroblast attachment to fresh MTA; but Camilleri et al. (Int Endod J 2004;37:699-704, PMID 15347295, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2591.2004.00859.x)) found 1-day-cured MTA supported a confluent Saos-2 monolayer while **28-day-cured** samples were *less* biocompatible; and in dogs fresh MTA gave cementum in 12/12 vs 8/12 for set MTA (Apaydin 2004). Do not show "fresh = toxic, set = safe" as a rule.
9. **"Mild early inflammation settles"** — in rat subcutaneous tissue the initial reaction to MTA was described as **severe** with coagulation necrosis (Moretton 2000) or mild-to-moderate (Gomes-Filho 2009), subsiding with time. "Early inflammation that subsides" is safe; "mild" is not universally true. **[FC]** Nor is "settles/resolves": in Moretton 2000 the subcutaneous reaction to MTA was still "mostly moderate" at 60 days, and the EBA cement comparator did better there (moderate → mild).
10. **Aeinehchi 2003** is a self-described preliminary report with 14 teeth analysed — fine for the caption, too weak for an on-screen number. Nair 2008 (33 teeth, RCT, light + electron microscopy) is the stronger human histology source.
11. **Extruded sealer**: Ricucci et al. 2016 (J Endod 2016;42:243-9, PMID 26725179, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.joen.2015.11.020)) studied only conventional sealers (ZOE, AH Plus, Apexit, Endomethasone) — it cannot be cited for calcium silicate sealers. For those, the evidence is mixed (row 45). **[FC]** "Mixed" itself needs care: the one trial that found lower success with extrusion (Kim 2022) analysed extrusion across both of its arms (AH Plus and a calcium silicate sealer), so it is not evidence about calcium silicate sealer specifically, and the effect was gone at 5 years (Seog 2025).
17. **[FC] The Baek 2010 distances do not show osteoconduction.** The brief asked for "osteoconduction evidence" and the earlier passes offered "new bone 0.40 mm from MTA vs 1.29 mm from amalgam". A gap of about one PDL width is the opposite of bone on the surface; at a root end the tissue on the material is cementum. Osteoconduction evidence comes from materials implanted in bone (Torabinejad 1998; Moretton 2000; rows 52-53).
18. **[FC] "Calcium ... ion release up-regulating ALP, osteocalcin, BMP-2, RUNX2, DSPP"** — as a single mechanism this is not supported. Calcium is tied to BMP-2 only (Maeda 2010, PDL cells); ALP/osteocalcin/DSPP came from an extract experiment that did not isolate an ion (Peng 2011); RUNX2 appears in the verified abstracts only as one of the markers assayed in a bone-marrow stem cell study (Margunato 2015, row 46), with no ion link.
19. **[FC] Moretton 2000 is not "the only heterotopic test"** in the literal sense — many rat subcutaneous studies exist; it is the only one found that explicitly reports the presence or absence of bone at the non-bony site (see the third-pass note under the table, and rows 52-53).
12. **Bioactivity ≠ osteoinduction ≠ "bonding" proven in patients.** Apatite formation is demonstrated in phosphate-containing solutions and extracted teeth. The claim that this layer is *the basis* of clinical bonding/sealing is the authors' attribution (Sarkar 2005), not a demonstrated clinical fact.
13. Scope note: antibacterial (bacteriostatic/bactericidal) properties belong to reel 37 and setting chemistry to reel 20; both are deliberately not covered here.
14. **Human histology of cementum over MTA does exist** (second-pass finding; the first pass of this brief said none was identified). Bartols et al. 2017 (4 extracted human teeth, cementum-like layer on MTA in all), Tirone et al. 2018 (pilot RCT, 6 patients, experimental perforations, 3 months) and a single case (Olivieri 2012) — rows 47-49. All are very small; the animal studies remain the main evidence. So the "cementum over the material" picture should be tagged "animal histology" on screen, with "small human case series agree" kept for the caption.
15. **"Workers approach and stay" / cementum laid directly on the block** is the typical finding, not a universal one: in a 24-month dog study cementum re-formed "with no direct attachment to the MTA" (Al Hezaimi 2024, row 50), and in the 1997 monkey study it was 5 of 6, not 6 of 6.
16. A further systematic review worth citing for "no brand is better": Emara, Elhennawy & Schwendicke, J Dent 2018 (row 51) — 38 studies, "No CSC was clearly superior".

## (d) Controversies and caveats (do not overstate)

- **Terminology drift.** Endodontic papers routinely call MTA/Biodentine "osteoinductive" on the basis of marker expression in culture, mineralised (von Kossa-positive) deposits in rat subcutaneous tissue, or bone formation inside a bone defect (rows 46). None of these is the heterotopic-bone test. Under Albrektsson's broad definition one could argue any material that stimulates progenitor differentiation participates in osteoinduction; under the strict biomaterials definition it has not been shown. The reel should name which definition it is using.
- **Absence of evidence vs evidence of absence.** The one direct ectopic test found (Moretton 2000) was in rats, and Barradas 2011 notes that material-driven osteoinduction is rarely seen in rodents and usually needs large-animal intramuscular models. A PubMed search (MTA / calcium silicate cement / Biodentine + osteoinductive / ectopic bone) found no large-animal ectopic study of an endodontic calcium silicate cement. Safe statement: "not demonstrated", not "impossible".
- **Dystrophic calcification is not bone.** Subcutaneous von Kossa-positive/birefringent areas (Holland 1999; Gomes-Filho 2009; Souza 2021) are mineral deposits, and Moretton described the same finding as dystrophic calcification with coagulation necrosis.
- **Cell lines.** MG-63 and Saos-2 are osteosarcoma lines, MC3T3-E1 and OD-21 are murine lines; they are already committed cells. Up-regulated osteocalcin in committed osteoblasts is osteo-stimulation, not recruitment and induction of uncommitted cells.
- **Extract/dilution dependence.** In vitro results depend on fresh vs set state, extract dilution and exposure time (Malta 2024 meta-analysis; Camilleri 2004). High early pH is cytotoxic at the surface.
- **SBF/PBS apatite test validity** is disputed (Kokubo 2006 vs Bohner 2009). Label apatite formation "in vitro".
- **Cementum over the material** is mainly animal histology (dogs, monkeys; n = 6 per group in the monkey study). Human histology is limited to a 4-tooth case series, a 6-patient pilot trial and single case reports (rows 47-49) — do not put a "human" evidence tag on this picture on screen. One long-term dog study found cementum re-forming without direct attachment to MTA (row 50).
- **Material differences in tissue response are not established.** The human pilot (row 48) suggested cementum-like tissue with MTA but little with Biodentine; a dog study favoured a premixed putty over MTA (row 44); the pulp-cell systematic review found no material clearly superior (row 51); and human RCTs show no outcome difference between MTA and premixed putty (row 44). These point in different directions and none is strong enough for a ranking.
- **Class effect is not guaranteed.** Calcium release and dentin interaction differ between materials (Biodentine > MTA > premixed sealer; Han & Okiji 2013); resin-containing "calcium silicate" liners behave differently in vitro (Giraud 2019 review). Premixed putties performed like MTA in human RCTs (Safi 2019; Zhou 2017) but most newer materials have far less evidence than MTA (Torabinejad, Parirokh & Dummer, Int Endod J 2018;51:284-317, PMID 28846134, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/iej.12843): "most studies had several methodological shortcomings and low levels of evidence").
- **Extruded sealer**: "bioactive" does not mean extrusion is harmless or desirable. **[FC]** Two retrospective cohorts found no effect on healing; the one RCT signal of harm pooled an epoxy-resin sealer with a calcium silicate sealer and was not present at 5 years (row 45). Honest summary: no clear harm shown, no benefit shown, weak data.
- **[FC] Osteoconduction is not a special property of bioceramics.** Albrektsson & Johansson say it is "regularly seen in the case of bone implants" and is absent mainly with materials of low biocompatibility; in Moretton 2000 the EBA (zinc-oxide-eugenol-type) cement was "osteoconductive" by the same criterion as MTA. The second tick on the end card is true but modest.
- **[FC] At a root end, bone is not supposed to grow on the material.** The favorable histology is cementum on the material, then a ligament-like space, then bone (rows 13, 15, 16). "Bone grows on it" is shown where the material sits in bone (rows 8, 11, 52). The diorama must not merge the two pictures.
- **[FC] Biocompatible does not mean inert or better than everything else in every test.** Under rat skin MTA provoked a stronger reaction than EBA cement (row 8); MTA Angelus showed more lymphocytic infiltrate than control in a 2023 rat study (row 52b); MG-63 cells on MTA released IL-1 and IL-6 (row 24).
- **[FC] Attrition in the human trials** (Safi 2019: 120 of 243 teeth evaluated; Zhou 2017: 158 of 240; von Arx 2019: 119 of 195 at 10 years) limits how firmly "no difference" or a healed percentage can be stated; always give the denominator.
- **Human clinical success** (rows 39-43) reflects seal, technique and case selection as much as material biology; it does not prove a tissue-induction mechanism.
