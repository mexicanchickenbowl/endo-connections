## (b) Recommended on-screen facts (safe wording)

1. **BIOCOMPATIBLE** — "tissue tolerates it; early inflammation settles." Tag: *animal histology*. Support: rows 8, 10, 11, 12. Safe picture detail: fresh material is the irritant phase, set material is the tolerated phase (rows 7, 8) — but see caveat in (d) on fresh vs set.
2. **Cementum over MTA: 5 of 6 root ends (monkey, 5 months); amalgam: none.** Tag: *animal histology*. Source line: "Torabinejad 1997, J Endod". (row 13). This is the single most board-recognisable number.
3. **BIOACTIVE** — "apatite layer forms in phosphate-containing fluid — within hours." Tag: *in vitro*. Support: rows 18, 19, 21 (Sarkar 2005; Tay 2007; Gandolfi 2010).

## (c) Corrections to the brief

1. **Albrektsson & Johansson 2001 does not give the "ectopic bone" criterion** (at least not in its abstract). Its definition of osteoinduction is broad: recruitment of immature cells and their stimulation into preosteoblasts, "regularly seen in any type of bone healing". The strict heterotopic/ectopic-site test comes from the biomaterials literature (Urist 1965; Barradas et al. 2011; Habibovic & de Groot, J Tissue Eng Regen Med 2007;1:25-32, PMID 18038389, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/term.5)). Cite both, and say which definition the stamp refers to. Citation detail: Eur Spine J 2001;**10 Suppl 2**:S96-101.
2. **The decisive paper is missing from the brief**: Moretton et al. 2000 implanted MTA subcutaneously and intraosseously in rats and concluded in so many words that MTA is "not osteoinductive" but is "osteoconductive". It answers the reel's question directly and should be the on-screen source for beat 3.
3. **"Giraud et al. 2019 Dent Mater on calcium silicate sealers and PDL cells" is wrong.** Giraud T et al. Dent Mater 2019;35:24-35 (PMID 30269862, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dental.2018.09.008)) is a narrative review of **pulp-capping** materials and the inflammation/regeneration balance (resin-containing TheraCal vs resin-free Biodentine). The sealer/PDL-cell papers from the same group are Camps et al. J Endod 2015;41:1469-73 and Jeanneau, Giraud et al. J Endod 2019;45:1016-23 (row 31).
4. **Zanini et al. 2012 did not use dental pulp stem cells.** It used an immortalised **murine** pulp cell line (OD-21). The abstract does not name RUNX2/DSPP etc.; do not attribute a specific gene list to it on screen.
5. **Peng et al. 2011** used human dental pulp **cells** exposed to tricalcium silicate **extract** (pure Ca3SiO5, not a commercial cement). Markers verified: DSPP, DMP-1, osteocalcin, ALP, collagen I. RUNX2 and BMP-2 are **not** in this abstract. BMP-2 up-regulation is verified for **PDL cells + MTA** (Maeda 2010), not for pulp stem cells.
6. **"Silicon ion release up-regulating markers"** — the verified endodontic mechanism paper (Maeda 2010) implicates **calcium** via the calcium-sensing receptor (CaCl2 reproduced it, MgCl2 did not). A specific role for Si ions from endodontic cements was not verified in this search; keep Si off screen or label it "proposed".
7. **Sanz systematic reviews**: three relevant ones, all of in vitro studies — Materials 2019 (DPSCs, 37 studies), Dent Mater 2021 (signalling pathways, 13 studies), Int Endod J 2021 (PDL stem cells, cements and sealers, 20 studies). None is evidence of in vivo osteoinduction.
8. **Fresh vs set cytotoxicity is not a clean story.** Torabinejad 1995 tested both and ranked MTA favourably in both states; Balto 2004 found no PDL-fibroblast attachment to fresh MTA; but Camilleri et al. (Int Endod J 2004;37:699-704, PMID 15347295, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2591.2004.00859.x)) found 1-day-cured MTA supported a confluent Saos-2 monolayer while **28-day-cured** samples were *less* biocompatible; and in dogs fresh MTA gave cementum in 12/12 vs 8/12 for set MTA (Apaydin 2004). Do not show "fresh = toxic, set = safe" as a rule.
9. **"Mild early inflammation settles"** — in rat subcutaneous tissue the initial reaction to MTA was described as **severe** with coagulation necrosis (Moretton 2000) or mild-to-moderate (Gomes-Filho 2009), subsiding with time. "Early inflammation that subsides" is safe; "mild" is not universally true.
10. **Aeinehchi 2003** is a self-described preliminary report with 14 teeth analysed — fine for the caption, too weak for an on-screen number. Nair 2008 (33 teeth, RCT, light + electron microscopy) is the stronger human histology source.
11. **Extruded sealer**: Ricucci et al. 2016 (J Endod 2016;42:243-9, PMID 26725179, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.joen.2015.11.020)) studied only conventional sealers (ZOE, AH Plus, Apexit, Endomethasone) — it cannot be cited for calcium silicate sealers. For those, the evidence is mixed (row 45).
12. **Bioactivity ≠ osteoinduction ≠ "bonding" proven in patients.** Apatite formation is demonstrated in phosphate-containing solutions and extracted teeth. The claim that this layer is *the basis* of clinical bonding/sealing is the authors' attribution (Sarkar 2005), not a demonstrated clinical fact.
13. Scope note: the user's request also mentions bacteriostatic/bactericidal properties; per the brief this is reel 37 and is deliberately not covered here.

## (d) Controversies and caveats (do not overstate)

- **Terminology drift.** Endodontic papers routinely call MTA/Biodentine "osteoinductive" on the basis of marker expression in culture, mineralised (von Kossa-positive) deposits in rat subcutaneous tissue, or bone formation inside a bone defect (rows 46). None of these is the heterotopic-bone test. Under Albrektsson's broad definition one could argue any material that stimulates progenitor differentiation participates in osteoinduction; under the strict biomaterials definition it has not been shown. The reel should name which definition it is using.
- **Absence of evidence vs evidence of absence.** The one direct ectopic test found (Moretton 2000) was in rats, and Barradas 2011 notes that material-driven osteoinduction is rarely seen in rodents and usually needs large-animal intramuscular models. A PubMed search (MTA / calcium silicate cement / Biodentine + osteoinductive / ectopic bone) found no large-animal ectopic study of an endodontic calcium silicate cement. Safe statement: "not demonstrated", not "impossible".
- **Dystrophic calcification is not bone.** Subcutaneous von Kossa-positive/birefringent areas (Holland 1999; Gomes-Filho 2009; Souza 2021) are mineral deposits, and Moretton described the same finding as dystrophic calcification with coagulation necrosis.
- **Cell lines.** MG-63 and Saos-2 are osteosarcoma lines, MC3T3-E1 and OD-21 are murine lines; they are already committed cells. Up-regulated osteocalcin in committed osteoblasts is osteo-stimulation, not recruitment and induction of uncommitted cells.
- **Extract/dilution dependence.** In vitro results depend on fresh vs set state, extract dilution and exposure time (Malta 2024 meta-analysis; Camilleri 2004). High early pH is cytotoxic at the surface.
- **SBF/PBS apatite test validity** is disputed (Kokubo 2006 vs Bohner 2009). Label apatite formation "in vitro".
- **Cementum over the material** is animal histology (dogs, monkeys; n = 6 per group in the monkey study). No comparable human histological series was identified in this search — do not tag it "human".
- **Class effect is not guaranteed.** Calcium release and dentin interaction differ between materials (Biodentine > MTA > premixed sealer; Han & Okiji 2013); resin-containing "calcium silicate" liners behave differently in vitro (Giraud 2019 review). Premixed putties performed like MTA in human RCTs (Safi 2019; Zhou 2017) but most newer materials have far less evidence than MTA (Torabinejad, Parirokh & Dummer, Int Endod J 2018;51:284-317, PMID 28846134, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/iej.12843): "most studies had several methodological shortcomings and low levels of evidence").
- **Extruded sealer**: "bioactive" does not mean extrusion is harmless or desirable; clinical data conflict (row 45).
- **Human clinical success** (rows 39-43) reflects seal, technique and case selection as much as material biology; it does not prove a tissue-induction mechanism.

## (e) Do not claim

- "Bioceramics are osteoinductive" (unqualified) or "induce bone formation like BMPs".
- "MTA regenerates bone / cementum in humans" as a histological fact (animal histology only).
- "Bioceramics recruit stem cells and turn them into osteoblasts in patients."
- "Apatite layer chemically bonds the material to bone/dentin in vivo" as a demonstrated clinical fact.
- "Set = non-toxic, fresh = toxic" as a rule; or "no inflammation" (there is an early reaction).
- "Extruded bioceramic sealer is harmless / promotes healing."
- Any brand ranking or "X is more bioactive, therefore clinically better" (in vitro ion-release differences have not been tied to outcomes).
- Any specific gene (RUNX2, DSPP, BMP-2) attributed to a paper that does not report it (see corrections 4-6).
- Williams' definition as a verbatim quote from the 2008 paper (the classic wording is the 1987 consensus definition).
- Antibacterial claims (reel 37) or setting chemistry (reel 20).
