# 32 — External replacement resorption (ankylosis) — evidence brief

Resorption series 4/5. Research stage only (no build). Prepared 2026-11-22.

**Fact-checked 2026-11-22 (independent adversarial pass).** Every PMID cited in the tables and the reference list was
re-fetched from PubMed and each quoted abstract sentence re-read against the record. Full text was re-read
independently for Lin 2022 (PMC9546147), Abbott & Lin 2022 (PMC9543863), Patel 2022 (PMC9790676), Müller 2020
(PMC7028940) and the IADT 2020 avulsion guideline (AAPD reprint, text-searched for every quoted sentence and section
number). Wording marked `[FC]` was changed or added by the fact-checker; every change is listed in the
**Fact-check log** at the end of this file. Where this brief and the log disagree with an older note, the log wins.

**How this was checked.** Bibliographic data and abstracts were retrieved from PubMed (PMIDs and DOIs below).
**Full text was read for:** the IADT 2020 avulsion guideline (Fouad et al.; AAPD Reference Manual reprint of the Dent
Traumatol paper, all 9 pages), Patel et al. 2022 (PMC9790676), Abbott & Lin 2022 (PMC9543863), Lin et al. 2022
(PMC9546147) and the results/discussion of Müller et al. 2020 (PMC7028940). Everything else is **abstract only**
unless marked. **No abstract in PubMed** (existence and citation verified, content not): Line, Polson & Zander 1974;
Melcher 1976; Löe & Waerhaug 1961; Lindskog et al. 1985; Andersson et al. 1985; Hammarström et al. 1986 (two papers);
Tronstad 1988; Andreasen 1985; Andreasen & Hjørting-Hansen 1966; Andreasen & Kristerson J Endod 1981.
**Not indexed in PubMed:** Andreasen 1975 (Acta Odontol Scand 33:325-35), Malmgren et al. 2006 (Endod Topics),
Trope 2002 (Endod Topics) — these appear below only through a secondary source that was read.

Terms for the same entity: **external replacement resorption, ERR** (AAE Glossary; Patel 2022; Abbott & Lin 2022) =
**ankylosis-related (replacement) resorption** (IADT 2020) = **dentoalveolar ankylosis with replacement resorption**
(Karolinska group: Andersson, Hammarström, Malmgren) = **ankylotic root resorption** (Fuss 2003; Lin 2017) =
"osseous replacement" (Trope). *Ankylosis* is the fusion; *replacement resorption* is what follows it (see C1).

## (c) Corrections to the brief

1. **"Andreasen 1981/1985"** — these are not one study. The papers that actually carry the replacement-resorption
   mechanism are: Andreasen JO, **Swed Dent J 1980;4:231-40** (pathogenesis and topography of ankylosis, monkeys);
   Andreasen JO, **Acta Odontol Scand 1981;39:15-25** (cell damage vs resorption type); Andreasen JO, **Int J Oral Surg
   1981;10:43-53** (dry time and storage media). Andreasen 1985 (Int Endod J 18:109-18) is a review with no abstract —
   do not cite it for anything on screen.
2. **"Andersson et al. 1984/1989"** — two different papers by different author groups. **1984** = Andersson, Blomlöf,
   Lindskog, Feiglin, Hammarström, *Int J Oral Surg* 13:423-31: **monkeys**, diagnosis (percussion/mobility/radiograph
   vs histology). **1989** = Andersson, Bodin, Sörensen, *Endod Dent Traumatol* 5:38-47: **humans**, progression. The
   1989 paper is not a survival analysis "by age"; its conclusion is that after >= 1 h dry storage a tooth is "ankylosed
   and resorbed within 3-7 years" in 8-16-year-olds and may function "considerably longer" in 17-39-year-olds.
3. **"About 20%"** — correct source (Andersson 1984) but it is **monkey histomorphometry**, and the wording should be
   "reliably detectable once **more than 20%** of the root surface is ankylosed" (below 10%: never; 10-20%: 2 of 4
   teeth). It is a detection threshold, not the amount of injury needed to cause ankylosis. Note that Patel 2022
   wrongly describes this study as "based on conventional radiograph only".
4. **"Andreasen and Kristerson 1981 (surface damage size and repair)"** — there are four Andreasen & Kristerson 1981
   papers; the right one is **Acta Odontol Scand 1981;39:1-13** (PMID 6943904). Numbers: 1 and 4 mm2 lesions ->
   transient ankylosis gone by 8 weeks; 9 and 16 mm2 -> ankylosis persisted in most cases; adjacent vital PDL can
   reclaim about 1-1.5 mm. Monkeys, 8-week end point.
5. **"Bone marrow cells reach the root surface first"** — say **"bone from the socket wall"** (or simply "bone").
   Verified wording is "rapid osteogenesis in the alveolus" and "endosteal osteoblasts". `[FC]` The "first / before
   PDL cells" half of the sentence is a model, not a verified observation: the only text read that states it
   (Lin 2022) names *osteoclasts*, and Andreasen & Kristerson 1981 present their version as a postulate. The brief's
   "bone marrow cells" most likely comes from Line, Polson & Zander 1974 (MeSH: Bone Marrow Cells; no abstract, not
   read) — do not put it on screen. Also get the **order** right:
   in Hammarström 1989 "the initial ankylosis was **not preceded by root resorption**" — bone bridges the dead PDL and
   fuses to the root first (within 1-2 weeks in monkeys), then cementum and dentin are "gradually resorbed". Animate
   fusion -> then replacement, not a crater first.
6. **Dry time is a probability, not a switch.** IADT 2020: after 30 min dry "most PDL cells are non-viable"; > 60 min
   = "likely to be non-viable" **regardless of later storage in a medium**. But clinically about 14% of teeth dried
   > 60 min did not ankylose (Gul 2024; mature teeth in Lauridsen 2020 — and 40% of immature teeth), while 59% of
   mature teeth with < 20 min dry time did (`[FC]` the Lauridsen figures are model estimates at 900 days; Gul's
   cover a follow-up of 7 months to 23 years — "did not ankylose" means "not by then"). Mechanical
   crushing of the PDL (avulsion itself, intrusion, lateral luxation) also causes it. Write "likely non-viable".
7. **Storage media order** in IADT 2020 is **milk, HBSS, saliva, saline** (milk first); water is "poor" but "better than
   leaving the tooth to air-dry".
8. **"Flexible splint 2 weeks"** — correct ("passive flexible", wire up to 0.016 in / 0.4 mm; about 4 weeks and more
   rigid only with alveolar/jaw fracture). But splinting is not proven to prevent ankylosis: IADT itself says
   periodontal healing "is not likely to be affected by splinting duration"; Hinckfuss & Messer 2009 "inconclusive".
   `[FC]` Both of those statements are about *duration*. On splint *type* the same IADT section says "current
   evidence supports short-term, passive and flexible splints" and that healing is "promoted if the replanted tooth
   is subjected to slight mobility and function" (monkey data: Andersson 1988 thesis). So: flexible = recommended and
   biologically plausible; 2 weeks vs longer = no shown difference; neither is a proven anti-ankylosis measure in
   humans.
   List it as guideline care, not as a proven anti-ankylosis measure.
9. **"Malmgren et al. 1984 / Malmgren 2013 (decoronation, timing, preserves ridge)"** — 1984 = Malmgren, Cvek, Lundberg,
   Frykholm, *Scand J Dent Res* 92:391-9 (the technique: crown **and root filling** removed, root left, flap closed).
   2013 = *J Endod* 39(3 Suppl):S67-72 (review; duplicate in *Pediatr Dent* 35:164-9). **Timing data** are in Malmgren &
   Malmgren 2002 (*Dent Traumatol* 18:28-36) and Malmgren, Tsilingaridis & Malmgren 2015 (*Dent Traumatol* 31:184-9).
   "Preserves the ridge" is reasonably supported for **height** (`[FC]` Malmgren 2015: "considerable increase" at a
   mean age of 14.6 y in boys / 13 y in girls, "unchanged or reduced" after 16; the phrase "before or during
   pubertal growth" is from the Malmgren 2013 review, which adds that later cases also gained bone "but not at the
   same rate" — the 2015 abstract does not mention the growth spurt); for **width** the best wording is "helps
   preserve" — one cast study still found the site narrower than the contralateral side, and the evidence is one
   retrospective cohort plus case series (GRADE low).
10. **IADT 2020 decoronation trigger** is qualitative: infra-occlusion "deemed esthetically unacceptable and cannot be
    corrected by simple restorative treatment". No millimeter value is in the 2020 text (a "> 1 mm" figure circulates
    from the 2012 version — not verified here).
11. **"Lin et al. 2017 on infraposition"** — Lin S, Ashkenazi M, Karawan M, Teich ST, Gutmacher Z. *Oral Health Prev Dent*
    2017;15:467-74 is a short review proposing a treatment/decoronation protocol; it has no infraposition data. Use
    **Kawanami 1999** and **Malmgren & Malmgren 2002** for infraposition.
12. **"Hammarström et al. 1989"** — correct: *Endod Dent Traumatol* 5:163-75, monkeys (M9).
13. **"Trope 2002/2011"** — both are narrative reviews whose abstracts contain no findings; nothing on screen should
    be attributed to them.
14. **"Faster in young patients"** — correct for *progression and tooth loss* (Andersson 1989; Coste 2020; Ebeleseder
    1998). But ankylosis is *more frequent in mature-root teeth* (Lauridsen 2020 HR 2.1; Gul 2024). Say "progresses
    faster in children", never "more common in young teeth".
    `[FC]` Both "mature > immature" papers use the Copenhagen material, and the finding is not uniform: Hecova 2010
    reports that after avulsion "primarily, immature teeth were affected by these complications", and Müller 2020
    found no relevant difference by root stage. Safest: put nothing on screen about which root stage ankyloses more
    often.
15. **"Cannot currently be arrested"** — correct for progressive replacement resorption; add that small areas can be
    **transient** (Andreasen & Kristerson 1981; Andreasen 1975 via Lin 2022) and that a small case series reports arrest
    after regenerative endodontics (Yoshpe 2020). Preferred wording: "no known way to arrest it".
16. **"Replanted incisor ... crown remains"** — accurate, and one extra detail makes the drawing more truthful: a tooth
    replanted after > 60 min dry time is root-canal treated (IADT: within 2 weeks), so the **root filling persists as
    a line in the bone** while the dentin around it is replaced (and it is removed at decoronation — Malmgren 1984).
17. **Time-lapse durations** — sourced anchors exist and should replace free invention: fusion at 1-2 weeks (monkey
    histology), clinical/radiographic detection at 1-2 months (human), root resorbed in 3-7 years at age 8-16 after

    `[FC]` The three anchors are three different kinds of evidence (monkey histology; human clinical detection; an
    authors' conclusion from a radiographic index with 4.8 y mean follow-up). They justify the order of magnitude of

## (d) Controversies and caveats (do not overstate)

- **Ankylosis vs replacement resorption.** Often used as synonyms (IADT: "ankylosis-related (replacement)
  resorption"). Abbott & Lin 2022 object: ankylosis (fusion) can exist without resorption and can follow other
  resorption types; they prefer "ankylosis and replacement resorption" and split ERR into **transient** and
  **progressive**. The reel title can use both words, with ankylosis shown as the first step.
- **What the percussion note sounds like.** IADT 2020 and Andersson 1984: "high", "high-pitched (metallic)". Abbott
  & Lin 2022: descriptions vary ("high-pitched", "metallic", "woody") and "the key point is that the percussion sound
  is usually dull and very different to the adjacent teeth". Safe: "metallic / different from the neighbors".
  `[FC]` Never print "dull": Andersson 1984 uses it for the *normal* tooth ("dull" -> "high" once ankylosed), Abbott
  & Lin 2022 use it for the *ankylosed* one.
- **The 20% figure** comes from a small monkey experiment (the 10-20% band had 4 teeth). Human validation is indirect
  (Campbell 2005: sensitivity 76-92% by sound alone).
- **Mechanism wording.** "Bone cells win the race" is a model built on monkey histology and classic periodontal
  wound-healing concepts; authors differ on the first cell (osteoblasts / endosteal cells vs clastic cells). Hammarström
  1989 argues the resorption is ordinary hormonally regulated bone turnover rather than an inflammatory process.
- **Early calcium hydroxide.** In monkeys with a necrotic PDL, immediate Ca(OH)2 shifted healing toward ankylosis
  (Lengheden et al. Scand J Dent Res 1991;99:139-46, PMID 2052895 — `[FC]` abstract now checked: experimentally
  contaminated and necrotized PDL; "a significant shift towards ankylosis not associated with root resorption
  (greater than 80% of the total root surface area) was noted following calcium hydroxide treatment"); Abbott & Lin
  2022 therefore advise against immediate Ca(OH)2, while IADT 2020 recommends Ca(OH)2 (started within 2 weeks) or a
  corticosteroid-antibiotic paste. Unsettled; keep off screen.
- **Inflammatory vs replacement trade-off.** Controlling infection-related resorption in a tooth with a dead PDL does
  not give a normal PDL — it gives ankylosis (Andersson 1988). Ankylosis is the *expected and accepted* outcome of
  delayed replantation (IADT 2020; Panzarini et al. Dent Traumatol 2008;24:277-82, PMID 18410388).
- **Incidence figures are soft.** Pooled 51% rests on very-low-certainty observational data; single series range
  43-87%; detection method (percussion vs radiograph) and follow-up length differ.
- **Decoronation evidence** is one retrospective cohort plus case series and reports; no trials, no direct comparison
  with extraction; width preservation is less certain than height. Alternatives (autotransplantation, orthodontic space
  closure, composite build-up, surgical repositioning, distraction) exist and the choice is multidisciplinary.
- **Imaging.** Periapicals miss labial/lingual ankylosis; CBCT is not a validated detector of ankylosis (D8).
  Diagnosis remains clinical (percussion, mobility, infraposition) plus radiographic change over time.
- **No randomized evidence** for anything that prevents or treats it (Cochrane 2015 and 2019).
- **Animal evidence.** All histology (order of events, lesion size, timing in weeks) is from monkeys; human evidence
  is clinical and radiographic.
- **Metaphor check.** "Fossilized" is a picture, not biology: the root is replaced by *living, remodeling bone*, not
  mineralized in place. Avoid "calcifies", "turns to stone", "petrified" as literal claims.
- `[FC]` **The "race" between bone and PDL.** No primary source read states that bone cells reach the denuded root
  before PDL cells: Andreasen & Kristerson 1981 "postulate" rapid osteogenesis; Lin 2022 (review) names osteoclasts;
  Abbott & Lin 2022 say "the osseous healing response dominates"; the classic paper (Line 1974) has no abstract.
  Show bone bridging the dead PDL; do not print a claim about which cell wins.
- `[FC]` **"3-7 years".** One human radiographic study (Andersson 1989; teeth dry >= 1 h; mean follow-up 4.8 y; sample
  size not in the abstract). It is an authors' conclusion and has not been replicated as a number; Müller 2020 saw
  losses from replacement resorption at 3.9-8.8 y but most affected teeth were still present at the end of
  observation.
- `[FC]` **Enamel matrix derivative.** Two clinical series found no prevention or cure of ankylosis (Schjøtt &
  Andreasen 2005; partial, small-area success in Filippi 2006). A 2024 meta-analysis (Lin Y et al., Ann Med; 375
  teeth) found no gain in normal PDL healing but a lower extraction risk with EMD, on poor-quality evidence.
  "No known way to arrest it" stands; "EMD does nothing" is not what the literature says.
- `[FC]` **Root stage.** More ankylosis in mature teeth in the Copenhagen data (Lauridsen 2020; Gul 2024); the
  opposite impression in Hecova 2010; no difference in Müller 2020. Keep off screen.
- `[FC]` **Decoronation and age.** Malmgren 2015: bone "unchanged or reduced" after 16; Malmgren 2013 (review): later
  cases also gained bone "but not at the same rate". Say "gains most before/during pubertal growth".

## (e) Do not claim

- A resorption rate in mm/year, or a time to complete resorption other than "3-7 years, age 8-16, >= 1 h dry
  (Andersson 1989)". No number of years for adults.
- "20% of the root must be damaged" or "ankylosis is detectable at 20%" without "more than", "root surface" and
  "monkey histology".
- That > 60 min dry time *always* causes ankylosis, or that < 60 min is safe (59% of mature teeth with < 20 min dry
  time ankylosed in Lauridsen 2020).
- That milk, HBSS or a splint *prevents* ankylosis (they improve the odds / are guideline care).
- That root canal treatment, calcium hydroxide, antibiotics, fluoride, Emdogain or bisphosphonates arrest it.
- That it is caused by infection or bacteria (that is series 3/5), or that it hurts.
- That it is more common in young/immature teeth (it is *faster* in young patients; *more frequent* in mature teeth).
- That decoronation "regrows" or fully preserves the ridge, or that IADT 2020 gives a millimeter trigger.
- That the tooth should not be replanted after a long dry time (IADT 2020: replanting is "almost always the correct
  decision").
- That the crown resorbs (it does not; it is left in infraposition or loses its support).
- That CBCT diagnoses ankylosis reliably.
- Anything numeric attributed to Trope 2002/2011, Tronstad 1988, Andreasen 1985, Line 1974, Melcher 1976, Andreasen
  1975, or the IADT 2012 guideline.
- Do not draw bowl-shaped craters with an inflamed radiolucent halo (series 3/5) or a cervical entry point
  (series 5/5); in replacement resorption there is **no radiolucency** — bone sits directly against, then inside, root.

Added by the fact-check `[FC]`:

- That bone cells (or "bone marrow cells") are *shown* to reach the root before PDL cells — a model/postulate; the
  review that says it names osteoclasts. Not on screen in any wording ("first", "wins the race", "gets there before").
- That ankylosis "happens at 1-2 months": that is when it is usually *detected* in humans (Andreasen 1995); monkey
  histology shows fusion at 1-2 weeks. Never mix the two on one unlabeled time mark.
- "Decades", "for life" or any adult time span as a fact (the thesis abstract says "possibly throughout life";
  Patel 2022 says "many years"; no data).
- "Slowly" as an unqualified property (age 8-16: resorbed within 3-7 years; IADT: rate "can be unpredictable").
- That IADT 2020 prescribes decoronation as *the* treatment (it is one listed option), that decoronation always
  gains bone (after 16: unchanged or reduced in Malmgren 2015), or that it keeps ridge *width*.
- That mature teeth (or immature teeth) ankylose more often — sources conflict.
- That a flexible splint, or a 2-week rather than longer splint, is proven to prevent ankylosis in humans.
- The Lauridsen 2020 hazard ratio (1.22) with any unit of time — the abstract gives none.
- The word "dull" for the percussion sound (used in opposite senses by Andersson 1984 and Abbott & Lin 2022).
- "> 20% of root surface" on screen without "ankylosed", "detectable by percussion/mobility" and "monkey" — caption
  only.
- Group sizes for Andersson 1984 or Andersson 1989, or "n =" for Malmgren 1984 — none are in the abstracts.
- That enamel matrix derivative is useless for replanted teeth in general (it did not prevent or cure *ankylosis* in
  two series; a 2024 meta-analysis reports fewer extractions on poor-quality evidence).
