## (c) Corrections to the brief

1. **"Heithersay 1999 Quintessence Int" is three separate papers**, and each claim belongs to a different one:
   features and radiology = Quintessence Int 1999;30(1):27-37 (PMID 10323156); predisposing factors =
   1999;30(2):83-95 (PMID 10356560); classification + treatment outcome = 1999;30(2):96-110 (PMID 10356561).
   The original spelling is "trichloracetic acid".
2. **Patel 3-D classification is in Int Endod J, not J Endod**: Patel S, Foschi F, Mannocci F, Patel K.
   Int Endod J 2018;51:206-214 (PMID 28746776; epub 2017).
3. **"Patel et al. 2009/2016 (CBCT vs periapical)" are three different papers with different first authors**:
   Patel S, Dawood A, et al., Int Endod J 2009;42:831-838 (only 5 ECR teeth); **Patel K**, Mannocci F, Patel S,
   J Endod 2016;42:1435-1440 (clinical, 115 teeth); and the ex-vivo accuracy numbers are **Vaz de Souza D** et
   al., J Endod 2017;43:121-125 (published online Dec 2016).
4. **Gunst et al. 2013 is a case report** (Int Endod J 2013;46:877-887), not a histology series. The systematic
   histology is Mavridou 2016 (27 vital teeth) and Mavridou 2017 Int Endod J (7 root-filled teeth).
5. **PRRS composition and thickness**: "predentin, dentin, and occasionally reparative mineralized (bonelike)
   tissue", "fluctuating thickness averaging 210 μm". It is not predentin alone, and 210 µm is a mean from
   extracted teeth measured ex vivo — a CBCT "measurement tool" cannot honestly read it (a ~0.2 mm
   layer is at the limit of clinical CBCT resolution). **[FC]** The first draft quoted "typical endodontic CBCT
   voxel 0.08-0.125 mm"; that figure comes from none of the cited papers and has been removed — do not print a
   voxel size. If the reel shows a caliper on the sheet, label the value as the published average ("avg, ex vivo").
6. **"The canal stays walled off" needs "until late".** Advanced lesions perforate. In recent CBCT cohorts most
   lesions were already coded "p" (probable pulpal involvement): 75.0% (222/296; Patel 2026) and 57.5%
   (Machado 2024).
7. **The three stages overlap.** Mavridou 2016: resorption and repair "progress in parallel at different areas
   of the tooth". Show repair patches appearing while resorption is still active elsewhere; do not show a
   lesion that stops or shrinks.
8. **Mavridou 2017 percentages are non-exclusive frequencies** (they sum to well over 100%) from a referral
   series with no control group: orthodontics 45.7%, trauma 28.5%, parafunction 23.2%, poor oral health 22.9%,
   malocclusion 17.5%, extraction of a neighboring tooth 14%; more than one factor in 59%. Note the abstract's
   sex split is internally inconsistent ("175 (54%)" and "162 (46%)" of 337 teeth) — do not quote it.
9. **Intracoronal bleaching is a minor factor numerically**: sole factor in 3.9% of teeth (Heithersay 1999);
   1.96% of bleached teeth developed ICR (Heithersay 1994, thermocatalytic 30% H2O2). **[FC]** "None in Jeng 2020"
   was deleted: the Jeng 2020 abstract simply does not list bleaching among its factors, which is not a count of zero.
   "Surgery" in Heithersay = sole factor in 5.4% of teeth. Listing ortho/trauma/bleaching/surgery as four
   equal causes overstates the last two.
10. **"Junctional epithelium" is not the wording of the sources.** Mavridou: "below the gingival epithelial
    attachment". Use "epithelial attachment". The entry site follows the attachment level, not strictly the
    CEJ (Patel height 1 = "at CEJ level or coronal to the bone crest").
11. **Pink spot**: real but uncommon — 5.1% of 215 lesions (Patel 2023). Do not make it the lead sign.
12. **"Sharp thinned edges"**: NOT VERIFIED in any text I could read. What is verified: profuse bleeding on
    probing and a "hard and scratchy" feel (Patel 2022, citing Patel 2009). Leave "sharp thinned edges" off.
13. **"Canal wall traceable through the lesion"** is correct as classic teaching (Heithersay: "a characteristic
    radiopaque line generally separates the image of the lesion from that of the root canal"), but Luso & Luder
    2012 showed that on conventional films this wall "frequently could not be recognized". Word it "often still
    traceable", and let the CBCT panes show the wall clearly.
14. **Outcome by class** (Heithersay 1999): 100% for classes 1 and 2, 77.8% class 3, 12.5% class 4 — success
    (no resorption, no periapical/periradicular pathosis), ≥3 years, 101 teeth, TCA protocol. Later papers
    misquote it (Rotondi 2020 prints "2%" for class 4; Patel 2026 introduction gives "survival" of 96.8% and 50%
    for classes 3 and 4). Use only the abstract's numbers, labelled "success".
15. **TCA is not current standard**: Patel et al. 2022 explicitly do not recommend it. Do not depict TCA as the
    treatment.
16. **CBCT wording**: statements say CBCT "should be considered" when the resorption appears amenable to
    treatment after clinical and conventional radiographic examination — not CBCT for every suspected case.
    The AAE/AAOMR statement has a **2025 update** (Sousa Melo et al., J Endod 2026;52:4-13); cite that rather
    than only the 2015 version. The ESE ECR statement (2018) has been followed by the ESE position statement on
    root resorption (Int Endod J 2023;56:792-801).
17. **Heithersay's classification is 2-D (periapical)**. In a CBCT-viewer reel the 3-D Patel classification is
    the natural lead; if Heithersay 1→4 is animated, label it "2-D".

## (d) Controversies and caveats (do not overstate)

- **Etiology is unknown.** All listed factors are associations from uncontrolled series. The only case-control
  study (Irinakis 2020) found just trauma and diabetes significant; orthodontics was not. A 2026 systematic
  review on trauma concludes causality is not established.
- **Role of bacteria and hypoxia** in driving the lesion is hypothesis (Mavridou 2019 is a single case report;
  two competing mechanisms summarized in Rotondi 2020). Keep off screen.
- **Cats / feline herpesvirus**: von Arx 2009 = 4 patients; Patel 2025 = cat ownership in 15.8% with no control
  group. A hypothesis only.
- **Prevalence** figures (0.02% to 2.3%) come from very different denominators (general patients, endodontic
  referrals, CBCT archives) and are rising with CBCT use (Huang 2023). No single prevalence number is safe on
  screen without its denominator.
- **CBCT vs periapical**: CBCT is consistently better, but the pooled evidence is graded "very low" certainty
  (de Sousa 2026), the best-known accuracy figures are ex vivo with simulated cavities (Vaz de Souza 2017), and
  periapical *detection* is not poor (sensitivity 0.85-0.86). The real 2-D deficit is extent, circumferential
  spread and treatment planning.
- **Parallax**: classic teaching (Gartner 1976; restated in Patel 2022) is that ECR shifts with tube angulation
  and internal resorption does not. Patel K et al. 2016 found parallax radiographs gave "no additional
  benefit" over a single periapical for ECR assessment. Both are true: parallax helps tell external from
  internal, it does not reveal extent.
- **Which classification predicts outcome is unsettled.** Irinakis 2022: Heithersay class predicted failure.
  Patel 2026: Heithersay not significantly associated; among Patel 3-D categories only spread C (>180°-<270°)
  reached significance (multivariable HR 12.9, 95% CI 1.81-93.10), D (>270°) did not, and grouped >180° vs ≤180°
  was borderline (HR 2.42, 95% CI 0.99-5.94, p = 0.054) in an analysis the authors call exploratory **[FC]**.
  DeLuca 2023: neither
  correlated with 1-year outcome. Reliability: Jebril 2020 kappa 0.69 (Heithersay) vs 0.40 (Patel), 13
  patients; DeLuca 2023 "substantial agreement" for both.
- **Outcomes**: all retrospective, no randomized trials, heterogeneous definitions (success vs survival),
  strong confounding by indication (teeth chosen for "periodic review" are often quiescent/reparative lesions).
  Ten-year survival ranges from 28.6% (Mavridou 2022) to 80.5% (Patel 2026, 3 teeth at risk). **[FC]** Survival
  flatters the picture: in Patel 2026 success (asymptomatic and healed) was 62.7% at 5 y and 50.9% at 7 y against
  survival of 85.7% and 80.5%; in Fernández-Grisales 2026 success was 61.7% (periapical) / 49.3% (CBCT) against
  77.8% survival. The Patel 2026
  paper states the ESE position statement regards ECR management options as "still experimental".
- **Bone-like tissue is repair, not cure.** It marks the reparative phase and supports monitoring in
  asymptomatic, inaccessible lesions, but it also makes the tooth more prone to fracture at extraction
  (Patel 2022).
- **"Pulp protected"** applies to vital teeth; in root-filled teeth the pulp and the sheet "had been removed
  during root canal treatment", the resorption stage was "more intense than the repair stage" and bonelike repair
  formed "to a lesser extent" (Mavridou 2017, 7 teeth) **[FC: exact wording restored]**.
- **"Walled off" versus the CBCT cohorts [FC].** The histology (27 extracted vital teeth) says the front does not
  penetrate the pulp space, yet on CBCT most lesions are coded "p" — "probable pulpal involvement" in the 2018
  classification, "probable or actual pulp involvement" in Patel 2026 (75.0%, 222/296) and 57.5% in Machado 2024;
  Patel 2023: "the majority of ECRs were in (close) proximity to the pulp". "p" is proximity on a scan, not proof
  of perforation. On screen keep "until late" and never draw the sheet as a guarantee.
- **PRRS evidence base [FC].** The sheet and its 210 µm average come from one research group (Leuven: Gunst 2013
  case report; Mavridou 2016, 27 extracted teeth; Mavridou 2017, 7 teeth), supported by Luso & Luder 2012
  (9 extracted teeth: innermost dentin intact) and single-case reports (Noma 2024; Ricucci 2023). There is no
  in-vivo measurement of the sheet.
- **Terminology**: external cervical resorption (ESE, Patel) = invasive cervical resorption (Heithersay) =
  extracanal invasive resorption (Frank & Torabinejad). Use "External cervical resorption (invasive cervical
  resorption)" once; then "ECR".
