## (c) Corrections to the brief

1. **"Sattapan 2000: torsional vs flexural percentages" must not be read as "torsion is the commoner cause".** Sattapan found
   55.7% torsional / 44.3% flexural among the fractured files of 378 Quantec files from ONE practice, classified from the
   lateral view. The larger Parashos 2004 sample found the reverse (3.5% flexural vs 1.5% torsional), a 2021 systematic
   review found flexural fatigue predominant in 10 of 12 clinical studies (62–92%), and Cheung 2005 showed that lateral-view
   classification is unreliable. Safe statement: both occur; the mix depends on file, size, technique and operator.
2. **"Unwinding before fracture" is not a dependable warning.** Unwinding is plastic deformation from torsional overload and is
   a reason to discard a file (12% of discarded files in Parashos 2004), but most clinically fractured ProTaper files showed
   no visible plastic deformation (Spanaki-Voreadi 2006: 73.9% of 46 FAILED files, i.e. 34 of the 38 fractured ones; Peng
   2005). Word it "may unwind first", not "unwinds first". [FC] Spanaki-Voreadi attributed those fractures to a single
   overload event, not to fatigue — so "no warning" is not a property of fatigue alone.
3. **Spili 2005 is a case-control study nested in specialist-practice records**, follow-up "at least 1 year", outcome =
   healing on masked radiographs. The pre-operative lesion lowered healing in both arms; the case-vs-control difference was
   not significant with or without a lesion. Do not write "a lesion plus a fragment significantly worsens prognosis" from Spili.
4. **Panitvisai 2010 pooled only two case-control studies (199 cases)** — Crump & Natkin 1970 and Spili 2005. The 80.7% vs
   92.4% contrast is lesion vs no lesion among teeth with fragments, not fragment vs no fragment.
5. **Crump & Natkin 1970 has no PubMed abstract.** Numbers (178 cases, 53 matched pairs, 91% success, no significant
   difference) come from a textbook chapter only. Name the study in the caption if wanted; keep its numbers off screen.
6. **Suter 2005 does not show a location effect.** Its abstract reports 87% removal and explicitly "no statistically
   significant difference in the success rate with respect to the location of the fractured instrument (tooth/root type)";
   success fell with longer treatment time. The location-relative-to-the-curve gradient comes from Shen 2004, Hülsmann &
   Schinkel 1999, Ward 2003 and Cujé 2010.
7. **Shen 2004 defines success as removal OR complete bypass** (overall 53%, 72 teeth). Label the 100/60/31% numbers
   "removal or bypass".
8. **"Yared et al. 2001" is two papers.** Int Endod J 2001;34(1):47–53 (speed, torque, operator: 150 rpm safer than 350 rpm;
   torque setting made no difference at 150 rpm; least experienced operator broke more) and Int Endod J 2001;34(6):471–5 (no
   difference between air, high-torque and low-torque motors). Neither supports "low torque prevents separation"; Berutti 2004
   even found ProTaper "worked better at high torque", and Iqbal 2006 found no clinical difference with torque control.
9. **Berutti 2004 and Patiño 2005 are laboratory studies.** Berutti used resin training blocks; Patiño compared extracted
   teeth with the authors' own earlier experiments (historical controls). Label "in vitro"; do not present 12% vs 26% as a
   controlled clinical result.
10. **Iqbal 2006 also reported two "negative" findings** the brief omits: no difference between first- and second-year
    residents and none between torque-controlled and non-torque-controlled handpieces.
11. **Shen 2013 (and Zupanc 2018) are reviews of laboratory properties.** Heat treatment raises cyclic-fatigue resistance in
    vitro. [FC] Torsional behavior is NOT settled: the Ohri 2024 review says both that martensitic instruments "do not perform
    as well in terms of torsional resistance" and that "most newer-generation files made from heat-treated NiTi wire have a
    greater torsional resistance than conventional NiTi files". No clinical comparison showing fewer separations was found;
    the 10-year series (Natanasabapathy 2025) studied only already-separated, referred instruments and cannot measure incidence.
    The McGuigan 2013 review abstract (Br Dent J 214:341–8, PMID 23579132) does state that "manufacturer modifications" reduce
    fracture incidence, while noting the evidence is mostly in vitro. Do not claim "heat-treated files do not break", "break
    less in patients" or "are weaker in torsion".
12. **McGuigan, Louca & Duncan 2013 is a three-part Br Dent J series**: outcome 214(6):285–9; causes and prevention
    214(7):341–8; decision-making 214(8):395–400. Their abstract wording ("if apical disease is present, file fracture
    significantly reduces prognosis") is stronger than the controlled data (Spili, Panitvisai) support.
13. **Incidence ranges attributed to Parashos & Messer 2006 could not be read in the primary** (paywalled; the abstract has
    no numbers). The ranges 0.25–6% (stainless steel) and 1.3–10% (NiTi) are quoted in the 2025 expert consensus full text.
    Prefer a single well-defined study (Iqbal 2006) on screen.
14. **Souter & Messer 2005:** only the qualitative findings are in the abstract. The specific figures often quoted for it
    were not verified — leave them out.
15. **"Taper lock" is a clinical/teaching term**, not a measured variable in the cited studies. The verified facts behind it:
    torque rises with tip size and taper and in small canals (Sattapan, Palamara & Messer 2000), and torsional failure was
    attributed to excessive apical force (Sattapan 2000). Use "tip/taper binds" as a diagram label, not as a cited claim.
16. **Grossman citation trap:** Oral Surg 1969;28:746–52 (PMID 5259460) is "Guidelines for the prevention of fracture of
    root canal instruments" (no abstract); the outcome data are in J Br Endod Soc 1968;2:35–7 (PMID 5246221). Neither was
    read. [FC] Fox et al. 1972 IS in PubMed: Fox J, Moodnik RM, Greenfield E, Atkinson JS. "Filing root canals with files
    radiographic evaluation of 304 cases." N Y State Dent J 1972;38(3):154–7, PMID 4500382 (no abstract, no DOI) — the earlier
    statement that it could not be found was wrong. It has still not been read. Strindberg 1956 was not looked up again. All
    three: content NOT VERIFIED — do not cite on screen.
17. [FC] **Panitvisai 2010 does not name its two case-control studies in the abstract.** That they are Crump & Natkin 1970
    and Spili 2005 follows from 53 + 146 = 199 cases; state it as an inference in NOTES.md.
18. [FC] **AAE Case Difficulty Assessment Form:** the levels are LOW / MODERATE / HIGH difficulty (not "minimal").

## (d) Controversies and caveats (what must not be overstated)

- **Which mechanism dominates** is unsettled and method-dependent (lateral view vs fracture-surface SEM). Clinical fractures
  are probably a combination of fatigue and torsion, and "torsional fatigue" (repeated sub-critical locking, e.g. with
  auto-reverse) is a proposed third route (Ohri, Chien & Peters 2024).
- **Incidence numbers are not interchangeable**: per tooth/case (Iqbal 1.68%, Spili 3.3%, Di Fiore 1.9%), per canal
  (Knowles 1.30% for LightSpeed, PMID 16410061 — [FC] NON-RETRIEVABLE separations only, 46 in 3,543 canals), per instrument
  (Di Fiore 0.39%; Plotino 2015 0.47%). [FC] Wolcott's 2.4% is reported next to "4,652 canals" but the abstract does not state
  its denominator. Always give the denominator.
- **"Rotary breaks 7× more than hand files"** is one graduate program in 2000–2004 with early-generation files. The McGuigan
  2013 review abstract concludes reported NiTi and stainless-steel incidences are "similar" overall with inconsistent methods.
  [FC] Newer pooled evidence points the same way: a 2026 systematic review and meta-analysis of treatments by undergraduate
  students (10 studies) found "no significant differences" between engine-driven NiTi and stainless-steel hand files for
  instrument separation, certainty of evidence low (Barbosa MCRF, et al. Int Endod J 2026;59(6):968–85. PMID 41139814.
  [doi](https://doi.org/10.1111/iej.70056); abstract). Do not present "7×" as a general property of rotary files.
- [FC] **"No warning" is not specific to fatigue.** Spanaki-Voreadi 2006 found most fractured ProTaper files without visible
  deformation yet attributed them to a single overload event; Cheung 2005 found fatigue in 18 of 27. The honest on-screen
  hedge is "may unwind" (torsion) and "often no visible warning" (fatigue).
- **Number of uses**: no evidence-based safe number. Wolcott (no difference to four uses) vs Gomes (more fractures beyond four
  teeth) vs Arens (new files also fracture) vs Parashos (operator matters more than count).
- **Glide path**: strong in vitro signal; clinical proof is indirect. A large clinical series used Reciproc without a hand
  glide path and still had only 0.47% fractures per instrument (Plotino G, Grande NM, Porciani PF. Int Endod J
  2015;48(2):199–205. PMID 24754602; [FC] 1,696 instruments, each used in ONE tooth only, 0.21% of canals). The 2020 J Endod
  systematic review of glide path/preflaring (Plotino et al., PMID 32334856) made no quantitative synthesis "for most of the
  parameters" and its abstract draws no conclusion on file separation.
- **Reciprocation**: higher cyclic-fatigue resistance in vitro (Ahn 2016, PMID 27185740; Ferreira 2017, PMID 26825427), but
  the clinical systematic review found no significant difference (Gomes 2021).
- **Speed**: Pruett found rpm does not change the number of cycles to failure — but at higher rpm those cycles are used up
  sooner (Li 2002: time to failure fell with speed; Martín 2003; Yared 2001).
- **Prognosis evidence is thin**: two case-control studies, 199 cases, one of them from 1970 with hand instruments, both from
  skilled operators. "Not significantly reduced" is not the same as "proven harmless": Spili's lesion subgroup (86.7% vs
  92.9%) may be underpowered. The stage of disinfection at the moment of fracture has never been measured in an outcome
  study; it is a biologic rationale (Simon 2008; McGuigan 2013; Madarati 2013).
- **Retrieval success rates (87–100%) come from referral practices and expert operators** with microscope and ultrasonics;
  pooled figures are lower (66%, Zhao 2026) and definitions differ (removal only vs removal or bypass).
- **Leave vs retrieve has no trial.** Fu 2011: removed vs left, no significant difference in healing. Ungerechts 2014
  suggests removal matters in infected cases but is small and unmatched. Malentacca 2023 (158 teeth, one operator; PMID
  37422250): 131 fragments removed (82.9%); 10 of 131 teeth (7.6%) had failed at 5 years, all from root fracture, although the
  authors judged this not a significant increase in root-fracture incidence.
- [FC] **"Removal weakens the root" rests on conflicting ex vivo data.** Souter & Messer 2005 and Madarati 2010 found lower
  fracture resistance after removal from the middle/apical third, but Shahabinejad H, et al. (J Endod 2013;39(6):824–8. PMID
  23683286. [doi](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.joen.2013.02.008); abstract; 70 extracted maxillary premolars) found that ultrasonic
  removal "did not significantly affect the required force for root fracture" (80% removal success; before the curve 11.5 times
  more successful than beyond it). Say "may weaken the root (ex vivo)", never a percentage.
- [FC] **The "healing" criteria and follow-up are thin.** Spili's abstract gives "at least 1 year" and no definition of
  healing; a late failure would not be captured. No newer systematic review of prognosis than Panitvisai 2010 was found in
  PubMed (searched 2026-11-22), so the 2010 pooled estimate has not been superseded — nor strengthened.
- **No US guideline was found.** Madarati 2013: "Guidelines… have not been formulated". The AAE item is a Communiqué article
  (clinical opinion); the 2025 consensus is Chinese. Do not write "AAE recommends".
- Informing the patient and documenting the event is recommended by every review read (McGuigan 2013; Simon 2008; AAE 2026).

## (e) Do not claim

- "Most files break from torsion" or "most break from fatigue" as a universal fact; or "torsional fracture always unwinds first".
- "A separated file does not affect prognosis" without the qualifiers (case-control, ≥ 1 yr, specialist practice, not
  significant). Never "a retained fragment is harmless".
- "A separated file causes failure" — the fragment is an obstacle to disinfection, not the pathogen (Lin 2005).
- Any Crump & Natkin, Strindberg, Grossman or Fox number on screen (primaries not read).
- Souter & Messer percentages; "root strength falls by X%".
- "Glide path cuts separation by half/80%" as a clinical figure; "low torque prevents fracture"; "single use eliminates
  fracture"; "heat-treated/gold/blue files don't separate"; "reciprocation separates less" (clinical difference not significant).
- "Never retrieve from the apical third" — the source says "should not be routinely attempted" (apical third of curved canals).
- "Always remove the fragment" or "always leave it" — the decision is case-based (Madarati 2013).
- "AAE guideline/position statement says…" — none was found on this topic (web search of aae.org, 2026-11-22).
- Any fracture-incidence number without its denominator and setting.
- A specific safe number of uses, rpm or torque value (system- and manufacturer-specific; not evidence-based across files).
