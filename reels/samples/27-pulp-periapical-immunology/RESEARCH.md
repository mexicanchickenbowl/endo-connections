# RESEARCH — Reel 27: Pulpal and periapical immunology (lines of defense, dentin to periapex)

Evidence brief for the build stage. Research only; nothing here is built.
Compiled 2026-11-22 from PubMed (abstracts for every PMID listed; PMC full text read for Graves 2011,
Farges 2015 and Galler 2021). According to PubMed, the records below say what is quoted; every reference
carries its DOI link in section (g). "Verified from" tells the builder how far a claim was actually checked.

**How to read the confidence column**
- HIGH = stated directly in an abstract/full text I read, consistent across sources.
- MEDIUM = stated in one source, or true only for a species/model, or a review-level synthesis.
- LOW = classic paper with no PubMed abstract, or conflicting data. Keep off screen unless hedged.

**The one-sentence summary a board examiner would accept**
Bacteria are sensed at the dentin-pulp border (odontoblasts, dendritic cells), the pulp mounts innate then
adaptive responses but can lose compartment by compartment; once the canal is necrotic it is a bacterial
reservoir the immune system cannot reach, so the defense is re-staged at the periapex, where the same
mediators that hold the infection in (IL-1, TNF, prostaglandins, RANKL) also resorb bone. The lesion is
protective and destructive at the same time, and it cannot sterilize the canal.

## (c) Corrections to the brief

1. **Hahn & Liewehr are not titled "parts 1-2".** The two 2007 J Endod reviews are "Innate immune responses of the dental pulp to caries" (33:643-51, PMID 17509400) and "Update on the adaptive immune responses of the dental pulp" (33:773-81, PMID 17804311). A third companion review exists: "Relationships between caries bacteria, host responses, and clinical signs and symptoms of pulpitis" (33:213-9, PMID 17320699). Cite by title/pages, not as parts.
2. **Graves 2011** is "Review of osteoimmunology and the host response in endodontic and periodontal lesions" (Graves, Oates, Garlet; J Oral Microbiol 2011;3:5304). It is a narrative review of mostly animal knockout/inhibitor data, not a T-cell-subset paper. Its Table 1, Table 2 and text disagree with each other on IFN-γ (text: deletion increased resorption; Table 1: "no effect") and IL-17 (Table 1: "reduced lesion size"; Table 2: "no effect"). Do not cite Graves 2011 for the direction of either.
3. **Čolić 2009** is Mol Immunol 2009;47:101-13 (human lesion cells in culture). It links Th17 to **exacerbation of inflammation in symptomatic lesions**, not directly to bone resorption, and states Th1 "is most probably important for all stages". The dedicated IL-17 paper is Čolić 2007, Eur J Oral Sci 115:315-20.
4. **"T cells generally more than B" is not safely supported.** The classic paper (Torabinejad & Kettering 1985) has no abstract; verified series show lymphocytes ≈ half of leukocytes/mononuclear cells but T and B "equally distributed in 60% of cases" (Liapatas 2003), and human lesions split into T-type and B/plasma-cell-type (Čolić 2009). Say "lymphocytes, plasma cells and macrophages" without ranking.
5. **"Neutrophils dominate acute responses" needs placing correctly in the pulp sequence.** Under dentin caries, before exposure, the first responders are odontoblasts and dendritic cells, then T cells; macrophages, neutrophils and B cells surge with deep caries (Izumi 1995 stage S4+; Farges 2015; Galler 2021: phagocytosis does not occur until the carious front reaches the pulp). Draw neutrophils arriving where bacteria actually contact pulp tissue (exposure/microabscess), not as the first cell under intact dentin.
6. **IL-6 should not be listed with the bone destroyers.** IL-6 knockout mice lost 30% more periapical bone (Balto 2001). IL-6 is induced in lesions, but its net effect in the model is anti-inflammatory. Keep the on-screen chain to IL-1, TNF, PGE2 → RANKL.
7. **IL-17 direction is unresolved** (IL-17A knockout: no lesions, Oseko 2009; IL-17 receptor A knockout: more bone destruction and abscesses, AlShwaimi 2013). Do not put "IL-17 → bone loss" on screen as fact; if Th17 appears, label it "linked to flare-ups / active lesions".
8. **"Th1 → bone resorption" is an association, not a knockout-proven cause.** IFN-γ, IL-12 and IL-18 knockouts individually did not change bone resorption (Sasaki 2004, Clin Diagn Lab Immunol 11:106-10, PMID 14715554), and IFN-γ knockouts had larger lesions in De Rossi 2008. Human active lesions do show higher IFN-γ/TNF-α/IL-17A (Araujo-Pires 2014). Safe wording: "pro-inflammatory T-cell signals (Th1/Th17-type) mark active lesions".
9. **"Th2 → restraint" is really IL-10/Treg.** IL-4 knockouts showed no effect in two independent studies (Sasaki 2000; De Rossi 2008); IL-10 knockout clearly increased resorption; Treg inhibition worsened lesions (Francisconi 2016). Write "IL-10 / Tregs restrain", not "Th2 heals".
10. **Species detail on IL-1 and TNF.** Rat/mouse lesions: IL-1α dominant. Human granulomas: IL-1β and TNF-β (lymphotoxin); anti-TNF-α had no effect in the human assay (Wang & Stashenko 1993), and TNF-α knockout mice still formed lesions (Oseko 2009). On screen write plain "IL-1" and "TNF" with no Greek suffix. (Reel 18 wrote "IL-1β, TNF-α"; "IL-1, TNF" is the form that is true in both species.)
11. **"The pulp loses, then the fight moves to the periapex" is not strictly sequential.** Necrosis advances gradually from coronal to apical and periapical inflammation begins early, before total necrosis (Yamasaki 1994, rat; IL-1α-positive cells in the periapex by day 7, Stashenko 1994). The panel order is fine as storytelling, but panel 3 should show necrosis creeping apically while the apex already reddens; do not show an instant whole-pulp death or a "strangled" pulp.
12. **"Osteoclasts resorb bone to make room for the defense" is teleology.** What the sources say: the periapical response is a host defense that prevents extraradicular invasion (Nair 2004; Márton & Kiss 2000; Galler 2021) and bone resorption is a by-product of the same effector mechanisms. Safe caption: "Bone gives way; inflammatory tissue walls off the infection." Avoid "to make room".
13. **"Plasma cells (IgG)" is correct**, but cite Matthews & Mason 1983 (81.9% IgG, n = 10), not Pulver 1978 / Stern 1981 (no abstracts).
14. **Odontoblast TLRs:** expression in situ in human teeth is verified (TLR2, TLR4); the functional story (chemokines, defensins, NO) is mostly in vitro or organ culture, as Farges 2015 itself states. Caption-level hedge: "lab and tissue studies".
15. **My own lead for Teles was wrong and is corrected here:** the SCID paper is Teles, Wang, Stashenko, **Infect Immun 1997;65:3781-7** (PMID 9284152), not Oral Microbiol Immunol.
16. **"No blood supply" as the reason treatment is needed: supported** (Graves 2011: necrotic pulp "inaccessible to leukocytes ... protected reservoir"). Keep.

## (d) Controversies and caveats (what must not be overstated)

- **Protective and destructive at once.** Inhibiting the host response in endodontic lesions tends to enlarge them because the canal reservoir persists (Graves 2011; Chen 1999; Balto 2001; AlShwaimi 2013), whereas blocking IL-1 with receptor antagonist reduced bone loss by 60% in rats (Stashenko 1994). Both are true; partial blockade vs complete loss of signaling, and bacterial control, explain the difference. The reel should show the lesion as a containment line that costs bone, not as a villain or a hero.
- **Animal models dominate.** Timelines (active phase days 0-15), CD4/CD8 reversal, knockout results and RANKL kinetics are from rat/mouse pulp-exposure models. Human data are cross-sectional biopsies of surgically removed (often treatment-resistant) lesions.
- **Is adaptive immunity needed for the lesion?** Lesion size was similar in SCID mice (Teles 1997) and athymic rats (Wallstrom 1993): innate mechanisms suffice for bone resorption. Adaptive immunity (especially antibody; Hou 2000) mattered for preventing abscess/dissemination. So panel 4 should present adaptive cells as "containment", not as the cause of bone loss.
- **Which cells make RANKL** differs by study: monocytes and dendritic cells (Vernal 2006, human flow cytometry), fibroblastic cells with few T cells (Kawashima 2007, rat), neutrophils/macrophages/endothelium/lymphocytes by IHC (Menezes 2006). Do not attach RANKL to one cell type on screen; an unlabeled "lesion cells → RANKL" arrow is safest.
- **Th1/Th2/Th17/Treg boxes are a simplification.** Čolić 2009 found most IL-17+ cells co-expressed IFN-γ; Araujo-Pires 2014 found multiple cytokine clusters within both active and inactive lesions; Cavalla 2021 frames activity as a switchable state. "T suppressor cell" in the 1989-1996 papers means CD8+ cells; do not write "suppressor T cells" on screen.
- **"Active vs inactive" lesions** in the Garlet-group papers are defined by RANKL/OPG mRNA ratio, so a cytokine "associated with activity" is partly circular. Menezes 2008 also reported higher RANKL/OPG in lesions < 5 mm than > 5 mm.
- **Cell proportions in human lesions vary widely** with method (H&E counts, IHC, flow cytometry) and case selection. No single percentage should be presented as "the" composition.
- **Healthy-pulp neutrophil figure.** Gaudin 2015 found granulocytes to be 50% of CD45+ cells in digested healthy pulps; this probably includes cells within vessels. Do not use it to claim neutrophils patrol healthy pulp tissue.
- **Granuloma vs cyst immunology** is inconsistent across studies (Liapatas 2003: no significant difference; Galler 2021 summarizes conflicting CD8 and macrophage-polarization data). Leave cysts out of this reel.
- **Pulp necrosis mechanism.** Low compliance is real (Heyeraas & Berggreen 1999), but the same review describes lymph drainage and capillary absorption that relieve pressure; total "self-strangulation" of the pulp is not what the physiology shows. Necrosis is driven by bacterial advance plus host enzymes (Izumi 1995; Ricucci 2014).
- **Clinical translation.** HIV status did not change 12-month periapical healing in a small retrospective study (Quesnell 2005). Do not imply that immunocompromised patients cannot heal, nor that immunity is irrelevant: the mouse data concern untreated infection.

- Do not claim the immune system can sterilize or "clear" an infected necrotic canal.
- Do not claim any human timeline (hours/days/weeks) for these stages; all kinetics are rodent.

- Do not state "T cells outnumber B cells" or rank the cells.

- Do not say bone is resorbed "in order to make room".
