**What this brief is.** Two different kinds of source, kept apart on purpose:

1. **Manufacturer directions** (EdgeEndo, edgeendo.com). The PDFs were downloaded and their text extracted and read in
   full (English sections). These are quoted as "manufacturer IFU/DFU" with document code, URL and access date. They are
   instructions, not evidence.
2. **Independent literature** (PubMed; abstracts via the PubMed tools / NCBI E-utilities, PMC full text where stated).
   Every DOI/PMID is given so each claim can be traced.

"Verified from" says exactly what was read:

- **manufacturer IFU/DFU (full PDF text)** = the statement is in the manufacturer's document, read in full.
- **manufacturer web page** = product page text on edgeendo.com (marketing copy; lowest weight).
- **abstract** = the PubMed abstract contains the statement/number.
- **full text (PMC)** = read in the open-access full text (targeted reading of methods/results/tables, not every line).
- **citation only** = the paper exists on PubMed with that citation, but it has no abstract and the full text was not read.
- **NOT VERIFIED** = could not be confirmed; keep off screen.

**Headline finding.** No study has compared shaping sequences for this system, and none has measured which is "most
efficient" or "most effective". What can honestly be shown is: *the manufacturer's sequence* + *habits that the general
NiTi literature supports*. The reel must say so (one small line: "Manufacturer IFU + evidence-informed habits · follow
the current DFU").

**Two manufacturer documents are live at the same time and they are not identical** (IFU-2026 vs DFU-US-2023). Where they
agree, the fact is safe. Where they differ (reuse wording, which file goes first, contraindications, "unwinding"), the
row says which document says what. The resident should check which IFU ships in his boxes.

**Fact-check: which sequence is in which document.** The 17/.04 → 25/.04 "Simplified Technique Guide" (the main line
of the planned map) is printed **only in IFU-2026** (a 97-page multilingual document whose address block is FKG
Dentaire Sàrl, Switzerland). The US-labelled PDF (DFU-RF-US Rev D 06/23), the EU 2023 DFU and the 2015 DFU contain
**only the crown-down text** (25/.04 to resistance → 20/.04 → occasionally 17/.04). The US product page does list
"Shaping Technique: Traditional crown-down or simplified technique" for the X7, and both PDFs are linked from it. So
the crown-down variant is the only sequence common to all four manufacturer documents; the simplified route must be
attributed on screen to "IFU 2026", and the crown-down alternative should stay visible (legend or dotted line).

### X. Independent literature that actually tested EdgeFile X7 / EdgeFile / Fire-Wire

PubMed search `"EdgeFile X7"` returns only 6 records (2026-11-22; count re-run and confirmed by the fact-checker).
Several often-quoted "EdgeFile" papers do not name the X7 in the abstract, or tested X3/X5/X1.

**Fact-check on the "6 records".** Only **five** of the six test the file itself (Gambarini 2019; Sharroufna 2020;
Lin 2021; Al-Amidi 2023; Chourasia 2025). The sixth (Salim F, Saleem BM. Eur Endod J 2025;10(4):326–32, PMID 40705445,
doi:10.14744/eej.2025.85866) only used X7 files to prepare roots for a sealer/fracture-resistance test. Conversely, at
least one paper that the phrase search misses did test the X7: Tanomaru-Filho 2018 (X4) says "Edge File" in the
abstract but "EdgeFile X7 20/0.06 … 25/0.06" in Table 1 of the full text. So "six records name it in title/abstract"
is correct; "only six studies exist" would not be.

## (b) Recommended on-screen facts

Eight items, best supported first. Wording is conservative and fits the ≥30 px / ≤60-word budget; each carries its tag.
**This list was revised at fact-check (2026-11-22): items 1, 3, 5, 6, 7 and 8 were reworded; use the wording below, not
the researcher's first draft.**

1. **The route (IFU 2026):** `ACCESS → SCOUT (hand files) → WORKING LENGTH #10 K or larger (apex locator + radiographs)
   → 17/.04 → 25/.04 → larger .04 / .06 if wanted → OBTURATE`. Source line: "EdgeFile X7 IFU, simplified technique
   (accessed Nov 2026)". [M4]
   - *Fact-check:* the scout station carries **no file size** — the IFU's "#10 K file or larger" is the working-length
     step (step 4), and it adds "this will also form a glide path". The US DFU (2023) asks for patency with a #10 K and
     "at least a #15 K-File to the terminus" (M12); if the capsule shows "#10–#15 K", NOTES must name both documents.
   - *Fact-check:* a final **IRRIGATE (activate)** station is not a step of the IFU-2026 simplified technique (the IFU
     says "irrigate before each hand or rotary file"; only the US DFU has an end-of-shaping rinse, whose recipe must not
     be shown — D8). Keep the station if the map needs it, but tag it `CONV`, never `IFU`.
   - *Fact-check:* the obturation label is "check cone fit — may need a size smaller" [M13], not "cone = last file".
2. **The detour (IFU):** "25/.04 will not reach length? → 20/.04, alternate 20 ⇄ 25." [M4] Optional second detour, crown-down
   variant: "meets resistance → smaller file (25 → 20 → 17), hand file between". [M5]
3. **Taper rule (IFU):** ".04 — all canals · .06 — straight to mildly curved". [M6] The two phrases are the headings of
   the crown-down sections (both documents); no angle is given. Supporting evidence for the caption, older conventional
   NiTi on the bench: larger taper and diameter shortened fatigue life (Haïkel 1999); the larger instrument size failed
   sooner and curvature mattered more than rpm (Pruett 1997 — Lightspeed #30 vs #40; it did not test taper).
4. **Line info panel (IFU):** "300–500 rpm · torque limit 300 g·cm (2.94 N·cm) · minimal apical pressure". [M3; "minimal
   apical pressure" = DFU-US 2023, M11]
5. **Service icons between stations (IFU):** "irrigate · recapitulate #10/#15 · clean flutes · inspect". [M7, M8, M10]
   ("Recapitulate with a #10 or #15" is the crown-down wording; the simplified technique says "alternating with
   small-sized hand files if necessary" — M7.) One evidence tag is enough: "wet canal = lower torque (Boessler 2007,
   in vitro)" [P27] — ProFile 30/.06 in canals milled in human dentin; 1% NaOCl behaved like water, a chelator lowered

## (c) Corrections to the brief

1. **Order of files.** The brief suggests stations "20/.04, 25/.04 …" (ascending). The IFU does not do that. The
   *simplified technique* is **17/.04 → 25/.04**, with **20/.04 only if 25/.04 will not reach length** (alternate 20 ⇄ 25).
   The *crown-down* text starts with **25/.04 to resistance**, then 20/.04, occasionally 17/.04, then steps up. Both are in
   IFU-2026; only the crown-down version is in DFU-US-2023.
2. **"Glide path" is not a separate rotary step.** IFU-2026: the "#10 K file or larger" used for working length "will also
   form a glide path". DFU-US-2023 is stricter: patency with a #10 K 1 mm past the terminus and "at least a #15 K-File to the
   terminus". A rotary glide-path file (EdgeGlidePath, a separate product) is only an option "if more coronal flare is
   desired… used in a brushing motion". Draw one interchange "WORKING LENGTH / GLIDE PATH (#10–#15 K)", not a rotary station.
3. **Pecking amplitude and stroke count are not in the X7 directions.** The 2–3 mm pecking text in DFU-US-2023 belongs to
   other EdgeEndo files. For X7 the documents say "minimal apical pressure", "passively", and (DFU-US-2023 only) "each file to
   length only one time and for no more than one second". Pecking is supported only by bench literature (Li 2002, NiTi
   ProFile; Dederich 1986 tested stainless-steel engine files, so it is historical support only) and was the technique
   used in vivo by Gambarini 2019 (1 mm at a time). Tag it `EV/CONV`, never `IFU`, and give no mm.
4. **Reuse statement.** Not simply "single use". DFU-US-2023: "single use only", "once file is used do not reuse"
   (fact-check: "single patient use" is the EU 2023 / 2015 DFU wording, not the US document's). IFU-2026: "up to
   four (4) canals during a single procedure. Discard after use". Use a wording true under both documents ("one patient,
   one procedure, then discard · max 4 canals per file (IFU 2026)") and name the document for the 4-canal cap.
5. **Torque.** 300 g·cm = 2.94 N·cm is a **torque limit** (IFU-2026). DFU-US-2023 offers it as an option ("use the same…
   settings you are currently using… or if you wish…"). The web page rounds to 2.9 N·cm and shows "450 RPM" in one panel.
6. **Cross-section.** Unverifiable: the manufacturer's page says "parabolic"; three independent papers say "triangular
   with a variable helix angle" (fact-check: only Gambarini 2019 shows its own cross-section image; Al-Amidi 2023 and
   Chourasia 2025 make the statement with a citation, so this is one observation repeated, not three). Leave it off
   screen; do not draw a recognizable cross-section.
7. **"Express for easy canals / branch for tight or curved canals."** Supported only in this form: .04 taper "for all
   canals", .06 taper "for straight to mildly curved canals"; drop to a smaller file (and hand files) when the file meets
   resistance. No document gives an angle, radius or the word "tight". Do not print degrees. DFU-US-2023 lists "very severe
   and sudden curvatures" as a contraindication; IFU-2026 lists none.
8. **"Coronal preflaring before working length."** The IFU order is working length first (#10 K), then rotary files; the
   IFU even warns "do not over enlarge the coronal portion" (DFU-US-2023). The literature supports *re-confirming* length
   after flaring (Stabholz 1995; Ibarrola 1999; de Camargo 2009; Davis 2002; Plotino 2020), but the measured change is
   about 0.1–0.5 mm (Davis 2002; Schroeder 2002 calls 0.17 mm "clinically unimportant"). If shown, tag `EV` and word it
   "re-check length after flaring".
9. **Patino 2005** is **Patiño** PV et al.; the 12% vs 26% comparison is against the authors' *earlier* studies
   (historical control), in canals curved more than 30°, and the strongest factor reported was number of uses (>8 vs 1–2,
   OR 22.7). **Berutti 2004** also found every instrument "worked better at high torque" — it cannot be cited for low torque.
   **Plotino 2020** (systematic review) supports anatomy preservation and length accuracy, and does *not* conclude that a
   glide path reduces separation; that claim rests on in vitro / historical-control studies.
10. **Khademi 2006** = in vitro SEM of debris and smear layer in 40 mesiobuccal canals — not bacteria, not healing.
    **Boutsioukis 2010** = computational fluid dynamics; its wording is "sizes larger than 25", and the side-vented needle
    did not exchange irrigant to working length even at size 55. Two Boutsioukis 2010 Int Endod J papers exist (size:
    43:874–81; taper: 43:909–16) — cite the right one.
11. **"Fewer instruments = less torsional load" is not supported.** Schrader & Peters 2005 found the opposite trade-off: a
    mixed-taper sequence lowered torque and rotations but needed *more* instruments. Peters 2004 lists "specific instrument
    sequence" among the *less* significant factors.
12. **"Studies including EdgeFile X7."** Only six PubMed records name the X7 in title or abstract, and one of those
    (Salim & Saleem 2025) merely used it to prepare roots. Dosanjh 2017 and Topçuoğlu 2020 say "EdgeFile" (25/.04; model
    not named in the abstract). Tanomaru-Filho 2018 says "Edge File" in the abstract but names the **X7** in its Table 1
    (fact-check, full text). Drukteinis 2020 used the X5 series (full text confirmed), Yılmaz 2020 the X3 (abstract
    confirmed). Cite each with the exact product name the paper uses.
13. **EdgeX7 Utopia is a different file** (Blaze heat treatment; 500 rpm; 410 g·cm / 4.0 N·cm). Its tip card shows the same
    17 → 25 (20) → larger sequence, but its settings must not be shown for the X7.
14. **Autoclave studies** are irrelevant to current practice (supplied sterile, not reprocessed) and contradict each other
    (X5). Leave out.
15. **"Activate" station.** Fine as a convention, but no healing benefit has been shown (P38). Do not imply one.

## (d) Controversies and caveats (do not overstate)

- **D1. No comparative evidence for any X7 sequence.** "Most efficient and effective" has no study behind it. The single
  in vivo study (Gambarini 2019) measured only the first file in 10 canals. Efficiency claims must stay generic.
- **D2. Two live manufacturer documents differ** (first file, hand-file size, reuse wording, contraindications, the
  "unwinding" sentence, the 1-second rule). Use what is common; name the document where they differ.
- **D3. Torsion vs fatigue.** Sattapan 2000 (55.7% torsional, one practice, Quantec) vs Feghali 2021 (flexural 62–92% in
  10 of 12 studies). Mode depends on file size, design, canal and operator. Show both habits (light pressure → torsion;
  keep moving, respect curves → fatigue), no percentage.
- **D4. Reuse.** Wolcott 2006: no rise in separation over four uses (ProTaper). Arens 2003: defects after one use.
  Gomes 2021: more fractures beyond four teeth. Logsdon 2020: 74% of US endodontists reuse. The IFU forbids reprocessing.
  The reel should state the IFU and not endorse reuse.
- **D5. Speed.** Older-NiTi data favor lower rpm for fatigue and locking (Yared 2001; Martín 2003; Lopes 2009; Li 2002),
  while Pruett 1997 found cycles to failure independent of rpm (that time to failure then shortens is arithmetic, not a
  reported result). The speed-and-separation studies reused each file set up to 10 canals (Yared) or 20 times (Martín),
  with very wide confidence intervals in Martín — not single-procedure use. Nothing tests X7 at 300 vs 500 rpm. "Lower end of the range in curves" is an inference — caption only, flagged as such, or omit.
- **D6. Torque.** No consensus (P23). Show the IFU limit only.
- **D7. Apical size.** Larger sizes clean and irrigate better in vitro and in models (P30–P33) and one RCT/one review
  favor adequate enlargement (P34, P35), but the evidence is limited and anatomy sets the ceiling; "too large a file taken
  to length increases the risk of canal transportation and file separation" (DFU-US-2023). Give "#30" only with "in vitro".
  Fact-check: the two model results pull in different directions on the needle — in the CFD work a side-vented needle
  3 mm short did not exchange irrigant to working length at any size up to 55, so "bigger apical size = irrigant gets
  there" is not a general rule; needle type and depth matter at least as much (P31, P32).
- **D8. Irrigation recipe.** The DFU-US-2023 "disinfecting" sequence ends with 2% chlorhexidine "or EDTA". Ng 2011 found
  adjunct chlorhexidine associated with *lower* healing. Do not reproduce the recipe; "irrigate" is enough.
- **D9. Patency / recapitulation.** Associated with better irrigant delivery (Vera 2011, 2012) and healing (Ng 2011), no
  more pain (Yaylali 2018; Abdulrab 2018), but still debated, and no trial isolates recapitulation between rotary files.
  Tag recapitulation `IFU/CONV`.
- **D10. Fatigue rankings are test-dependent.** Results flip with temperature, canal geometry, file size and comparator
  (X2–X4, X9; de Vasconcelos 2016). EdgeFile ranked first in some tests and no better than a non-heat-treated file in
  another (Tanomaru-Filho 2018 — the X7 itself, 25/.06). Mean cycles to fracture for a new X7 range from 756 to 19,618
  between two rigs (X5). No brand ranking and no fatigue number on screen.
- **D11. Heat-treated ≠ unbreakable.** Flexible martensitic files resist fatigue but deform under torsion; the IFU says to
  discard at any distortion, while older DFUs called unwinding a "safety feature… may be used until the files unwind
  backwards". Use the stricter, current wording.
- **D12. NaOCl corrosion (Lin 2021)** appeared only after ≥1 h immersion. Not a reason to avoid NaOCl; possibly a reason
  not to leave files soaking. Caption at most.
- **D13. Prognosis after separation** is not significantly worse in specialist hands (P18). Do not dramatize separation in
  this reel; the companion "file separation" reel covers it.
- **D14. Commercial neutrality.** This is a named product on a clinician's account. Plain text name only, no logo, no
  color scheme borrowed from the brand, no superiority language, and the caption should state whether there is any
  sponsorship or relationship (the resident must supply that).
- **D15. Funding / conflicts of interest of the cited X7 studies were not systematically checked.** Fact-check: Al-Amidi
  2023 declares none; the PMC text of the other X7 papers that was retrieved carries no funding or conflict statement,
  which is not the same as "none".
- **D16 (added at fact-check). The evidence flags rest on old, conventional-NiTi bench work.** Berutti 2004 (ProTaper,
  resin blocks), Boessler 2007 (ProFile, milled dentin), Khademi 2006 (SEM, 40 canals), Haïkel 1999 and Pruett 1997 are
  all in vitro and none used a heat-treated file. They support the *habits*, not this product. One on-screen qualifier
  ("in vitro") per flag is the minimum.
- **D17 (added at fact-check). Hand-file size before the first rotary file.** IFU-2026 accepts a "#10 K file or larger";
  the US DFU asks for at least a #15 K to the terminus; the only in vivo X7 study went to #15 K; Patiño 2005 used hand
  files up to #20. No study shows that a #10 K alone is an adequate glide path for the 17/.04.
