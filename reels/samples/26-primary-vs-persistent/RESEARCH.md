**The one-sentence answer a board examiner would accept**
A primary infection (untreated necrotic pulp) is a mixed community of many species per canal, dominated by
obligate anaerobes, Gram-negative and Gram-positive alike; treatment removes most of it, and what is found in
a root-filled tooth with apical periodontitis is by culture and targeted PCR a much smaller community,
predominantly Gram-positive, with a higher share of facultatives (enterococci, streptococci, *Actinomyces*,
propionibacteria) and occasionally yeast. *Enterococcus faecalis* is the species most often detected
there by culture and targeted PCR, but it is rarely the dominant one, and sequencing studies show the post-treatment flora is more mixed
than the classic culture papers suggested.

**Related reels already published (keep numbers consistent):** 10-biofilm-succession (Fabricius 1982,
85–98 % obligate anaerobes in sealed monkey canals) and 09-caoh2-bacteria. Do not contradict them.

## (c) Corrections to the brief

1. **"Primary = largely Gram-negative" is wrong as stated.** Primary infections hold Gram-positive and
   Gram-negative species in roughly equal numbers (Figdor & Sundqvist 2007); what unites them is obligate
   anaerobiosis. Gram-positive anaerobes (*Parvimonas, Pseudoramibacter, Olsenella, Peptostreptococcus,
   Eubacterium*) are among the most frequent isolates in every classic series (Sundqvist 1992; Byström &
   Sundqvist 1981; Rôças & Siqueira 2008). **Consequence for the design:** the two Gram colors cannot be the two
   team colors. TEAM PRIMARY must deal a mixed pink-and-violet deck (anything from 5 : 5 to 6 : 4 pink : violet is defensible and
   the ratio is schematic: Figdor & Sundqvist say "approximately equal", the review's genus sentence lists 8
   Gram-negative and 9 Gram-positive genera, and its Table 1 lists 9 Gram-negative and 5 Gram-positive taxa for
   chronic apical periodontitis; fact-check count); TEAM PERSISTENT is the mostly violet one. The axis that separates the
   teams is oxygen tolerance and deck size, so give the "OBLIGATE ANAEROBE" / "FACULTATIVE" badge the visual weight.
2. **"Far fewer species per canal" holds for culture and targeted PCR only.** 1–2 strains by culture, 1–5 taxa
   by early molecular methods in adequately treated teeth; but a clone library found a mean of 10 taxa (Sakamoto
   2008), pyrosequencing a mean of 20 species (Zandi 2018), and Illumina sequencing a mean of 116 OTUs in the
   apical 3–5 mm of adequately treated teeth (Siqueira 2016). Sequencing comparisons disagree: two found no
   difference, one found persistent infections more diverse, two larger ones found primary infections more
   diverse. State the method next to the number.
3. **"Persistent = Gram-positive facultatives" overstates it.** The data say predominantly Gram-positive (83 %,
   Pinheiro 2003) with facultatives at 57–69 % of isolates (Pinheiro 2003; Molander 1998). Obligate anaerobes
   were still 43 % of species in Pinheiro's series, Figdor & Sundqvist describe "an equal distribution of
   facultative and obligate anaerobes", and the 2022 review says both dominate. Safe wording: "mostly
   Gram-positive, more facultatives".
4. **Rôças & Siqueira 2012 is in J Clin Microbiol (2012;50:1721-4), not J Endod.** 42 teeth by checkerboard,
   29 by qPCR. Exact abstract numbers: *E. faecalis* 38 % of cases and 9.76 % of counts; streptococci 41 % and
   65.78 %. The most prevalent taxon in that study was *Propionibacterium acnes* (52 %). *Fact-check:* the two
   "of counts" figures are mean shares within the positive teeth only (full text; see 5.6), so they cannot be
   shown as "10 % of the cells" or "66 % of the cells" in treated teeth.
5. **"Cutibacterium/Propionibacterium" mixes two organisms.** *Cutibacterium* is the new genus for the
   cutaneous species (*C. acnes*, formerly *P. acnes*; Scholz & Kilian 2016). The classic endodontic species
   *Propionibacterium propionicum* was moved to *Pseudopropionibacterium propionicum* (Scholz & Kilian 2016).
   Tindall 2019 points out that this name, *Propionibacterium propionicum* and the older *Arachnia propionica*
   share one type and are homotypic synonyms, and discusses the consequences. *Fact-check:* the abstract does
   not state which name wins, so "is now correctly *Arachnia propionica*" was softened; recent sequencing
   papers use both (*Arachnia* in Ordinola-Zapata 2023, *Pseudopropionibacterium propionicum* in 2024). Safe card title:
   "*Propionibacterium* (now *Cutibacterium* / *Arachnia*)".
6. ***Candida* is a yeast, not a Gram-positive bacterium,** and pooled data do not show it to be specific to
   persistent infection: fungal prevalence did not differ by infection type in a meta-analysis of 54 studies
   (Persoon 2017). The "up to 18 %" figure comes from single retreatment series. Give it its own badge and a
   pooled number.
7. **Sundqvist 1976 is a doctoral thesis and is not in PubMed.** I could not open it; its usual numbers (32
   teeth, 18 of 19, > 90 % anaerobes) rest on secondary citations here. For a PubMed-verifiable figure from the
   same group use Byström & Sundqvist 1981 (88 % of strains anaerobic, 1–10 strains per canal, 17 teeth).
8. **"Sundqvist 1992" is two papers.** Oral Microbiol Immunol 1992;7:257-62 is the 65-canal association study;
   J Endod 1992;18:427-30 is the "Ecology of the root canal flora" review. Sundqvist 1994 is Oral Surg Oral Med
   Oral Pathol 1994;78:522-30.
9. **Sundqvist et al. 1998: only the abstract-level statements are verified** (54 teeth; "mainly single species
   of predominantly gram-positive organisms"; *E. faecalis* most common; retreatment success 74 %). The often
   quoted 24/54, 9 teeth and 38 % are not in the abstract.
10. **Molander et al. 1998 has no DOI in its PubMed record** (Int Endod J 1998;31:1-7, PMID 9823122). Cite by PMID.
11. **Ricucci & Siqueira 2010 does not separate the two infection types by biofilm.** It shows apical biofilm
    in both (80 % untreated, 74 % treated). Use it to say both are biofilm infections, nothing more.
12. **Classic papers use old names.** *Peptostreptococcus micros* = *Parvimonas micra*; *Eubacterium
    alactolyticum* = *Pseudoramibacter alactolyticus*; *Bacteroides melaninogenicus/intermedius* = *Prevotella*;
    *Bacteroides forsythus* = *Tannerella forsythia*; *Wolinella recta* = *Campylobacter rectus*. Use current names on cards.
13. **"Species per canal by culture vs molecular methods; cell counts":** verified as 1–10 strains (culture,
    Byström & Sundqvist 1981), usually > 3 and at most 10 species (culture, Gomes 2004), 10–20 taxa (molecular,
    review), 10³–10⁸ cells (review). The textbook figure "10–30 species" (Siqueira & Rôças chapter in Cohen's
    Pathways of the Pulp) was not checked and is not used. *Fact-check addition:* the discussion of Rôças &
    Siqueira 2008 (full text) summarizes culture studies as "a mean number of 2.6 to 5.4 taxa per canal" in
    primary infections, against 7 (DGGE), 11 (T-RFLP), 10 to 12 (clone libraries) and 20 (culture plus clone
    library) by molecular methods. That is the cleanest statement of the culture-versus-molecular gap.
14. **"Secondary = entered after treatment" is too narrow** (fact-check). The source definition is "introduced in
    the root canal at some time after professional intervention": during treatment, between visits, or later
    through leakage. On screen: "got in during or after treatment".

## (d) Controversies and caveats (what must not be overstated)

- ***E. faecalis* as "the" cause of failure.** For: it is the species most often recovered from root-filled
  teeth with lesions, with about 7 times the odds versus untreated chronic infection (Zhang 2015), and it has
  documented survival traits. Against: it is rarely the dominant member (Rôças & Siqueira 2012; Rôças 2004;
  Siqueira 2016), it is as common in root-filled teeth without lesions (Zoletti 2006; Kaufman 2005), and some
  series hardly find it (Antunes 2015: 14 %; Anderson 2012: 2 of 21 by culture; Ordinola-Zapata 2023: 1 of 40
  root-end specimens, full text). A fair reading is "frequent finding in culture and PCR series, unproven as main
  pathogen". When it is present it can still dominate a given case (Antunes 2015), and one new sequencing study
  from India reports the opposite of the trend, *E. faecalis* "38.5% vs. 2.1%" in treated vs untreated molars
  (Shivanand 2026, 6.8; the abstract does not say whether that is abundance or prevalence).
  "Survivor" is itself an inference (see the origin bullet below); on screen "found" is safer than "survives".
- **How simple is the post-treatment flora?** Entirely method-dependent (correction 2). Culture misses
  uncultivated taxa (about 55 % of taxa in treated canals, Sakamoto 2008); sequencing also reads DNA from dead
  cells, can carry PCR and analysis artifacts, and root-end specimens can be contaminated in handling (all three
  are raised in the Siqueira 2016 discussion; "low-biomass contamination" was the researcher's gloss, not the
  paper's wording, and was removed in the fact-check). Neither extreme is the truth. Sequencing tally so far: no
  difference in diversity in three studies (Hong 2013; Keskin 2017; Park 2024), persistent more diverse in one
  (Tzanetakis 2015, nominal significance, 79 % poor fillings), primary more diverse in three (Ordinola-Zapata
  2023 and 2024, one group, different sampling for the two arms; Shivanand 2026).
- **"Mostly Gram-positive" is a culture-era finding.** 83–85 % of isolates by culture (4.4, 3.5), but sequencing
  of adequately treated root apices found mostly Gram-negative phyla (Proteobacteria 46 %, Fusobacteria 15 % of
  sequences; Siqueira 2016) and *Fusobacterium* was the predominant genus in treated teeth in Ordinola-Zapata
  2023. Keep "by culture" attached to it, at least in the caption.
- **Treatment quality confounds every comparison.** Tzanetakis 2015 found "more diverse" persistent infections
  in a sample where 79 % of root fillings were poor; short-filled canals held more taxa than well-filled ones
  in the same PCR panel (mean 5 vs 3, 4.6).
- **Primary vs secondary vs persistent cannot be assigned in a sampled root-filled tooth.** Non-oral organisms
  (*Pseudomonas*, enteric rods, staphylococci) are read as hints of secondary infection (Siqueira 2016; Niazi
  2010 calls *P. acnes* and *S. epidermidis* in refractory cases "likely to be nosocomial"), but that is inference.
- **Where *E. faecalis* comes from** (survivor vs latecomer through leakage or multiple visits) is unsettled;
  Siqueira & Rôças 2009 suggest it "may be a secondary invader". Do not animate it as a proven survivor of the
  primary deck, and do not animate it as a proven latecomer either.
- **All of this is cross-sectional association.** "Any species found in the canal has the potential to be an
  endodontic pathogen" (Siqueira & Rôças 2009). *Fact-check:* "causation still remains unproven" is in the same
  review but is said of *E. faecalis* only, not of the field; it was moved out of this general sentence. For the
  general point the 2008 review says it "remains to be determined by longitudinal studies if any
  species/phylotypes persisting after treatment procedures can influence outcome", and the 2022 review treats
  the community as the unit of pathogenicity.
- **Sampling site matters.** Paper points sample the main canal; the apical 3–5 mm and its ramifications may
  differ (Siqueira 2016; Siqueira 2024).
- **Geography and era.** Scandinavian, Brazilian, Lithuanian and US series give different *E. faecalis* rates
  (30 % of culture-positive teeth in North Carolina vs 64–70 % in Lithuania).
- **Symptomatic root-filled teeth look more like primary infections** (polymicrobial, anaerobes; Pinheiro 2003).
- **Yeast.** Low and heterogeneous prevalence, mostly high or unclear risk of bias (Mergoni 2018).
- ***E. faecalis* survival data are in vitro or ex vivo**, single strains, and pH inside dentin is lower than
  in the paste. Reel 09 already covers this; keep the two consistent.

## (e) Do not claim

- Do not say *E. faecalis* "causes" failed root canals, or that it is the dominant organism in failures.
- Do not say persistent infections are monoinfections, or that they contain "one species". Say "1–2 by culture" with the method.
- Do not say primary infections are Gram-negative infections.
- Do not put Sundqvist 1976 or Sundqvist 1998 numbers (18/19, > 90 %, 24/54, 38 %) on screen.
- Do not say *Candida* occurs only, or mainly, in persistent infections. Do not call it Gram-positive.
- Do not say anaerobes cannot survive treatment; anaerobes are regularly found in treated teeth (*Fusobacterium*,
  *P. alactolyticus*, *Dialister*, *F. alocis*).
- Do not present the stat bars as measured values, and do not print a "pH 11.5 kills" line without "in vitro".
- Do not state that *E. faecalis* resists NaOCl; none of the sources read here says so.
- Do not give a single "species per canal" number without the method (culture / PCR panel / sequencing).
- Do not call "persistent" and "secondary" synonyms; define both, then use "persistent/secondary" for the root-filled tooth.
- Do not imply that biofilm distinguishes the two; both are biofilm infections.
- Do not use the phylum names as team labels (Firmicutes, Bacteroidetes were renamed Bacillota, Bacteroidota in 2021; not checked here, and unnecessary).

Added or moved here by the fact-check (2026-11-22):
- Do not print "*E. faecalis*: ~10 % of cells" (or "9.76 % of the bacteria in treated teeth"). That figure is the
  mean share inside the 11 positive teeth of one 29-tooth qPCR series; the median there was 1.1 %.
- Do not print "Survivors: 85 % Gram-positive · 107 teeth". It is 85 % of 248 isolates, from canals that were
  vital, necrotic or root-filled before treatment.
- Do not write "SECONDARY: entered after treatment". Secondary infection can be introduced during treatment.
- Do not show "10–20 taxa" and "1–5 taxa" as if one method had measured both. Method under each number.
- Do not badge every primary card "obligate anaerobe" or write "obligate anaerobes" without "mostly" (88 % of
  strains in the best culture series, about 70 % in others).
- Do not state the Gram-positive majority in treated teeth as a sequencing-era fact; it is a culture finding.
- Do not attach "10 studies, 972 teeth" to the odds ratio of 7.2 (it was computed on a subset), and do not
  print "1.9 % of reads" for *Enterococcus* in Siqueira 2016 (denominator unclear).
- Do not write "116 species" for the Illumina result; they are OTUs.
- Do not say treatment "removes" or "kills" 99 % of bacteria; the qPCR study measured a > 99 % fall in bacterial
  DNA in canals that were still positive.
- Do not print the *Candida* 8 % as a property of persistent infection; it is pooled over all infection types.
- Do not quote Shivanand 2026 (6.8) or Tzanetakis 2015 (6.4) on screen as the answer to "which is more diverse".
- Do not name *Arachnia propionica* as "the correct name" on screen; the abstract read here does not settle it.
