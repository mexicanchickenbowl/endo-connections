# 39 — Ten factors: periapical healing after periapical surgery (ten-pin bowling study aid)

## Concept
A retro bowling alley. Ten pins in the classic 4-3-2-1 triangle stand under a vintage back-lit overhead score sheet
("10 FACTORS"). The ten pins are the ten factors that Ng & Gulabivala (ch. 17, *Cohen's Pathways of the Pulp*,
12th ed.) list for the outcome (periapical healing) of periapical surgery. Each factor is dealt one at a time: its
lamp comes on, its pin lights up, its numbered line is written into the score sheet (factor name, the side associated
with better healing as a solid pill, the other side dimmed), the header counter flips to "n /10" and one more dot of
the ten-dot pin indicator fills in. Nothing is knocked down — no ball is thrown — so the picture never suggests that
a factor is "eliminated".

Evidence grouping is carried by colour everywhere at once:
- **teal** = the chapter's unpublished pooled analysis = pins 1–7 (the back seven: rows of 4 and 3), teal neck bands,
  teal score-sheet strip, teal number discs, teal dots, lane label "BACK 7 · pooled";
- **coral** = individual studies = pins 8–10 (the front three: rows of 2 and 1), coral bands, coral strip, discs,
  dots, lane label "FRONT 3 · individual studies".

The score sheet builds itself into the summary: every line stays on screen from the moment it appears, so the last
4.5 s is a complete, screenshot-ready table of all ten with both evidence groups labelled. Poster = t 27.8 s.

Duration 30.0 s (the brief allows up to 30 s for a ten-item study aid), silent, loops (the lights go down in the
last 0.45 s and the sheet returns to the empty state of t = 0).

Pin numbers are the factor numbers in the chapter's order, running back row → head pin. This is deliberately not
regulation pin numbering (where the head pin is 1): the brief maps the back seven to the pooled analysis.

## What could not be verified
- **Edition.** The PDF prints the chapter number, title and authors (Yuan-Ling Ng and Kishor Gulabivala) and the
  Part III outline, but not the edition. "12th ed." is taken from the resident's description; the part outline and
  page numbers (ch. 17 starts p. 652) are consistent with it.
- **Direction of factors 8–10.** The chapter names the three factors and gives risk ratios but does not say which
  category does better. The directions on screen come from the PubMed abstracts of the studies the chapter cites.
  For #8 the Villa-Machado abstract reports a univariate association that did not stay independent after
  multivariate regression. Full texts were not read, and the chapter's three risk ratios were not re-derived.
- **The pooled analysis itself** is unpublished (personal communication / unpublished manuscript, 2014), so its
  methods cannot be checked. The chapter notes that the pooled studies differ in follow-up time and radiographic
  criteria. The reel says "unpublished (per chapter)" on screen for that reason.
- Factor 2: the chapter's 95% CI reaches 1.0 (1.0, 1.5). It is listed because the chapter lists it.

## Schematic / artistic licence
- The alley is decoration. The target arrows are drawn near the pin deck for the look (real ones sit near the foul
  line), and pin numbers follow the chapter's order, not regulation pin numbering.
- No numbers other than "≤5 mm / >5 mm" and the counts 10, 7 and 3 are on screen. No risk ratios, no success rates.

## Text budget
About 95 must-read words, which is above the usual ~60-word budget: this is a ten-item study aid and the brief asks
for every factor with both sides. Sizes: title 96 px; subtitle 34 px; factor names 40 px (row 8: 37 px); favorable
pills 36 px; "vs" side 32 px; second line of row 7 and the row-8 tag 30 px; strips 30 px (labels and right-hand notes);
bezel scope line 30 px; lane labels 36/30 px; pin numerals about 45–50 px on screen. Fine print: source line 24 px,
handle 26 px.

## How it is built
- `index.html` — timeline, the 2-D overhead display, light beams, counter; one full-frame 2-D canvas (fast path).
- `alley.js` — three.js alley: regulation pin profile (lathe), canvas textures (band + numeral per pin, maple lane),
  mirrored reflection pass, shadowed key light, per-pin spotlight.
- Software WebGL needs 1–2 s per frame here, so the 3-D scene is rendered only 21 times (lazily, the first time each
  is needed) and kept as additive light layers: DARK, LIT[i] − DARK, SPOT[i] − LIT[i]. Every video frame is then a
  2-D composite: DARK + Σ lit_i·LIT_i + Σ spot_i·SPOT_i. Fully deterministic; `render(t)` depends only on `t`.
- "≤" is not in the vendored condensed fonts (latin + latin-ext only); it is drawn as "<" plus a bar.

## Independent review

Reviewer did not build the reel. What was checked, and how:

**Chapter (read independently).** The whole text extraction of the resident's copy of the chapter was searched, and
the section "Factors affecting periapical health or healing following periapical surgery and root-end filling" was
read in full, together with the survival and concluding sections after it. Because the text layer garbles ≤, > and =,
the two book pages carrying the list (pp. 695 and 699) were also rendered to images and read visually.
- Seven bullets from the "(as yet) unpublished meta-analyses" (ref. 130, an unpublished manuscript, 2014), then one
  sentence naming three more factors "revealed as significant prognostic factors by individual studies" (refs 249,
  222, 249). The 7 + 3 split, the order and all ten items match the resident's list.
- Threshold confirmed on the page image: "Small (≤5 mm) versus large (>5 mm)".
- Rows 1–7: factor, favorable side and wording match the chapter. Chapter words kept: "minimum" bevel and
  "retro-cavity" (the resident's list says "minimal" and "root-end cavity"; same meaning).
- Row 7: the chapter's bullet groups MTA with SuperEBA and IRM "versus amalgam", says MTA gave "a similar outcome" to
  SuperEBA or IRM, and gives its risk ratio against amalgam for SuperEBA and IRM. The on-screen wording follows that
  and does not claim a separate MTA-vs-amalgam figure.
- Rows 8–10: the chapter names the factors and gives risk ratios but does not say which category does better.
- Nothing on screen states or implies tooth survival, causation or a guarantee. The chapter treats tooth survival
  after surgery in a separate section (one study at the time of writing); it is not used.

**Cited studies (PubMed abstracts, re-read independently; no full text available in PMC).**
- Villa-Machado et al., Int Endod J 2013;46:1063–76, PMID 23560363,
  [doi:10.1111/iej.12100](https://doi.org/10.1111/iej.12100): presence of preoperative signs/symptoms and
  unsatisfactory coronal restoration were associated with the unfavorable outcome on univariate analysis; after
  multivariate regression only coronal restoration quality and root-end filling quality stayed independent.
  Supports the direction of rows 8 and 10 and the row-8 caveat.
- Song et al., J Endod 2013;39:1491–7, PMID 24238435,
  [doi:10.1016/j.joen.2013.08.026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.joen.2013.08.026): lesion type "isolated endodontic
  lesion" had a positive effect on outcome, compared with endodontic–periodontal lesions. Supports row 9.

**Decision for rows 8–10.** The favorable side stays on screen. Reasons: the direction is verified in the studies the
chapter itself cites; the three rows sit under a strip that names those studies; the chapter's own phrase
("preoperative presence of signs or symptoms" as a prognostic factor) points the same way; and ten rows in one format
keep the count of ten and the frame readable. Row 8 now carries "univariate only", and the caption says the favored
side of the front three comes from the cited studies, not from the chapter. For a quiz on the chapter itself, the
answer for 8–10 is the factor name; the chapter does not give a direction.

**Visual QA** (16 stills with the safe overlay, 19 timed stills, zoomed crops, before and after the edits).
- Rows numbered 1–10 once each; pin numerals match; back seven pins teal, front three coral, same as the strips,
  discs and indicator dots. Counter correct at every sampled step (1 at 2.3 s … 7 at 17.75 s, 8 at 18.1 s, 9 at
  22.65 s, 10 at 23.6 s).
- "≤" (drawn as "<" plus a bar) reads correctly. No clipping, overlap or missing glyphs.
- Nothing must-read in the top 230 px, bottom 340 px or the right-hand column; the lowest must-read item is the
  numeral on pin 10 (y ≈ 1530).
- Hook readable at t = 0. Summary complete from 23.55 s and held to 29.55 s (6 s). Poster (27.8 s) is the summary.
  Loop: the sheet is back to the empty t = 0 state by 29.95 s.
- Defects fixed: three must-read qualifiers were below 30 px (scope line 24 px; both strip notes 25 px); the dimmed
  "vs" side was about 4:1 contrast. See change log v2.

**Left as is, on purpose.**
- Pacing: each row is the active line for 2.0–3.1 s and is fully drawn 0.85 s in, which is less than `Kit.readSecs`
  for the row (2.3–2.9 s; 5.0 s for row 7). Rows are never removed and the full table holds for 6 s, so the reel
  meets the visibility rule, but a first-time viewer will need the hold or a pause. Ten two-sided rows cannot be read
  one by one inside 30 s.
- The source line and handle are fine print (24 and 26 px).
- Faint mirrored numerals in the lane reflections are decoration.

**Not verified.** The edition (the copy does not print it); the unpublished pooled analysis itself; the chapter's three
risk ratios for rows 8–10 against the papers' full texts.
