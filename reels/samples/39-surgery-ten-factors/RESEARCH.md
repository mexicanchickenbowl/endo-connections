- Lead-in to the list (pp. 694–695): factors "having a major impact" on the outcome of periapical surgery "in teeth

  from studies listed in Table 17.11 are as follows:" — then **seven bullets**.
- Ref. 130 = "Mehta D, Gulabivala K, Ng Y-L: Personal communication. Outcome of periapical surgery, unpublished

### The one real gap: direction of items 8–10

The chapter sentence lists the three factors and their risk ratios but does not say which category does better.
The direction shown in the reel therefore comes from the PubMed abstracts of the two studies the chapter cites:

- **Ref. 249 — Villa-Machado PA, Botero-Ramírez X, Tobón-Arroyave SI.** Retrospective follow-up assessment of
  prognostic variables associated with the outcome of periradicular surgery. *Int Endod J* 2013;46(11):1063–76.
  PMID 23560363. [doi:10.1111/iej.12100](https://doi.org/10.1111/iej.12100). 171 teeth / 154 subjects, 1–16 yr.
  Abstract (PubMed): univariate analysis showed "a positive association of the presence of preoperative
  signs/symptoms, unsatisfactory quality of the coronal restoration, pronounced root-end resection bevel and
  inadequate quality of root-end filling" with the unfavorable outcome; after multivariate regression only
  unsatisfactory coronal restoration and inadequate root-end filling stayed independently associated.
  → supports #8 (absent is favorable; **univariate only**) and #10 (satisfactory is favorable; independent).
  Citation in chapter verified on PubMed (authors, journal, year, volume, first page all match).
- **Ref. 222 — Song M, Kim SG, Lee SJ, Kim B, Kim E.** Prognostic factors of clinical outcomes in endodontic
  microsurgery: a prospective study. *J Endod* 2013;39(12):1491–7. PMID 24238435.
  [doi:10.1016/j.joen.2013.08.026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.joen.2013.08.026). 584 teeth, 431 recalled ≥12 months.
  Abstract (PubMed): "lesion type (isolated endodontic lesion)" had a positive effect on outcome, compared with
  endodontic–periodontal lesions (with sex, tooth position and arch type). → supports #9.
  Citation in chapter verified on PubMed; the chapter's reference list prints the pages as "39:14917"
  (typo for 1491–7).
- Supporting, not cited by the chapter for these three: von Arx T, Peñarrocha M, Jensen S. Prognostic factors in
  apical surgery with root-end filling: a meta-analysis. *J Endod* 2010;36(6):957–73. PMID 20478447.
  [doi:10.1016/j.joen.2010.02.026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.joen.2010.02.026). Abstract: higher healed rates for
  "cases without preoperative pain or signs" and for "absence or size ≤ 5 mm of periapical lesion". (Chapter ref. 250.)

Only abstracts were read for these three papers (no full text). The risk ratios for items 8–10 are the chapter's
figures; they were not re-derived from the papers and are **not shown on screen**.

## Things deliberately left out of the reel (they are in the chapter, kept for the caption / notes)

- Pooled complete-healing estimate 67.5% (62.9–72.0%) from the same unpublished analysis; plateau after ~2 years.
- Factors the chapter lists as having "minimal effect": age, gender, general health, tooth type, radiographic quality
  of the preexisting root filling, histologic diagnosis (cyst or granuloma).
- Membranes / grafts for through-and-through defects: conflicting reports; no significant influence in the pooled data.
- Tooth survival after surgery: one study only at the time of writing — a different outcome, not part of this reel.

## Wording rules derived from the source

- "Associated with better healing" / "favorable side" — never causal language. The pooled analysis is unpublished and
  the chapter itself notes that the pooled studies vary in follow-up time and radiographic criteria.
- Scope line: teeth with a preoperative periapical radiolucency; outcome = periapical healing, not tooth survival.
- No risk ratios on screen (ten numbers with ten different denominators would add clutter and the direction of 8–10
  is not stated by the chapter); they are recorded here and in NOTES.md.
