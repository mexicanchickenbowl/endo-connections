# Reel 40 — evidence brief: eight conditions associated with better tooth survival after root canal treatment

Scope: TOOTH SURVIVAL (the tooth stays in the mouth) after nonsurgical root canal treatment. Periapical healing is a
separate outcome (sibling reel 41) and is deliberately not mentioned on screen.

## How the chapter itself summarizes it

The summary box opens: "The following conditions have been found to significantly improve tooth survival following root
canal treatment" and then lists exactly eight bullets. It closes with one extra sentence about ensuring "favorable
distribution of occlusal forces" when designing restorations (advice, not a ninth condition).

Note the chapter's verb is "improve". The reel uses association wording ("associated with better tooth survival;
associations, not guarantees") because the underlying evidence is observational (cohort studies and meta-analyses of
them).

### In the chapter's survival section but NOT in its eight-item summary (so not on screen)
- Patient factors: diabetes and systemic steroid therapy were linked to a higher chance of extraction (ref 151).
- Preoperative cervical resorption (ref 151).
- A preoperative periapical lesion had no significant influence on tooth survival (ref 151). Not shown: the brief asks
  to keep periapical topics out of this reel.
- Pooled survival figures (Fig. 17.26): 0.93 at 2 years, 0.92 at 5 years, 0.88 at 10 years. Not shown: the reel is
  the list of conditions, and numbers would need their denominators on screen.

## Cross-check with the primary papers (PubMed abstracts)

**Ng, Mann & Gulabivala 2011 (part 2, prospective cohort; 759 primary and 858 secondary treatments by endodontic
postgraduates, followed 2 to 4 years; Cox regression).** Thirteen prognostic factors. Agreement with the chapter list:

| Chapter item | In Ng 2011 abstract? |
|---|---|
| 1 nonmolar | Not among the thirteen factors named in the abstract. In the chapter it comes from the pooled analysis (Fig. 17.27, ref 149). |
| 2 both adjacent teeth | Yes: "proximal contacts with both mesial and distal adjacent teeth". |
| 3 not distal-most | Yes: "terminal location of the tooth". |
| 4 cast restoration | Yes, but worded as "cast restoration versus temporary restoration"; the abstract does not restrict it to molars. |
| 5 cast post and core | Yes: "presence of cast post and core". |
| 6 abutment | Not among the thirteen factors in the abstract (consistent with the chapter's remark that n = 94 was too small). |
| 7 preoperative conditions | Yes: "narrow but deep periodontal probing depth; pain; discharging sinus; and iatrogenic perforation (for 2°RCTx cases only)". So the perforation item applied to retreatment cases only, and the probing defect is "narrow but deep". |
| 8 patency / extrusion | Yes: "patency at apical terminus; and extrusion of root fillings". Time-dependent: patency lowered tooth loss in the first 22 months (HR 0.3) with no significant effect later; gutta-percha extrusion had no effect in the first 22 months and raised the hazard afterwards (HR 3.0). |

Also in Ng 2011 but not in the chapter's eight: diabetes, systemic steroid therapy, intraoperative iatrogenic
perforation.

**Ng, Mann & Gulabivala 2010 (systematic review and meta-analysis, 14 studies).** Four conditions "significantly
improve tooth survival": a crown restoration after treatment; both mesial and distal proximal contacts; not an abutment
for a removable or fixed prosthesis; nonmolar tooth type. The abstract also says "Evidence for the effect of prognostic
factors on tooth survival was weak". This is the source of items 1 and 6 (and supports 2 and 4). Note: the 2010 review
says "removable or fixed prosthesis"; the chapter summary says "fixed prosthesis".

## Wording rules derived from the above
- Say "associated with better tooth survival" and "associations, not guarantees". Never "causes", "ensures",
  "protects", "guarantees".
- All eight conditions on screen, numbered 1 to 8, in the chapter's order.
- No odds ratios or survival percentages on screen.
- Source line: "Ng & Gulabivala, in Cohen's Pathways of the Pulp, 12th ed., ch. 17 · Ng et al., Int Endod J 2011".
