# 31 — External inflammatory (infection-related) root resorption — evidence brief

Resorption series 3/5. Research stage only (no build). Prepared 2026-11-22.

**How this was checked.** Bibliographic data and abstracts were retrieved from PubMed (PMIDs and DOIs below).
Full text was read for: IADT 2020 avulsion guideline and IADT 2020 fractures/luxations guideline (AAPD
Reference Manual reprints of the Dent Traumatol papers), Patel et al. 2022 (PMC9790676), Abbott & Lin 2022
(PMC9543863), Lin et al. 2022 (PMC9546147) and the AAE/AAOMR 2015 CBCT position statement (hosted PDF).
Everything else is **abstract only** unless marked. Several classics have **no abstract in PubMed** (Andreasen &
Hjørting-Hansen 1966, Andreasen 1985, Tronstad 1988, Cvek 1973, Tronstad 1981, Hammarström 1986, Andreasen FM &
Pedersen 1985): their existence and citation details are verified, their content is not, and they are cited
below only through a secondary source that was read.

Terms: EIR = external inflammatory resorption (AAE Glossary / Patel); IADT 2020 calls the same entity
"infection-related (inflammatory) resorption"; Fuss 2003 "pulpal infection root resorption"; Tronstad 1988
"progressive inflammatory resorption"; Trope 1998 "peri-radicular root resorption of pulpal origin".

## (b) Recommended on-screen facts (safe wording, <= ~60 must-read words in total)

1. **Equation (the hook):** `DAMAGED ROOT SURFACE + INFECTED CANAL = INFLAMMATORY RESORPTION`
   Source: Fuss 2003; Andreasen 1981. (Optional small line under the first term: "luxation / avulsion".)
   `= FAST RESORPTION` is also acceptable: "fast" is supported qualitatively (T1 "rapid extension", T4 "very rapid in
   children") but must never be given a rate.
2. **Pathway label:** `TOXINS VIA DENTINAL TUBULES` (or "bacterial products via tubules"). Source: Andreasen 1981 (monkey histology).
3. **Lesion label:** `ROOT + BONE RESORB` / `BOWL-SHAPED CRATERS`. Source: Andreasen & Hjørting-Hansen 1966 (via Patel 2022); Abbott 2016; IADT 2020.
4. **Timer anchors (label the timer "schematic"):** `WEEK 1 — first lacunae (monkey histology)` -> `~1 MONTH — visible on X-ray` -> `MONTHS — root loss`.
   Sources: Andreasen 1980 Swed Dent J; Andreasen 1995 Endod Dent Traumatol; "months" = review statement (Patel 2022) and must not carry a number.
   Optional warning strip: `VERY RAPID IN CHILDREN — IADT 2020`.
5. **The fix:** `DISINFECT THE CANAL` + `Ca(OH)2 DRESSING` -> `STIMULUS CUT`. Source: Fuss 2003; IADT 2020 (Bourguignon, section 13.3).
6. **Outcome number (one only):** `97% HEALED — 192/197 luxated incisors, Ca(OH)2 then GP, radiographic` — `Cvek 1992 Endod Dent Traumatol`.
7. **End card:** `TREAT THE CANAL — EARLY`, with one guideline line:
   `Replanted mature tooth: start RCT within 2 weeks — IADT 2020` (and/or `Resorption seen? Start RCT immediately`).

Optional (caption rather than screen): ~23% of replanted avulsed teeth, pooled, very-low-certainty (Souza 2018).

## (c) Corrections to the brief

1. **"Trope 2002 Dent Traumatol 'Root resorption due to dental trauma' (Endod Topics 2002)"** conflates two papers.
   "Root resorption due to dental trauma" is **Endodontic Topics 2002;1:79-100** (not PubMed-indexed; content not
   verified). Trope's **Dent Traumatol 2002;18:1-11** paper is "Clinical management of the avulsed tooth: present
   strategies and future directions" (PMID 11841460). Do not cite either for a number.
2. **"Andreasen JO 1981/1985"** are two different papers. The study is **J Endod 1981;7:294-301** (monkeys;
   reprinted J Endod 2021;47:865-72). The 1985 item is a review, Int Endod J 18:109-18, with no abstract. Cite 1981 only.
3. **"Andreasen and Hjørting-Hansen 1966"** is two papers (Acta Odontol Scand 24:263-86, 110 teeth, radiographic;
   24:287-306, 22 teeth, histologic). No abstracts — cite "as summarized by Lin 2022 / Patel 2022".
4. **"Cvek 1973/1992 success figures":** the only verifiable figure is **Cvek 1992 — 192/197 (97%)** radiographic healing of
   inflammatory resorption in luxated non-vital incisors treated with Ca(OH)2 then gutta-percha (5 went on to ankylosis).
   Cvek 1973 has no abstract; do not attach a number to it.
5. **IADT 2020 wording.** "Within 2 weeks" and "calcium hydroxide up to 1 month" are correct, but (i) they apply to
   **closed-apex** replanted teeth — open-apex teeth are *not* root-treated unless necrosis/infection is evident;
   (ii) the corticosteroid or corticosteroid/antibiotic option has a different protocol: "placed immediately or shortly
   after replantation and left in situ for at least 6 weeks"; (iii) that passage is about **prevention** after
   replantation. For **established** resorption the IADT text is in Part 1 (Bourguignon et al. 2020, section 13.3):
   start RCT immediately; Ca(OH)2 for 3 weeks, then replaced every 3 months until the radiolucencies disappear.
6. **Older "7-10 days" rule is superseded** (Trope 1995; earlier guidelines). IADT 2020 says "within 2 weeks".
   Note that Patel et al. 2022 still write "7-10 days" while citing Fouad 2020 — do not copy that.
7. **"Bacteria travel through the tubules"** — Andreasen's wording is diffusion of "toxic elements" from infected
   necrotic pulp (or an "infected leukocyte zone") once the resorption cavity has **penetrated the cementum and reached
   tubules**. So: trauma damages the surface -> ordinary surface resorption begins -> when a cavity reaches tubules that
   communicate with an infected canal, it becomes progressive inflammatory resorption. Label the moving particles
   "toxins" or "bacteria + toxins", not bacteria alone.
8. **"Needs BOTH surface damage and infection"** — correct for the post-trauma (lateral) form. But EIR is also the apical
   resorption of ordinary apical periodontitis, where the pathway is the apical foramen (Laux 2000: 81% histologically;
   Vier 2002: 87%). Keep "after trauma" on the corner/equation and put this nuance in the caption.
9. **"Faster in young teeth"** — supported as "very rapid in children" (IADT 2020) and by age effects (Bastos 2014,
   Andersson 1989). It is **not** established that it is *more frequent* in immature teeth: Tewari 2024 found higher
   EIRR risk in mature-apex teeth after lateral luxation (pulp necrosis is more likely), and immature teeth may
   revascularize. Say "faster in children / young patients", not "more common in immature teeth".
10. **"Craters stop and repair"** — arrest is well supported. "Repair" means a cementum-like lining, re-formed PDL space
    and bone fill; the lost dentin is not regenerated and the defect contour remains (Cvek 1992 links healed cervical
    defects to later cervical root fractures). Do not animate the crater refilling to the original outline.
11. **Bryson et al. 2002** is correct (Dent Traumatol 18:316-21) but is a **dog** study of *immediate* intracanal
    Ledermix vs Ca(OH)2 after 60 min dry time — prevention, not treatment of established EIR.
12. **Fuss et al. 2003** = Fuss, Tsesis, Lin; Dent Traumatol 19:175-82; their term is "pulpal infection root resorption".
13. **Timer values:** only T1 (1 week, monkey histology) and T2 (about 1 month, human radiographs) are sourced.
    "Weeks to months" for destruction is a review-level statement without measurements.

## (d) Controversies and caveats (do not overstate)

- **Name.** IADT: "infection-related (inflammatory)". Abbott & Lin 2022 (Dent Traumatol 38:267-85, PMID 35605161) argue
  "inflammatory" is the better term (infection alone is not sufficient) and split it into external **apical** and external
  **lateral** inflammatory resorption; post-trauma = usually lateral. "Bowl-shaped" is classic but "not always the case".
- **Calcium hydroxide vs corticosteroid-antibiotic paste first.** IADT 2020 recommends Ca(OH)2 (paste optional).
  Abbott (2016; 2022) says Ca(OH)2 "should not be used as an immediate medicament" and prefers corticosteroid-antibiotic
  paste first. Animal data favor Ledermix when placed immediately (Bryson 2002; Chen et al. Dent Traumatol 2008;24:74-8,
  PMID 18173671; Pierce & Lindskog, Oral Surg 1987;64:216-20, PMID 3114696). In monkeys with a necrotic PDL, immediate
  Ca(OH)2 shifted healing toward ankylosis (Lengheden et al. Scand J Dent Res 1991;99:139-46, PMID 2052895). The only
  human RCT (Day 2012, 27 teeth) found no significant difference and was underpowered. No RCTs on treating established
  external resorption (Cochrane 2015).
- **Short vs long-term Ca(OH)2.** Dog data: no difference when started at 14 days (Trope 1992); long-term better for
  *established* resorption (Trope 1995). Against long-term use: weakened immature roots (Andreasen 2002; Cvek 1992
  cervical fractures). Monkey study found no difference between long-term Ca(OH)2 and gutta-percha, with replacement
  resorption dominating (Dumsha & Hovland, Int Endod J 1995;28:7-11, PMID 7642331).
- **How early is early?** Evidence is observational: >14 days is associated with inflammatory resorption (Hinckfuss
  2009); <=10 days not shown to be better than 10-14; Kinirons 1999 "not critical unless the delay exceeded 20 days";
  Bastos 2014 "each day" +1.2%. The guideline figure (2 weeks) is consensus.
- **Systemic antibiotics** reduced inflammatory resorption in dogs (Sae-Lim et al. Endod Dent Traumatol 1998;14:216-20,
  PMID 9855800) but showed no effect in Bastos 2014 (humans, mature teeth). They do not arrest established EIR.
- **Incidence figures are soft.** Pooled 23% after replantation rests on very-low-certainty observational data;
  single-center figures range widely.
- **Radiographs underestimate.** Buccal/lingual defects are missed on periapicals (Durack 2011; Estrela et al. J Endod
  2009;35:1491-7, PMID 19840636: resorption seen in 68.8% of radiographs vs 100% of CBCT scans; 48 radiograph/CBCT
  pairs from 40 patients).
- **Regenerative endodontics** for EIR in immature teeth: promising, case-report level.
- **Animal evidence.** Mechanism and treatment histology come from monkeys and dogs; human evidence is radiographic.

## (e) Do not claim

- A resorption rate, or "the root is gone in X weeks".
- That every replanted or luxated tooth develops it, or that concussion/subluxation commonly do.
- "97% success" without its denominators (Cvek 1992: luxated non-vital incisors, Ca(OH)2 then GP, radiographic, 192/197).
- That IADT says 7-10 days (2020: within 2 weeks), or that RCT is routine for open-apex replanted teeth.
- That calcium hydroxide (or any medicament) regrows the lost root, or that craters "fill back in" with dentin.
- That Ca(OH)2 is proven superior/inferior to corticosteroid-antibiotic paste in humans.
- That antibiotics stop it.
- That it only happens after trauma (it is also the apical resorption of apical periodontitis).
- That it is more *frequent* in immature teeth (say faster / very rapid in children).
- Numbers attributed to Cvek 1973, Tronstad 1988, Andreasen 1985, Trope 2002 (Endod Topics) or Andreasen &
  Hjørting-Hansen 1966 directly.
- Do not draw bacteria arriving from the gingival sulcus (that is the cervical/invasive story) or bone replacing root
  (replacement resorption = another reel in the series).
