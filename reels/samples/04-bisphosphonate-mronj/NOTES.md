# 04 · Bisphosphonates, osteoclasts & MRONJ — "Extract or endo?"

**Style:** Swiss / Bauhaus poster. Off-white paper (#EEE9DD), black ink, signal red (#E1251B), one accent (ochre #D6981F).
Strict 6-column grid (faint column hairlines left visible as a Swiss "construction" texture), giant Bebas Neue section
numerals, Archivo Black headlines, Inter labels. Flat shapes only, no gradients or shadows.
**Technique:** kinetic geometric storytelling on one full-frame 2D canvas. Text enters and leaves through masks
(slides up through its own line box), shapes pop with eased overshoot, and wipes run along the grid.
The bottom third of the frame is a permanent black "bone" field with a faint hexagonal apatite texture. Every
actor (drug, osteoclast, tooth) works on that one surface, so the story stays continuous.
**Duration:** 30 s, 1080×1920, 30 fps, silent. Poster frame t = 1.6 s (the hook).

## Concept
A clinician's question is the hook: *"Patient on a bisphosphonate: EXTRACT or ENDO?"* The reel then shows the
mechanism in four poster "plates": the P–C–P hook binds bone mineral, most where bone remodels; the osteoclast resorbs,
swallows the drug, loses function and dies; turnover slows (not stops) while the drug stays in bone for years; then the
decision split with the AAOMS 2022 post-extraction risk numbers. It ends on a red answer plate, **ENDO.**, stratified by
risk. In the last 0.5 s the red plate lifts off the already-composed hook, so the loop reads as answer → question.

## Corrections to the brief
- **"Jaw bone has high turnover."** The comparative evidence is mostly animal histomorphometry (e.g. Huja & Beck, *Anat Rec* 2008, PMID 18085627, in dogs). I did not state it as a fact on screen. The reel says "most where bone remodels" (general BP pharmacology) and "turnover slows" (Allen & Burr 2009 show BP-reduced remodelling in the jaw).
- **"Non-surgical endo beats extraction."** There is no head-to-head MRONJ incidence after RCT vs extraction; AAOMS 2022 calls the risk after endodontic procedures *unknown*. So the right column shows no number: it says "NO SOCKET" and "MRONJ risk here: not quantified". The recommendation is attributed to guidance (AAE 2010, AAOMS 2022), not to outcome data.
- **Applies mainly to high-risk patients.** AAOMS 2022 says elective dentoalveolar surgery is not contraindicated in osteoporosis patients. The takeaway fine print says so, so the message isn't overstated for the large osteoporosis population.
- **Which risk figures to show.** The brief suggested overall BP vs IV risk. I showed the *post-extraction* risks from the same AAOMS paper, because those answer the clinical question in the reel. The overall figures are listed above.
- **"Collapse".** For N-BPs the primary antiresorptive effect is loss of osteoclast function. Apoptosis is secondary and not required (Halasy-Nagy 2001), and osteoclast numbers can even rise in patients (Weinstein 2009). v2 therefore shows loss of function first (ruffled border retracts, cell detaches) and apoptosis second (round-up, then apoptotic bodies). The caption reads "LOSS OF FUNCTION / APOPTOSIS".
- **Retention ≠ effect.** 10+ years is the *estimated* skeletal retention of alendronate, not the duration of suppressed turnover. v2 says "retained in bone" and the footer says "drug retained ≠ effect lasting".
- **Stratification.** The non-surgical RCT advice is strongest for cancer-dose antiresorptives. v2 puts this into the main takeaway rows, not fine print.

## What is schematic / modelled
- The molecule glyph is schematic: the R1 (–OH) group is omitted and the N side chain is an abstract ochre disc. Not to scale.
- The Ca²⁺/hydroxyapatite inset is schematic. The chelation bonds are illustrative, not a crystal structure.
- The osteoclast is a half-dome with 5 nuclei and a comb-like ruffled border. Real osteoclasts carry many more nuclei. The pit depth and the number of molecules are illustrative. The apoptotic bodies are schematic.
- The **remodelling-rate bar is a toy model**, labelled on screen. Its 30% floor is chosen to sit inside the measured 60–90% suppression. The ochre blips are schematic remodelling units.
- The teeth, the obturation (ochre), the socket hole with its red rim and stalled ochre repair front, and the periapical lesion are all schematic. The lesion shrinks to a remnant within seconds, but real healing takes months (footer: "healing: months").
- The footers carry "Schematic, not to scale", "Toy model" and "Schematic".

## Design rationale
- **Swiss/Bauhaus grammar:**
  - Hierarchy comes from scale contrast: 300–380 px numerals against 24–40 px labels.
  - Strict left alignment on the 64 px margin, a visible column grid, primary geometry (circle = cell, square = carbon/full stop, bar = bone), and only 3 colours plus ochre.
  - Colour is semantic. Red = drug and risk (risk numbers, socket rim, lesion). Ochre = biology and repair (osteoclast, N side chain, remodelling, obturation, the recommended ENDO column). Black = bone and type. Chapter numerals are ink outlines, so solid red is kept for data. The takeaway is the first time the answer "owns" red.
- **One surface, one focal point:** the black bone field never moves, and each plate has one actor on it. Labels in the bone are white-on-black, everything else is ink on paper.
- **Kinetic type:**
  - Every text line enters and leaves through its own mask (no fades for headlines), which keeps the poster crisp at every frame.
  - The three-word RESORB / SWALLOW / COLLAPSE headline is the story's spine: each word lights up on its biological beat.
  - Plate cuts overlap: the next plate's type rolls in while the old one rolls out, and a full-width ink bar sweeps under the header.
- **Legibility:** headlines 84–150 px; captions 32–40 px; labels at least 30 px; fine print 22–24 px (one footer line per plate). All readable text sits within x 64–1016 and y 230–1580; nothing readable is in the right-hand button column. The hook reads inside the 3:4 grid crop.
- **Loop:** the red answer plate lifts upward off the composed question plate (the mirror of its downward wipe), and the treated tooth slides out. The last frame matches frame 0.
