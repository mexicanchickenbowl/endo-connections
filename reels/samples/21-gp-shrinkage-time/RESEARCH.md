# 21 — How long does warm gutta-percha keep shrinking? — evidence brief

Research stage only (nothing built). Compiled 2026-11-22. Bibliographic data and abstracts were retrieved from
**PubMed** (abstract read for every PubMed paper cited; PMC full text read for Hsu 2020; PMC full text queried for
Tait 2025, Vishwanath 2019, Huang 2023, Dijanic 2026). Two non-PubMed sources were read directly: the OCR full text
of A. S. Goodman's Boston University thesis (the data behind the Schilder/Goodman/Aldrich series) from the BU
repository, and the abstract of Dong 2019 (Polymer Testing) from the Semantic Scholar record. "Verified from" says
exactly what was read. Anything recalled from memory but not found in an abstract/full text is marked
**NOT VERIFIED** and must stay off screen. DOIs are given for every source that has one.

**The one-paragraph answer (what the literature actually supports)**

1. Warm gutta-percha (GP) does not "set" — it cools, stiffens around 50→40 °C and then **crystallizes**. Most of the
   measured shrinkage happens while it cools to 37 °C, within minutes (Lottanti 2014: 1.0–2.3 % *linear* in a
   10-minute window; Capurro 1993: changes significant by 5 min, no further significant change 5→30 min).
2. It does **not** stop when it reaches body temperature. In the only PubMed-indexed study that timed it (Lee 1997,
   volume dilatometer, four thermoplastic GP products held at 37 °C for 24 h) shrinkage **continued for 45 min to
   10 h** before the volume stabilized. Goodman's 1973 dilatometry (thesis behind Schilder 1985) independently
   reports contraction that "continued over a 24 hour holding period" at 37 °C.
3. Beyond 24 h nobody has measured GP volume. There are **no data** for days, weeks or years. What long-term in vitro
   data exist point the other way (less leakage of sealer-free GP at 6 months; fewer voids on micro-CT at 54 months).
4. Everything above is bench data on free bulk material. No clinical study links this shrinkage to outcome, and
   warm vs cold obturation shows no outcome difference in systematic reviews.

## (b) Recommended on-screen facts (pick 5–7; wording is safe as written)

1. **Hook:** "Warm GP feels set in seconds. When does it stop shrinking?"
2. **COOLING (minutes):** "1.0–2.3 % linear shrinkage in the first 10 min" + qualifier "75→37 °C, in vitro" —
   source line `Lottanti 2014 J Endod`. (T5)
3. **CRYSTALLIZING (hours):** "At 37 °C it kept shrinking 45 min – 10 h" + "4 products, dilatometer" —
   source line `Lee 1997 J Endod`. (T1) This is the headline fact of the reel.
4. **STABLE:** "then stable — watched for 24 h" (Lee 1997). Stamp wording must carry "in vitro". (T1)
5. **After 24 h:** "not measured" (L4). Draw the gap in the record; do not draw a line.
6. **Why:** "shrinkage ≈ crystallization" (Dong 2019: ~99 % from the crystalline phase transition; Grassi 1989:
   2.2–2.6 % isothermal). Optional — only if it fits as a 2-word stamp; the stamp CRYSTALLIZING already says it. (T8, T9)
7. **What to do:** "Hold pressure while it cools" + "sealer takes the rest" with qualifier "in vitro evidence" —
   source line `Marlin & Schilder 1973 · Wu 2000`. (C1, C3)

Real time anchors for the time axis (everything between them is schematic): **0**, **10 min** (end of Lottanti's
window), **45 min** and **10 h** (Lee's range), **24 h** (end of Lee's and Goodman's observation).
Real temperature anchors for the blue pen: extrusion **58–104 °C** (gun set 200 °C; T6) or the **75 °C** start of
Lottanti's controlled test, and **37 °C**. The seconds it takes to get to 37 °C are not verified → the blue trace

## (c) Corrections to the brief

1. **The key paper was missing from the notes.** Lee, Chang, Cobb, Robinson, Hellmuth, J Endod 1997;23:579-82 is the
   only PubMed-indexed study that reports *how long* shrinkage continues at body temperature (45 min–10 h, 24-h hold).
2. **Lottanti 2014 does not answer "how long".** Correct citation: Lottanti S, Tauböck TT, Zehnder M. J Endod
   2014;40(5):721-4. It measured **linear** (not volume) shrinkage during a controlled 75→37 °C drop and reports the
   total "after 10 minutes". It is cooling-phase data; it neither shows nor excludes later shrinkage.
3. **"Tsukada et al. Dent Mater 2004" is the wrong journal.** It is J Oral Rehabil 2004;31(11):1139-44 (Tsukada G,
   Tanaka T, Torii M, Inoue K). The abstract has no shrinkage percentage and no time course.
4. **Meyer 2006** is Schweiz Monatsschr Zahnmed 2006;116(4):356-61: 6.5–7.3 % on cooling 90→35 °C; no time course;
   and the α-GP product did **not** shrink less than conventional GP.
5. **Schilder/Goodman/Aldrich series, exact attribution:** Part I (Schilder first author, compressibility) 1974;37:946-53,
   PMID 4524894; Part II (Goodman first, history/chemistry) 1974;37:954-61, PMID 4598930; Part III (Schilder first,
   phase transitions) 1974;38:109-14, PMID 4525950; Part IV (**Goodman** first, thermal profile) **1981**;51:544-51,
   PMID 6941146; Part V (Schilder first, volume changes) **1985**;59:285-96, PMID 3856822. Volume/time data = Part V;
   the "24 hour holding period" wording was verified only in Goodman's thesis abstract.
6. **Hsu 2020 did not measure shrinkage.** It is DSC + rheometry on regular- vs soft-type backfill pellets; shrinkage
   is inferred from crystallization exotherms. The abstract ("exhibited pronounced volume shrinkage") overstates the
   full text ("may exhibit more obvious volume shrinkage"). Do not cite it for a shrinkage amount or time.
7. **Combe 2001** (Combe EC, Cohen BD, Cummings K, Int Endod J 2001;34:447-51) is thermal analysis of α/β content;
   no shrinkage data. Useful only for "most products are β" and "10 years' storage changed nothing".
8. **"After it has set" is the wrong mental model.** GP has no setting reaction. It stiffens steeply around
   50→40 °C and crystallizes; rigidity arrives long before the volume is stable — that is the point of the reel.
9. **"Minutes–hours–days?"** Evidence supports minutes and hours (≤10 h in one study, observed to 24 h). There is
   no evidence for days. The DAYS segment of the chart must be shown as *not measured*, not as a flat or falling line.
10. **Carrier-based vs injectable:** no verified comparative data. Leave it out.
11. **"Sealer fills the gap" is only indirectly supported** (more leakage without sealer at 48 h, Wu 2000), and sealers
    have their own dimensional change over weeks (ZOE types shrink 0.3–1 %; Ørstavik 2001). Word it as
    "sealer takes the rest" / "sealer covers the interface", not "sealer compensates the shrinkage".
12. **"Compaction during cooling"** is supported in vitro (Marlin & Schilder) and by technique convention (Tait 2025),
    not by clinical outcome data. Do not imply that more backfill increments are better: single vs multiple
    increments leaked the same (Johnson & Bond 1999).
13. Earlier reel 12-gp-phases already carries "keeps shrinking for minutes–hours (Lee 1997)". This reel should add
    the time axis and the "not measured after 24 h" honesty, not repeat the phase diagram.

## (d) Controversies and caveats (what must not be overstated)

1. **Magnitude depends on method, by nearly 10×.** Linear 1.0–2.3 % (Lottanti, 75→37 °C, 10 min); volume ~2 %
   (Dong); volume 2.2–2.6 % for isothermal crystallization alone (Grassi); −2.2 to −3.5 % for 80→24 °C (Lee);
   6.5–7.3 % for 90→35 °C (Meyer, type of % not stated); net loss vs the as-received cone only 0.03–1.1 % after a
   +3–5 % expansion (Goodman). These are not the same quantity. Never show one "true" percentage, never convert
   linear to volume, and always attach temperature range + method to any number.
2. **Lee 1997's magnitudes do not add up from the abstract** (+11.6–12.3 % expansion, −2.2 to −3.5 % shrinkage,
   net +5.5–7.2 %), and its expansion is 2–4× Goodman's (3–5 % at 80 °C). Use Lee for the *time* statement only.
3. **One study, 1997 products.** "45 min–10 h" is the spread across four products in a single dilatometer study.
   Modern backfill pellets differ (soft type crystallizes during cooling; regular type shows no exotherm at
   5–20 °C/min, Hsu 2020). The range may not transfer to today's materials. Say "one in vitro study".
4. **Free specimen ≠ canal.** All shrinkage data are on unconfined bulk GP (dilatometer, linometer-type device,
   blocks, glass tubes). In a tooth the GP is compacted under pressure, surrounded by sealer and dentin, and only
   part of it is heated above the transition: apical GP stays ≤45 °C (Goodman 1981; Venturi 2002) and so largely
   avoids the phase change (Schilder 1985). The reel's gap is an illustration, not a measurement: label
   "gap exaggerated / schematic" inside the safe area whenever it is visible.
5. **How the shrinkage is split between "cooling" and "after 37 °C" is unknown from the sources read.** Lottanti
   calls the early shrinkage "quick and extensive"; Lee shows a tail lasting hours. Draw a steep early drop and a
   slow tail, label the curve shape schematic, and do not put a y-axis scale on the hours segment.
6. **Clinical relevance is unproven.** No clinical study relates GP shrinkage to outcome. Meta-analysis and the
   systematic review behind the ESE S3 guideline found no outcome difference between obturation techniques
   (Peng 2007; Pirani & Camilleri 2023, high risk of bias). Long-term in vitro data do not show a growing gap:
   sealer-free GP leaked *less* at 6 months (Wu 2000) and voids *decreased* by 54 months (Liu 2021).
7. **Sealer change is a separate curve on a separate clock** (hours to weeks; direction depends on chemistry:
   ZOE shrinks, epoxy expands slightly, silicone expands slightly; Ørstavik 2001, Kazemi 1993, Hammad 2008). Heat
   also alters some sealers (Camilleri, J Endod 2015;41:72-8, PMID 25115660). If a sealer trace is drawn, it must be
   a different pen and labelled "different process".
8. **Long-term ageing** of GP in vivo is oxidative degradation of the polymer over 5–15+ years (Maniglia-Ferreira
   2007). That is chemistry, not a measured volume change. Do not draw it as shrinkage.
9. **α vs β:** the claim that α-phase GP shrinks less is widely repeated but was not supported by the one direct
   measurement found (Meyer 2006). Unsettled; leave it out.

## (e) Do not claim

- "GP shrinks X % in the canal" — every number is from a free bench specimen.
- "GP keeps shrinking for days / weeks / years." No data. Supported range: minutes to ≤10 h (observed to 24 h).
- "GP is stable after exactly 10 h / 24 h" as a universal rule — one study, four 1990s products.
- "Lottanti showed shrinkage is complete at 10 minutes" — 10 min was the observation window.
- "Hsu 2020 measured shrinkage" / any percentage attributed to Hsu.
- "Tsukada, Dent Mater 2004" (it is J Oral Rehabil) or any percentage attributed to Tsukada.
- Any volume percentage "from Schilder 1985" other than as "Goodman thesis abstract" (3–5 % expansion at 80 °C;
  0.03–1.1 % net loss after 24 h at 37 °C).
- "α-phase / low-temperature / carrier-based GP shrinks less (or more)."
- "It takes N seconds to cool to 37 °C."
- "Shrinkage causes leakage / failure" or "warm techniques fail more/less because of shrinkage."
- "Sealer compensates for GP shrinkage" (sealers shrink or expand themselves); "bioceramic sealer expands to fill
  the gap" (not verified here).
- "Backfill in small increments reduces leakage" (not shown; Johnson & Bond 1999).
- "ISO 6876 allows 1 % shrinkage" (not verified here).
- "GP sets" — say cools / stiffens / crystallizes.
