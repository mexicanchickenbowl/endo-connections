**The one-paragraph answer (what the literature actually supports)**

1. Warm gutta-percha (GP) has no setting reaction — it cools, stiffens steeply as it approaches body temperature
   (Hsu 2020: stiffness and viscosity rose "extremely rapidly" from 40 to 37 °C at 1 °C/min) and **crystallizes**.
   A fast, sizeable shrinkage happens while it cools to 37 °C, within minutes (Lottanti 2014: 1.0–2.3 % *linear*
   in a 10-minute window, "shrinks quickly and extensively"; Capurro 1993: change significant by 5 min, no further
   significant change 5→30 min). **How the total splits between the cooling phase and the later tail is not
   reported anywhere we could read — do not say "most".**
2. It does **not** stop when it reaches body temperature. In the only PubMed *abstract* found that states a
   duration (Lee 1997, volume dilatometer, four "thermosensitive" GP products cooled 80→37 °C in a water bath and
   held at 37 °C for 24 h) shrinkage **continued for 45 min to 10 h** before the volume stabilized. The abstract
   does not say where that clock starts (start of cooling or arrival at 37 °C); heating was "gradual", cooling was
   done by the water bath with readings every 2 °C (rate not given) — not a clinical thermal history. Goodman's dilatometry (BU thesis behind Schilder 1985) independently
   reports contraction that "continued over a 24 hour holding period" at 37 °C.
3. **No study was found** that follows the volume of *heat-cycled* GP beyond 24 h (PubMed searches; older papers
   with no abstract, e.g. Wong 1981, could not be read — so say "no data found", not "never measured"). The
   long-term in vitro data that do exist point the other way: GP cone sections in contact with eugenol *expanded*
   at 24 h, 7 and 30 days (Michaud 2008); sealer-free GP leaked less at 6 months (Wu 2000); voids on micro-CT
   decreased by 54 months (Liu 2021).
4. Everything above is bench data on free bulk material. No clinical study links this shrinkage to outcome, and
   systematic reviews find no consistent, durable outcome difference between obturation techniques (evidence of
   low to very low certainty).

1. **The key paper was missing from the notes.** Lee, Chang, Cobb, Robinson, Hellmuth, J Endod 1997;23:579-82 is the

   2014;40(5):721-4. It measured **linear** (not volume) shrinkage during a controlled 75→37 °C drop and reports the
   total "after 10 minutes". It is cooling-phase data; it neither shows nor excludes later shrinkage.
3. **"Tsukada et al. Dent Mater 2004" is the wrong journal.** It is J Oral Rehabil 2004;31(11):1139-44 (Tsukada G,

## (d) Controversies and caveats (what must not be overstated)

1. **Magnitude depends on method, by nearly 10×.** Linear 1.0–2.3 % (Lottanti, 75→37 °C, 10 min); volume ~2 %
   (Dong); volume 2.2–2.6 % for isothermal crystallization alone (Grassi); −2.2 to −3.5 % for 80→24 °C (Lee);
   6.5–7.3 % for 90→35 °C (Meyer, type of % not stated); net loss vs the as-received cone only 0.03–1.1 % after a
   +3–5 % expansion (Goodman). These are not the same quantity. Never show one "true" percentage, never convert
   linear to volume, and always attach temperature range + method to any number.
2. **Lee 1997's magnitudes do not add up from the abstract** (+11.6–12.3 % expansion, −2.2 to −3.5 % shrinkage,
   net +5.5–7.2 %), and its expansion is 2–4× Goodman's (3–5 % at 80 °C). The two dilatometer studies even
   disagree on the *sign* of the net result: Lee's final change is positive (GP ends larger than as received),
   Goodman's is a net loss of 0.03–1.1 %. Use Lee for the *time* statement only.
3. **One study, 1997 products.** "45 min–10 h" is the spread across four products in a single dilatometer study.
   Modern backfill pellets differ (soft type crystallizes during cooling; regular type shows no exotherm at
   5–20 °C/min, Hsu 2020). The range may not transfer to today's materials. Say "one in vitro study".
   *Fact-check additions:* (a) the abstract does not say whether the 45 min–10 h clock starts when cooling starts
   or when 37 °C is reached; (b) the dilatometer was heated "gradually" and cooled by its water bath, read every 2 °C, rate not given (Goodman's
   similar set-up ran at about 1 °C per 2 min and is described in the thesis as slow compared with clinical
   practice), so the GP spent far longer hot than backfill GP does, and crystallization kinetics depend on thermal
   history; (c) the abstract does not separate thermal equilibration of the instrument from change in the material.
   The honest on-screen form is "held at 37 °C: still shrinking for 45 min–10 h (in vitro)", with the time ruler
   marked as bench data.
4. **Free specimen ≠ canal.** All shrinkage data are on unconfined bulk GP (dilatometer, linometer-type device,
   blocks, glass tubes). In a tooth the GP is compacted under pressure, surrounded by sealer and dentin, and only
   part of it is heated above the transition: apical GP stays ≤45 °C (Goodman 1981; Venturi 2002) and so largely
   avoids the phase change (Schilder 1985). The reel's gap is an illustration, not a measurement: label
   "gap exaggerated / schematic" inside the safe area whenever it is visible.
5. **How the shrinkage is split between "cooling" and "after 37 °C" is unknown from the sources read.** Lottanti
   calls the early shrinkage "quick and extensive"; Lee shows a tail lasting hours. Draw a steep early drop and a
   slow tail, label the curve shape schematic, and do not put a y-axis scale on the hours segment.
6. **Clinical relevance is unproven.** No clinical study relates GP shrinkage to outcome. Systematic reviews find no
   consistent, durable outcome difference between obturation techniques: Peng 2007 (warm vs cold lateral, 10
   studies, long-term outcome RR 0.78, 0.58–1.05; more overextension with warm GP); Pirani & Camilleri 2023 (10
   studies, technique and material "did not affect the outcome of AP", though vertical compaction gave faster
   lesion resolution; high risk of bias); Mushtaq 2026 (84 studies; no significant differences among techniques
   at 6 and 12 months or beyond 3 years, transient advantages of cold lateral and carrier-based over single cone
   at 24 months; certainty low to very low). Write "no consistent difference", not "no difference".
   Long-term in vitro data do not show a growing gap: sealer-free GP leaked *less* at 6 months (Wu 2000), voids
   *decreased* by 54 months (Liu 2021, whole filling with sealer), GP expanded in contact with eugenol (Michaud
   2008), and warm vertical fillings have, if anything, fewer voids than cold lateral on micro-CT (Dashti 2025).
7. **Sealer change is a separate curve on a separate clock** (hours to weeks; direction depends on chemistry:
   ZOE shrinks, epoxy expands slightly, silicone expands slightly; Ørstavik 2001, Kazemi 1993, Hammad 2008). Heat
   also alters some sealers (Camilleri, J Endod 2015;41:72-8, PMID 25115660). If a sealer trace is drawn, it must be
   a different pen and labelled "different process".
8. **Long-term ageing** of GP in vivo is oxidative degradation of the polymer over 5–15+ years (Maniglia-Ferreira
   2007). That is chemistry, not a measured volume change. Do not draw it as shrinkage.
9. **α vs β:** the claim that α-phase GP shrinks less is widely repeated (uncited in Tait 2025). The one direct
   measurement found (Meyer 2006) gives a split answer: α 7.2 % vs β 7.3 % within one system (significant but
   trivial), and both more than conventional GP (6.5 %). Unsettled; leave it out in either direction.
10. **The time axis is a composite** *(added at fact-check)*. The 10-min anchor (Lottanti, tabletop linear
    shrinkage during a controlled 75→37 °C drop) and the 45 min–10 h / 24 h anchors (Lee, slow dilatometer cycle,
    clock start undefined) come from different instruments, materials, decades and thermal histories. No single
    experiment recorded the whole curve. The ruler needs "bench data" / "schematic" on it.
11. **A pressure hold is convention, and rebound is possible** *(added at fact-check)*. The only in vitro support
    for compaction is for the whole warm vertical technique (Marlin, thesis abstract, "further studies should be
    undertaken"). Hsu 2020 suggests compacted GP may partly spring back when the force is released. Do not present
    "hold 10 s" as proven to prevent a gap.
12. **Apical GP temperature is technique-dependent** *(added at fact-check)*: 45 °C peak with the original Schilder
    technique (Goodman 1981), about body temperature with System B (Venturi 2002, measured on the outer root
    surface) and 35.5–37.7 °C in a simulated canal (Wang 2025). If the cutaway shows a hot zone, keep it coronal
    and unlabelled or labelled "schematic".
