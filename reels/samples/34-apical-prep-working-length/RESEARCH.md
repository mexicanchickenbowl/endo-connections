# 34 — Working length and apical preparation size: evidence brief

Research stage only (nothing built). Literature checked on PubMed (abstracts via the PubMed tools / Europe PMC) on 2026-11-22.
**Fact-checked on 2026-11-22** by an independent reviewer who re-read every abstract on PubMed and opened three full
texts (see "Fact-check log", section h). Items changed by the fact-check are marked **FC**.
Every DOI below is given so the claim can be traced. "Verified from" says exactly what was read:

- **abstract** = the PubMed abstract contains the statement/number.
- **primary full text** = the number was read in the full text of the paper itself.
- **secondary full text** = the primary paper has no abstract or the abstract lacks the number, and the primary full text
  was not accessible (not in PMC / paywalled); the number was confirmed in the full text of open-access papers that quote it
  (named in the row). Treat as medium confidence and cite the primary with care.
- **full text (authors' review)** = read in the open-access full text of a review written by the same authors as the primary study.
- **NOT VERIFIED** = could not be confirmed; keep off screen.

## (c) Corrections to the brief

1. **Aminoshariae & Kulild 2015 is in Int Endod J, not J Endod.** Healing review: Int Endod J 2015;48(7):639–47
   (PMID 25113106). Companion microbial-reduction review: Int Endod J 2015;48(11):1007–22 (PMID 25387434). A third review
   by the same authors is Iran Endod J 2018;13(4):424–37 (PMID 36883036). The 2015 healing review found only four
   articles and says more research is needed; it does not grade the evidence as strong.
2. **"One RCT" is out of date.** **FC (expanded):** randomized clinical studies on apical size and healing found on
   PubMed: Saini 2012 (J Endod; + 2 worse than + 3 to + 6), Souza 2012 (Eur J Dent; 3 vs 4 files, no difference at 2 yr),
   Mittal 2016 (J Conserv Dent; split-mouth apical clearing, no difference at 12 months), Fatima 2021 (J Endod; + 2 / .04
   worst). Reviews: Sabeti 2024 (J Endod 50:292–8; certainty LOW) and Shao 2026 (J Endod, online ahead of print;
   + 3 better than + 2, RR 0.78). So do not write "the only RCT" or "2 RCTs".
3. **Kuttler's numbers are 0.524 mm (age 18–25) and 0.659 mm (age ≥55)** — "about 0.5–0.65 mm", not "0.5–0.7 mm".
   The paper has no PubMed abstract; numbers confirmed only through open-access papers that quote them (and those papers
   disagree about his sample size — see A1). Kuttler's figure for how often the foramen deviates from the apex could NOT
   be verified — use Martos 2009 instead (foramen at the root tip in 40%; abstract-verified).
4. **Sjögren 1990: the 94/76/68 numbers are not in the abstract** (the abstract gives 96% / 86% / 62% for other
   comparisons). They were confirmed through secondary full texts only. Two cautions: (i) the same pair, 94% and 68%,
   appears in **Sjögren et al. 1997 (Int Endod J 30:297–306, PMID 9477818)** for negative vs positive culture at
   obturation (55 single-rooted teeth, 5 yr; FC: abstract verified) — do not mix the two papers; (ii) per Ng &
   Gulabivala, the "short" group in Sjögren 1990 were roots that **could not be instrumented to full length**, so 68%
   reflects blocked/un-negotiable canals as much as a choice of end point (the 1990 abstract itself names "the
   possibility of instrumenting the root canal to its full length" as a factor).
5. **Chugal 2003 is correct (14% per mm, teeth with apical periodontitis)** — but the same paper found the opposite
   in teeth with normal pulp/periapex (successes averaged 1.23 mm short, failures 0.20 mm). The reel's "closer is
   better" message applies to infected teeth.
6. **Ng 2011 (FC: now read in the primary full text):** each mm of canal left un-instrumented short of the terminus
   reduced the **odds** of success by 12% (OR 0.88, 95% CI 0.79–0.97); the reference point was the electronic apex
   locator zero reading, not the radiographic apex. Patency at the terminus doubled the odds (OR 2.22, 1.37–3.59).
   A long root filling reduced the odds by 62% (OR 0.38, 0.27–0.54). These are odds from a regression that pools primary
   and retreatment roots — the authors themselves note odds are "not equivalent to chance of success".
7. **Schaeffer 2005 is weaker than "0–1 mm short is better":** 0–1 mm vs overfilled was only marginally significant
   (p < 0.10, 28.8% difference); 0–1 mm vs >1 mm short was not significant.
8. **Card 2002 is commonly over-read.** Its "small" preparation was already large (0.465 mm in molars, 0.60 mm in
   canines/premolars, .04 taper), and the further enlargement to 60/80 was **not** significantly better (p = 0.0617).
   It shows that large preparations with NaOCl can render simple canals culture-negative — not that bigger is
   significantly better.
9. **Shuping 2000 and Dalton 1998 are not apical-size studies in their abstracts.** Shuping: 61.9% bacteria-free with
   NiTi + 1.25% NaOCl, 92.5% after calcium hydroxide. Dalton: bacteria fall with progressive filing (saline), NiTi equal
   to stainless steel. Any statement that "NaOCl only helps beyond size X" from Shuping is full-text material that was
   not read — leave it out.
10. **Khademi 2006** measured debris and smear layer under SEM in extracted mandibular molar mesiobuccal canals — an in
    vitro cleanliness result, not bacterial reduction or healing.
11. **Boutsioukis 2010** is a computational model: all canals had a .06 taper, needles were 30-G placed 3 mm short. The
    conclusion is "sizes larger than 25 appeared to improve syringe irrigation", and a side-vented needle did not
    exchange irrigant to working length at any size tested. **FC:** sizes modeled were 25, 35, 45, 55 — it says nothing
    about size 30. The model that included 30/.06 is Boutsioukis & Gutierrez Nova 2021 (J Endod).
12. **Paqué/Peters:** two separate papers — Peters, Schönenberger & Laib, Int Endod J 2001 (≥35% of surface unchanged,
    maxillary molars) and Paqué, Balmer, Attin & Peters, J Endod 2010 (59.6–79.9% untreated in oval distal canals).
13. **Ricucci & Langeland 1998** is a histological case series with no success percentages; cite it for "at or short of
    the constriction", not for a number.

## (d) Controversies and caveats (do not overstate)

- **The "0–2 mm" zone is a radiographic surrogate.** The radiographic apex is not the foramen, and the foramen is not the
  constriction. The constriction averages 0.9 mm from the anatomic apex but ranges from 0.07 to 2.69 mm (Dummer; anterior
  teeth and premolars), the foramen is off the tip in most teeth (Martos), and a constriction is often absent (Wu 2000).
  A file 0.5–1 mm short on the radiograph can already be through the foramen.
- **Outcome-by-length data are observational and confounded.** Short fillings are often short because the canal was
  blocked, ledged or calcified; long fillings usually follow over-instrumentation. **FC (softened):** most retrospective
  studies recorded only the filling level; Chugal 2003 analyzed the instrumentation level (working length) and the
  Eastman cohort (Ng 2011) recorded both instrumentation and filling extent — and found the two strongly correlated.
- **Pulp status changes the answer.** Infected canals: closer to the terminus is better. Vital cases: outcomes were as
  good or better slightly shorter (Wu 2000: 2–3 mm; Chugal 2003: 1.23 mm). A single number for all teeth is a
  simplification.
- **"Long" is not one thing.** Sealer puffs, gutta-percha overextension and over-instrumentation with infected debris are
  pooled in old studies. Ricucci et al. 2016 (J Endod 42:243–9, PMID 26725179; 105 overfilled teeth, retrospective) found
  outcome after sealer extrusion was driven by preoperative apical periodontitis (79% healed with a lesion vs 100%
  without, >4 yr), not by sealer type. **FC:** in Ng 2011 a long root filling carried OR 0.38 in the final model, while
  the authors wrote that "extrusion of sealer did not seem to affect the success" (unadjusted OR 0.71, 95% CI 0.51–0.99,
  in primary treatment; 0.81, 0.60–1.09, in retreatment).
- **Apical size: the evidence points in two directions.** For "larger": in vitro debris/irrigant studies, flow models,
  most bacterial-reduction studies, two RCTs showing binding file + 2 is too small, a low-certainty meta-analysis and a
  2026 review. Against a simple "bigger is better": observational outcome studies show no significant adjusted effect and
  an inverse trend (Gulabivala & Ng 2023; in Ng 2011 the unadjusted figures were 85.7% healed at ≤ #30 vs 76.3% at > #30);
  two small randomized studies found no lasting benefit from more enlargement (Souza 2012; Mittal 2016); two clinical
  studies found no microbial difference between 25 and 40; Card's further enlargement was not significant; no size
  eliminates bacteria (Aminoshariae & Kulild 2015).
- **The RCTs are narrow.** **FC (rewritten):** Saini 2012 and Fatima 2021 are single-center, mandibular first molars,
  12-month follow-up, periapical-radiograph PAI outcomes, about 25–30 teeth per group, and both show only that the
  smallest preparation (binding file + 2 sizes; in Fatima, + 2 with .04 taper) under-performs. In Saini the pairwise
  differences from + 3 to + 6 (71% to 92%) were not significant, although a regression across groups did show a positive
  association between apical size and PAI improvement (β = 0.037, P = .001). Souza 2012 (anterior teeth and premolars,
  2 yr, heavy drop-out) and Mittal 2016 (20 split-mouth pairs) found no difference. None of them tests an absolute ISO size.
- **"First binding file" is a soft ruler.** It under-reads the canal in most cases (Wu 2002), and canals are often oval
  (Wu 2000), so "+3 sizes" does not guarantee circumferential planing. **FC (corrected):** in Mickel 2007 the first
  crown-down file to reach the apex was on average four sizes larger than the first file to bind, even though the
  binding file was gauged after preflaring — the gauge depends on when and how it is taken. Micro-CT shows 35% to about
  80% of wall area untouched regardless (Peters 2001; Paqué 2010).
- **Size is not the only lever for irrigation.** Taper (Boutsioukis 2010; Albrecht 2004), needle type and depth, flow rate
  and activation all change apical irrigant exchange. Larger apical sizes also lower wall shear stress in the models.
- **Costs of enlarging are not quantified here.** Transportation, dentin removal and debris packing are the usual
  arguments for conservative sizes; Gulabivala & Ng offer debris blockage and iatrogenic error as explanations for the
  inverse trend. No fracture or survival data were found that tie apical size to tooth loss.
- **Patency** was associated with better healing in one prospective cohort (Ng 2011: OR 2.22); it is observational, and
  instrumenting through the foramen increases early postoperative pain (Fatima 2025 meta-analysis). **FC:** the claim that
  patency also improved "early tooth survival" comes from Part 2 of the Eastman study, which was not opened — removed.
- **Guidelines:** the ESE 2006 quality guidelines give the 0.5–2 mm / constriction wording. The ESE S3 guideline (Duncan
  et al., Int Endod J 2023;56 Suppl 3:238–295, PMID 37772327) could not be opened; its commissioned instrumentation
  review (Bürklein & Arias, Int Endod J 2023;56 Suppl 3:395–421, PMID 35670625) compares instrument types, not apical
  sizes (FC: abstract verified). Do not cite the S3 guideline for a working-length or apical-size recommendation.

## (e) Do not claim

- That 94 / 76 / 68% applies to all teeth — it is roots with necrotic pulp and a periapical lesion in one Swedish cohort (1990).
- That 94% vs 68% is "negative vs positive culture" on this reel (that is Sjögren 1997).
- "Each mm short lowers success by 14 percentage points" — it is a change in odds / chance of failure from regression models.
- That the constriction is always 0.5–1 mm from the apex, always exists, or that the CDJ can be found clinically.
- **FC:** That Dummer's ≈0.9 mm is a distance on the radiograph, or that it was measured in molars.
- That an apex locator or radiograph finds the constriction exactly (covered by another reel; no accuracy number here).
- That a larger apical size "sterilizes", "eliminates bacteria" or is "proven" to heal more in general.
- That #30 (or #35, #40) is a proven clinical threshold for healing. #30 is an in vitro irrigant/debris finding; the RCTs
  are relative to the first binding file; the ≥30 vs <30 meta-analysis has two studies and low certainty.
- **FC:** "Boutsioukis 2010" as the source of a #30 threshold (size 30 was not in that model).
- **FC:** "2 RCTs", "the only RCT" or "RCTs agree" — there are at least four small randomized studies and they do not all agree.
- **FC:** That preflaring shifts the binding file by four sizes (Mickel 2007 did not test that).
- That Card 2002 showed a significant benefit of larger sizes, or that Shuping 2000 defined a size threshold.
- That a side-vented needle delivers irrigant to working length once the canal is big enough (the model says it did not).
- That "+3 sizes" cleans the whole canal wall.
- That overfilling "causes" failure, or that sealer puffs are harmful — association only, confounded by over-instrumentation.
- That patency filing improves healing (association in one cohort).
- Any Kuttler percentage for foramen deviation, Kuttler's sample size, Burch & Hulen figures, a Kerekes & Tronstad size
  table, an AAE glossary quotation, or an ESE S3 recommendation — none were verified for this brief.
- **FC:** The pooled "81% flush vs 66% long" as numbers from Ng 2008 Part 2 on screen (read only in the authors' later
  review, which cites two meta-analyses for them).
- **FC (changed):** The Ng 2011 odds ratio for long root fillings is now verified (OR 0.38, 95% CI 0.27–0.54) and may be
  used in the caption — but as odds ("62% lower odds"), never as "62% fewer teeth healed".
