## (c) Corrections to the brief

1. **Aminoshariae & Kulild 2015 is in Int Endod J, not J Endod.** Healing review: Int Endod J 2015;48(7):639–47
   (PMID 25113106). Companion microbial-reduction review: Int Endod J 2015;48(11):1007–22 (PMID 25387434). A third review
   by the same authors is Iran Endod J 2018;13(4):424–37 (PMID 36883036). The 2015 healing review found only four
   articles and says more research is needed; it does not grade the evidence as strong.
2. **"One RCT" is out of date.** There are at least two RCTs on healing (Saini 2012; Fatima 2021), a split-mouth trial of
   apical clearing (Mittal et al., J Conserv Dent 2016;19(5):396–401, PMID 27656054), and a 2024 meta-analysis (Sabeti
   et al., J Endod 2024;50(3):292–8) — certainty still LOW.
3. **Kuttler's numbers are 0.524 mm (age 18–25) and 0.659 mm (age ≥55)** — "about 0.5–0.65 mm", not "0.5–0.7 mm".
   The paper has no PubMed abstract; numbers confirmed only through three open-access papers that quote them.
   Kuttler's figure for how often the foramen deviates from the apex could NOT be verified — use Martos 2009 instead
   (foramen at the root tip in 40%; abstract-verified).
4. **Sjögren 1990: the 94/76/68 numbers are not in the abstract** (the abstract gives 96% / 86% / 62% for other
   comparisons). They were confirmed through secondary full texts only. Two cautions: (i) the same pair, 94% and 68%,
   appears in **Sjögren et al. 1997 (Int Endod J)** for negative vs positive culture at obturation — do not mix the two
   papers; (ii) per Ng & Gulabivala, the "short" group in Sjögren 1990 were roots that **could not be instrumented to
   full length**, so 68% reflects blocked/un-negotiable canals as much as a choice of end point.
5. **Chugal 2003 is correct (14% per mm, teeth with apical periodontitis)** — but the same paper found the opposite
   in teeth with normal pulp/periapex (successes averaged 1.23 mm short, failures 0.20 mm). The reel's "closer is
   better" message applies to infected teeth.
6. **Ng 2011:** the per-mm figure is a **12% reduction in the odds of success** per un-instrumented mm (authors' own
   reviews; not in the abstract), and the reference point was the electronic apex locator zero reading, not the
   radiographic apex. Patency doubled the odds of healing. Root-filling extrusion reduced healing (P ≤ 0.001 in the
   abstract); a specific odds ratio for long fillings was NOT verified — do not print one.
7. **Schaeffer 2005 is weaker than "0–1 mm short is better":** 0–1 mm vs overfilled was only marginally significant
   (p < 0.10, 28.8% difference); 0–1 mm vs >1 mm short was not significant.
8. **Card 2002 is commonly over-read.** Its "small" preparation was already large (0.465 mm in molars, 0.60 mm in
   canines/premolars, .04 taper), and the further enlargement to 60/80 was **not** significantly better (p = 0.0617).
   It shows that large preparations with NaOCl can render simple canals culture-negative — not that bigger is
   significantly better.
9. **Shuping 2000 and Dalton 1998 are not apical-size studies in their abstracts.** Shuping: 61.9% bacteria-free with
   NiTi + 1.25% NaOCl, 92.5% after calcium hydroxide. Dalton: bacteria fall with progressive filing (saline), NiTi equal
   to stainless steel. Any statement that "NaOCl only helps beyond size X" from Shuping is full-text material that was
   not read — leave it out.
10. **Khademi 2006** measured debris and smear layer under SEM in extracted mandibular molar mesiobuccal canals — an in
    vitro cleanliness result, not bacterial reduction or healing.
11. **Boutsioukis 2010** is a computational model: all canals had a .06 taper, needles were 30-G placed 3 mm short. The
    conclusion is "sizes larger than 25 appeared to improve syringe irrigation", and a side-vented needle did not
    exchange irrigant to working length at any size tested.
12. **Paqué/Peters:** two separate papers — Peters, Schönenberger & Laib, Int Endod J 2001 (≥35% of surface unchanged,
    maxillary molars) and Paqué, Balmer, Attin & Peters, J Endod 2010 (59.6–79.9% untreated in oval distal canals).
13. **Ricucci & Langeland 1998** is a histological case series with no success percentages; cite it for "at or short of
    the constriction", not for a number.

## (d) Controversies and caveats (do not overstate)

- **The "0–2 mm" zone is a radiographic surrogate.** The radiographic apex is not the foramen, and the foramen is not the
  constriction. The constriction averages 0.9 mm from the apex but ranges from 0.07 to 2.69 mm (Dummer), the foramen is
  off the tip in most teeth (Martos), and a constriction is often absent (Wu 2000). A file 0.5–1 mm short on the
  radiograph can already be through the foramen.
- **Outcome-by-length data are observational and confounded.** Short fillings are often short because the canal was
  blocked, ledged or calcified; long fillings usually follow over-instrumentation. Only the Eastman cohort (Ng 2011)
  separated instrumentation length from filling length.
- **Pulp status changes the answer.** Infected canals: closer to the terminus is better. Vital cases: outcomes were as
  good or better slightly shorter (Wu 2000: 2–3 mm; Chugal 2003: 1.23 mm). A single number for all teeth is a
  simplification.
- **"Long" is not one thing.** Sealer puffs, gutta-percha overextension and over-instrumentation with infected debris are
  pooled in old studies. Ricucci et al. 2016 (J Endod 42:243–9, PMID 26725179) found outcome after sealer extrusion was
  driven by preoperative apical periodontitis (79% healed with a lesion vs 100% without, >4 yr), not by sealer type.
- **Apical size: the evidence points in two directions.** For "larger": in vitro debris/irrigant studies, flow models,
  most bacterial-reduction studies, two RCTs and a low-certainty meta-analysis. Against a simple "bigger is better":
  observational outcome studies show no significant effect and an inverse trend (Gulabivala & Ng 2023); two clinical
  studies found no microbial difference between 25 and 40; Card's further enlargement was not significant; no size
  eliminates bacteria (Aminoshariae & Kulild 2015).
- **The RCTs are narrow.** Both are single-center, mandibular first molars, 12-month follow-up, periapical-radiograph PAI
  outcomes, and both show only that the smallest preparation (binding file + 2 sizes) under-performs. They do not
  establish a dose–response or an absolute ISO size. In Saini the trend from 71% to 92% was not statistically significant.
- **"First binding file" is a soft ruler.** It under-reads the canal in most cases (Wu 2002), changes by about four sizes
  with preflaring (Mickel 2007), and canals are often oval (Wu 2000), so "+3 sizes" does not guarantee circumferential
  planing. Micro-CT shows 35% to about 80% of wall area untouched regardless (Peters 2001; Paqué 2010).
- **Size is not the only lever for irrigation.** Taper (Boutsioukis 2010; Albrecht 2004), needle type and depth, flow rate
  and activation all change apical irrigant exchange. Larger apical sizes also lower wall shear stress in the models.
- **Costs of enlarging are not quantified here.** Transportation, dentin removal and debris packing are the usual
  arguments for conservative sizes; Gulabivala & Ng offer debris blockage and iatrogenic error as explanations for the
  inverse trend. No fracture or survival data were found that tie apical size to tooth loss.
- **Patency** was associated with better healing and early tooth survival in one prospective cohort (Ng 2011); it is
  observational, and instrumenting through the foramen increases early postoperative pain (Fatima 2025 meta-analysis).
- **Guidelines:** the ESE 2006 quality guidelines give the 0.5–2 mm / constriction wording. The ESE S3 guideline (Duncan
  et al., Int Endod J 2023;56 Suppl 3:238–295) could not be opened; its commissioned instrumentation review (Bürklein &
  Arias, Int Endod J 2023;56 Suppl 3:395–421) compares instrument types, not apical sizes. Do not cite the S3 guideline
  for a working-length or apical-size recommendation.

## (e) Do not claim

- That 94 / 76 / 68% applies to all teeth — it is necrotic teeth with a periapical lesion in one Swedish cohort (1990).
- That 94% vs 68% is "negative vs positive culture" on this reel (that is Sjögren 1997).
- "Each mm short lowers success by 14 percentage points" — it is a change in odds / chance of failure from regression models.
- That the constriction is always 0.5–1 mm from the apex, always exists, or that the CDJ can be found clinically.
- That an apex locator or radiograph finds the constriction exactly (covered by another reel; no accuracy number here).
- That a larger apical size "sterilizes", "eliminates bacteria" or is "proven" to heal more in general.
- That #30 (or #35, #40) is a proven clinical threshold for healing. #30 is an in vitro irrigant/debris finding; the RCTs
  are relative to the first binding file; the ≥30 vs <30 meta-analysis has two studies and low certainty.
- That Card 2002 showed a significant benefit of larger sizes, or that Shuping 2000 defined a size threshold.
- That a side-vented needle delivers irrigant to working length once the canal is big enough (the model says it did not).
- That "+3 sizes" cleans the whole canal wall.
- That overfilling "causes" failure, or that sealer puffs are harmful — association only, confounded by over-instrumentation.
- That patency filing improves healing (association in one cohort).
- Any Kuttler percentage for foramen deviation, Burch & Hulen figures, a Kerekes & Tronstad size table, an AAE glossary
  quotation, or an ESE S3 recommendation — none were verified for this brief.
- An odds ratio for long root fillings from Ng 2011 (not verified).
