## (c) Corrections to the brief

1. "Activity strongest fresh and falls after setting" depends on the model. The brief cites Wang, Shen, Haapasalo
   2014 for it, and that paper shows the OPPOSITE in dentin: its title is "Dentin extends the antibacterial effect
   of endodontic sealers" and dead-cell proportion rose from 1 to 7 to 30 days (for AH Plus as well as BC Sealer).
   The time-dial plate must not show one simple shrinking halo for every model: contact-test activity of SET
   material is weaker and declines (Farrugia 2017, full text); tubule killing by sealers accumulates slowly then
   plateaus (~30 d, Du 2015); with young biofilms the dead fraction falls again by 60 d (Liu 2022); with set MTA
   in tubules the dead fraction fell from day 1 to day 7 (Farrugia 2017).
2. Zhang 2009 tested iRoot SP, not a product named EndoSequence BC Sealer. Its 7-day finding is contradictory
   within the abstract — do not state "active at 7 days" or "inactive at 7 days" from it.
3. "Kapralos et al. 2018 on sealers fresh vs aged": the paper's key message is planktonic vs biofilm, and that
   TotalFill BC Sealer was LESS active than AH Plus against biofilms in the first 24 h while keeping planktonic
   activity to 7 days after setting. AH Plus lost its activity after 24 h.
4. "Koutroulis et al. 2019" is Sci Rep 2019;9:19019 — on pulp-protection materials, eluates against S. mutans and
   L. casei (confirmed in full text), showing growth inhibition correlates weakly with calcium ion release
   (r 0.3-0.49). It is not an E. faecalis/sealer study and it measured inhibition (optical density), not killing.
5. AlShwaimi 2016 covers all sealer categories by direct contact test against E. faecalis; it is not a
   bioceramic-specific review, and its "no activity once set for 2-7 days" conclusion does not match the
   calcium silicate sealer data (Zhang 2009; Kapralos 2018). Bioceramic-specific reviews: Simundic Munitic 2019
   PLoS One (37 studies, no ranking possible) and Seron 2024 Odontology (meta-analysis, similar to AH Plus).
6. "Zordan-Bronzel/Silva-era reviews": the verifiable one is Silva Almeida LH, Moraes RR, Morgental RD, Pappen FG.
   J Endod 2017;43(4):527-35 (PMID 28216270, [doi](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.joen.2016.11.019)), a systematic
   review of in vitro properties of premixed calcium silicate sealers (27 studies). Zordan-Bronzel's work is
   physicochemical, not an antibacterial review — not used. "Sfeir" appears only as fourth author of Hage W et al.
   J Contemp Dent Pract 2023;24(9):707-14 (PMID 38152946, [doi](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10024-3556);
   five calcium silicate sealers vs a multispecies biofilm, no significant differences between sealers) —
   low-weight source, not recommended on screen. Jerez-Olate is row 26.
7. Haapasalo et al. 2000 (dentin buffering) tested saturated Ca(OH)2 solution and irrigants with dentin powder,
   not calcium silicate cements. For calcium silicate materials the checked evidence does NOT show dentin
   switching the effect off: Wang 2014 ("Dentin extends..."), Koutroulis 2023 J Funct Biomater (adjacent dentin
   became antibacterial), and Farrugia 2017 full text ("a similar behavior... in the absence (direct contact
   test) or presence of dentin (intratubular infection test)"). So "dentin buffers bioceramics" is an
   extrapolation. If dentin buffering is drawn at all, label it "Ca(OH)2 solution + dentin powder, Haapasalo
   2000" and never put it under a Wang 2014 source line.
8. Torabinejad 1995 (antibacterial) is an agar diffusion study and found NO effect on any strict anaerobe. (That
   primary endodontic infections are anaerobe-dominated is textbook background, not sourced here.)
9. "Silicon/other ions" as an antibacterial mechanism: no source found that demonstrates it. NOT VERIFIED — leave

   the sealer effect.
10. "pH values and how long they stay high": only the MTA 10.2 -> 12.5 at 3 h figure is abstract-verified.
    Sealer-specific pH values (Candeiro GT et al. J Endod 2012;38(6):842-5, PMID 22595123,

    not given numerically in the abstract — NOT VERIFIED; do not print a sealer pH number.
11. (Added at fact-check.) "Whether any CLINICAL outcome benefit has been shown": the first draft answered "none"
    from Sabeti 2024 alone. Larger, newer syntheses exist (row 23b). The honest summary is "comparable healing";
    one 2026 meta-analysis reports fewer failures by loose criteria only (OR 0.70, 95% CI 0.50-0.99) and no
    difference by strict criteria. No trial attributes any difference to antibacterial activity.

## (d) Controversies and caveats

- Model dependence. Planktonic direct-contact tests with FRESH sealer say "fast and complete" (Zhang 2009);
  the same kind of test with SET MTA says "partial, under 2 log, declining" (Farrugia 2017); tubule/CLSM models
  with sealers say "slow, partial, continues for weeks" (Wang 2014; Du 2015) but with young biofilms the effect
  is front-loaded (Liu 2022); biofilm tests say "weaker than AH Plus" (Kapralos 2018). Different papers support
  opposite one-liners; the honest summary names the model and the material.
- Bioceramic vs epoxy resin. Results conflict: BC Sealer better (Bukhari 2019; fresh iRoot SP 2 min vs AH Plus
  5 min, Zhang 2009), no difference (Wang 2014; Seron 2024 meta-analysis), or worse (Kapralos 2018 on biofilm;
  Candeiro GTM et al. Int Endod J 2016;49(9):858-64, PMID 26281002, [doi](https://doi.org/10.1111/iej.12523):
  BC Sealer gave smaller inhibition zones, 6.00 vs 10.31 mm, and less activity than AH Plus up to 1 h of direct
  contact, similar at later times — the authors summarize it as "similar"). Do not claim superiority.
- CLSM live/dead staining reports membrane-compromised cells as "dead"; it is not a culture count and cannot prove
  sterilization. Untreated controls already showed 14-22% "dead" cells in Farrugia 2017.
- Agar diffusion zones depend on solubility and diffusion through agar and do not distinguish killing from
  inhibition (stated in Farrugia 2017 full text); set ProRoot MTA gave NO zone at all there. The halo plate must
  be labelled schematic.
- Bactericidal vs bacteriostatic is a laboratory, condition-dependent label (Pankey & Sabath 2004). Almost no
  bioceramic study measures it formally. "Kills on contact when fresh, in vitro" is supportable (iRoot SP killed
  all planktonic cells); for set material the only verified count data (ProRoot MTA, 1-h contact) show a
  0.8-1.7 log reduction — partial killing, short of the 3-log convention. "Bacteriostatic when set" is still an
  inference, not a measured finding — word it as "weaker, partial".
- Clinical conditions (blood, serum, fluid turnover) reduce alkalinity and antibacterial activity (Farrugia 2017;
  Koutroulis 2023 Eur J Oral Sci). Dentin's role is unsettled for these materials (see (c)7).
- The sustained alkalinity comes from continued leaching, i.e. solubility — a trade-off flagged by Donnermeyer D,
  Burklein S, Dammaschke T, Schafer E. Odontology 2019;107(4):421-36 (PMID 30554288,
  [doi](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10266-018-0400-3)) and visible in Fridland 2005.
- Clinical studies compare sealer + technique bundles (single cone vs warm vertical), many are small and short
  (most <= 2 yr; Zamparini 2024), and none was designed to detect an antibacterial benefit. Comparable outcomes
  are not proof of zero antibacterial contribution, and the loose-criteria advantage in Donnermeyer 2026 cannot
  be attributed to antibacterial action.
- E. faecalis dominates the laboratory evidence; multispecies data are few (Liu 2022; Jerez-Olate 2022; Hage
  2023), and the classic MTA paper found no effect on strict anaerobes.

## (e) Do not claim

- "Bioceramics sterilize the canal / kill bacteria in tubules completely."
- "Bioceramic sealers are more antibacterial than AH Plus" (or the reverse).
- "The antibacterial effect is permanent / lasts for the life of the filling."
- "Kills E. faecalis biofilm in 2 minutes" (the 2-min result is planktonic cells on fresh iRoot SP).
- "Fresh sealers / bioceramics kill in 2 min" without naming iRoot SP; "EndoSequence BC Sealer killed in 2 min".
- "The high pH is what killed them in 2 min" (Zhang 2009: pH alone did not explain the effect).
- "The effect is gone 2-7 days after setting" as a statement about bioceramic sealers (AlShwaimi 2016 is an
  all-sealer conclusion; Zhang 2009 and Kapralos 2018 disagree for calcium silicate sealers).
- "Dentin buffers / neutralizes bioceramics" (Haapasalo 2000 was Ca(OH)2 solution + dentin powder; sealer and
  cement data do not show it).
- "Kills only on contact, nothing at a distance" or any depth gradient in tubules as a measured finding — no
  abstract checked reports depth data; if drawn, label it schematic.
- "Never 100%" as a universal statement — say "partial" (best reported figure 83%, Du 2015).
- Any pH number for a specific sealer, any zone-of-inhibition size in mm for a calcium silicate material, any
  percent-killed for BC Sealer.
- "83% killed by bioceramic sealer" — the 83% is 5% NaOCl + MTA Fillapex at 60 days.
- "Active / inactive at 7 days" from Zhang 2009.
- "Set bioceramics are bacteriostatic" as a measured fact; "bactericidal = 3-log" cited to Pankey & Sabath.
- "Silicon ions are antibacterial."
- "Improves healing / success rates", "no clinical benefit proven" as an absolute, or any clinical antibacterial
  benefit. Say "comparable outcomes".
- Anything about anaerobes being killed by MTA (the classic paper found no effect).
- "Blood removes the inhibition zone" (set ProRoot MTA gave no zone in water either, Farrugia 2017).
- Biocompatibility, osteoconduction or osteoinduction claims — not researched in this brief.
- That the antibacterial effect substitutes for chemomechanical disinfection.
