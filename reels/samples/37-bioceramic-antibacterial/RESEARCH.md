## (c) Corrections to the brief

1. "Activity strongest fresh and falls after setting" is true for direct-contact / planktonic tests (AlShwaimi 2016; Zhang 2009), but the brief cites Wang, Shen, Haapasalo 2014 for it, and that paper shows the OPPOSITE in dentin: its title is "Dentin extends the antibacterial effect of endodontic sealers" and dead-cell proportion rose from 1 to 7 to 30 days. The time-dial plate must not show one simple shrinking halo for every model: contact-test activity fades; tubule killing accumulates slowly then plateaus (~30 d, Du 2015).
2. Zhang 2009 tested iRoot SP, not a product named EndoSequence BC Sealer (same formulation family, marketed under different names). Its 7-day finding is contradictory within the abstract — do not state "active at 7 days" or "inactive at 7 days" from it.
3. "Kapralos et al. 2018 on sealers fresh vs aged": the paper's key message is planktonic vs biofilm, and that TotalFill BC Sealer was LESS active than AH Plus against biofilms in the first 24 h while keeping planktonic activity to 7 days. AH Plus lost its activity after 24 h.
4. "Koutroulis et al. 2019" is Sci Rep 2019;9:19019 — on pulp-protection materials, eluates against S. mutans and L. casei, showing antibacterial effect correlates with calcium ion release (r 0.3-0.49). It is not an E. faecalis/sealer study.
5. AlShwaimi 2016 covers all sealer categories by direct contact test against E. faecalis; it is not a bioceramic-specific review. Bioceramic-specific reviews: Simundic Munitic 2019 PLoS One (37 studies, no ranking possible) and Seron 2024 Odontology (meta-analysis, similar to AH Plus).
6. "Zordan-Bronzel/Silva-era reviews": the verifiable one is Silva Almeida LH et al. J Endod 2017;43(4):527-35 (PMID 28216270), a systematic review of in vitro properties of premixed calcium silicate sealers. Zordan-Bronzel's work is physicochemical, not an antibacterial review — not used. "Sfeir" appears only as a co-author of Hage W et al. J Contemp Dent Pract 2023;24(9):707-14 (PMID 38152946; five calcium silicate sealers vs multispecies biofilm, no significant differences between sealers) — low-weight source, not recommended on screen.
7. Haapasalo et al. 2000 (dentin buffering) tested saturated Ca(OH)2 solution and irrigants with dentin powder, not calcium silicate cements. Present it as the mechanism for hydroxyl buffering, not as a bioceramic result.
8. Torabinejad 1995 (antibacterial) is an agar diffusion study and found NO effect on any strict anaerobe — a limit worth showing, since primary endodontic infections are anaerobe-dominated.
9. "Silicon/other ions" as an antibacterial mechanism: no source found that demonstrates it. NOT VERIFIED — leave off screen. Only pH/Ca(OH)2 and calcium release are supported.
10. "pH values and how long they stay high": only the MTA 10.2 -> 12.5 at 3 h figure is abstract-verified. Sealer-specific pH values (Candeiro 2012 J Endod, PMID 22595123, measured 3-240 h and found BC Sealer pH higher than AH Plus) are not given numerically in the abstract — NOT VERIFIED; do not print a sealer pH number.

## (d) Controversies and caveats

- Model dependence. Planktonic direct-contact tests say "fast, strong, short-lived"; tubule/CLSM models say "slow, partial, continues for weeks"; biofilm tests say "weaker than against planktonic". Different papers support opposite one-liners; the honest summary names the model.
- Bioceramic vs epoxy resin. Results conflict: BC Sealer better (Bukhari 2019), equal (Wang 2014; Candeiro 2016 Int Endod J, PMID 26281002; Seron 2024 meta-analysis), or worse against biofilm (Kapralos 2018). Do not claim superiority.
- CLSM live/dead staining reports membrane-compromised cells as "dead"; it is not a culture count and cannot prove sterilization.
- Agar diffusion zones depend on solubility and diffusion through agar and do not distinguish killing from inhibition; the halo plate must be labelled schematic.
- Bactericidal vs bacteriostatic is a laboratory, condition-dependent label (Pankey & Sabath 2004). Almost no bioceramic study measures it formally (no MBC/time-kill to a 3-log endpoint in a clinically relevant model). "Bactericidal on contact when fresh, in vitro" is supportable; "bacteriostatic when set" is an inference, not a measured finding — word it as "limited / mainly local, may only hold growth".
- Clinical conditions (dentin, blood, serum, fluid turnover) reduce alkalinity and killing (Haapasalo 2000; Farrugia 2017; Koutroulis 2023).
- The sustained alkalinity that gives the antibacterial effect comes from continued leaching, i.e. solubility — a trade-off flagged by Donnermeyer et al. Odontology 2019;107(4):421-36 (PMID 30554288).
- Clinical trials compare sealer + technique bundles (single cone vs warm vertical), are small, mostly 1-5 years, and were not designed to detect an antibacterial benefit. Absence of a shown benefit is not proof of no benefit.
- E. faecalis dominates the laboratory evidence; multispecies data are few (Liu 2022; Jerez-Olate 2022; Hage 2023).

## (e) Do not claim

- "Bioceramics sterilize the canal / kill bacteria in tubules completely."
- "Bioceramic sealers are more antibacterial than AH Plus" (or the reverse).
- "The antibacterial effect is permanent / lasts for the life of the filling."
- "Kills E. faecalis biofilm in 2 minutes" (the 2-min result is planktonic cells on fresh iRoot SP).
- Any pH number for a specific sealer, any zone-of-inhibition size in mm, any percent-killed for BC Sealer.
- "83% killed by bioceramic sealer" — the 83% is NaOCl + MTA Fillapex at 60 days.
- "Active / inactive at 7 days" from Zhang 2009.
- "Silicon ions are antibacterial."
- "Improves healing / success rates" or any clinical antibacterial benefit.
- Anything about anaerobes being killed by MTA (the classic paper found no effect).
- Biocompatibility, osteoconduction or osteoinduction claims — not researched in this brief.
- That the antibacterial effect substitutes for chemomechanical disinfection.
