# Short-Form Vertical Video Best Practices for Educational / Sci-Comm Explainers (Reels, TikTok, Shorts), 2024–2026

Context: notes to inform a 90-second, 9:16, narrated, animated explainer on placebo in endodontics, aimed at endodontists and residents, with Instagram Reels as the main platform.

Evidence labels used throughout:
- **[OFFICIAL]**: platform-published guidance (Instagram/Meta, TikTok, YouTube) or direct statements by Adam Mosseri, as reported.
- **[PEER-REVIEWED]**: journal articles.
- **[PREPRINT]**: arXiv, not peer-reviewed.
- **[INDUSTRY DATA]**: analytics vendors with stated samples (Socialinsider etc.). Not peer-reviewed.
- **[VENDOR/CREATOR CLAIM]**: blogs and tools with no traceable method. Treat as anecdote.
- **[OLDER]**: published before 2024.

Research date: Nov 25, 2026.

---

## 1. Hooks: what do platform guidance and data say about the first 1–3 seconds (visual vs. spoken hooks, open loops, curiosity gaps), and is there empirical research?

### Takeaway
Platforms do say the opening matters. TikTok's official creative playbook says "the first 3–6 seconds… are crucial" and that "the first six seconds are vital for impact," citing TikTok's own 2020 research. However, I found **no peer-reviewed study that isolates hook design (visual vs. spoken vs. open-loop) as a cause of retention on short-form platforms**. Two academic swipe-behavior datasets show that drop-off clusters at the very start of a video. Lab research on curiosity supports open loops for memory, with an important 2024 caveat. Precise numbers like "X% retained at 3 s" come from vendors and conflict with each other.

### Cited Findings
**Platform-official**
- TikTok's SMB creative playbook (TikTok for Business; undated, ads-oriented) says: "The first 3-6 seconds of your video are crucial. Captivate your potential customers with a powerful, attention-grabbing hook, related to your message and narrative." It also says: "The first 3-6 seconds are critical. TikTok is a fast-paced platform, and users scroll through content quickly." **[OFFICIAL]** — [TikTok SMB Creative Playbook (PDF)](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/SMB_Creative_Playbook_External.pdf)
- The same playbook says that "research shows that regardless of format, the first six seconds are vital for impact." It sources this to "TikTok, 'Creative Guide: Driving Brand Equity', 2020 (TikTok 'Value of View' Analysis)". The underlying research is from 2020 and is ad-focused. **[OFFICIAL][OLDER]** — [TikTok SMB Creative Playbook (PDF)](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/SMB_Creative_Playbook_External.pdf)
- The playbook's hook archetypes include problem/solution ("I have the perfect solution for anyone who has [problem]…"), audience call-outs ("Calling all…"), and numbered lists ("3 reasons why…", "3 signs you NEED…"). It also says: "Remember: Every hook needs a payoff." **[OFFICIAL]** — [TikTok SMB Creative Playbook (PDF)](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/SMB_Creative_Playbook_External.pdf)
- Third-party summaries attribute "introduce the core proposition within the first 3 seconds and land the hook within the first 6 seconds" to TikTok's creative best-practice pages. A "63% of top ads deliver the key message in the first 3 seconds" stat circulates with conflicting attribution, and it concerns **ads**, not organic content. **[SECONDARY]** — [MB Adv summary](https://www.mbadv.agency/tiktok-ads/creative-best-practices); [Kabo (notes the 3-s window is a practical inspection point, not a TikTok-published threshold)](https://kabo.sh/guides/how-to-use-sound-as-a-video-hook)
- I found **no Instagram/Mosseri statement in 2025–2026 that specifies a seconds-based hook rule**. Instagram's official 2025 Reels post covers length, originality, trial reels and editing tools, not hooks. **[OFFICIAL]** — [Instagram Creators blog, "Finding success on reels in 2025" (Jan 21, 2025)](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/the-latest-with-instagram)

**Empirical / academic**
- The Dashlet short-video streaming study found that users are most likely to swipe away either soon after playback begins or at the tail end of a video. Drop-off is front-loaded. **[PREPRINT][OLDER, 2022]** — [Dashlet, arXiv 2204.12954](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.12954)
- A TikTok data-donation study (347 users, 9.2M video views) found that **55% of videos are not watched to the end**. It does not isolate the opening seconds. **[PREPRINT][OLDER, 2023]** — [Analyzing User Engagement with TikTok's Short Format Video Recommendations, arXiv 2301.04945](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.04945)
- **Curiosity gaps and memory (lab):** Gruber, Gelman & Ranganath (Neuron, 2014) used a trivia question → delay → answer paradigm that builds on Kang et al. (2009). High curiosity increased memory for the answers. It also increased memory for **incidental** material shown during the anticipation window, and this was linked to midbrain/caudate and hippocampal activity. **[PEER-REVIEWED][OLDER]** — [Gruber et al. 2014, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4252494)
- **Caveat:** a 2024 *npj Science of Learning* study found **poorer** memory for scholastic facts presented after trivia questions that had elicited high curiosity, compared with low curiosity. The "curiosity spill-over" benefit may not transfer to academic content placed in the anticipation window. **[PEER-REVIEWED]** — [npj Science of Learning 2024 (s41539-024-00234-w)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41539-024-00234-w.pdf)

**Vendor data (low confidence)**
- One vendor analysis of "10,000 TikTok videos" reports median retention of **56% at 3 s, 44% at 5 s and 28% at 10 s**, with a 0.83 correlation between 5-second retention and total watch depth. Another blog claims an average 3-s retention of **32%** on TikTok. These baselines conflict and neither method is auditable. **[VENDOR/CREATOR CLAIM]** — [grow-it.se](https://www.grow-it.se/en/resources/short-form-video-retention-benchmarks); [blog.mean.ceo](https://blog.mean.ceo/video-hook-retention-statistics/)
- One firm claims "humor in first three seconds earns 51% more shares" from 47 A/B tests, but gives no design detail. **[VENDOR/CREATOR CLAIM]** — [Green Frog Labs](https://greenfroglabs.com/blog/anatomy-of-viral-hook)

### Inferences
- For a clinician audience, the hook should be the **core paradox of the lesson itself**, for example placebo responses in endodontic pain trials. It should not be a tangential teaser. The 2024 npj result suggests that curiosity about a side question may not help, and may even hurt, retention of the academic fact. The open loop and the learning objective should be the same thing.
- Use **three hook channels at once in frame 1**: a moving visual (no static title card), on-screen text in the safe zone, and spoken narration from 0.0 s. Sound-off viewers then get the text hook, and sound-on viewers get the voice. This "multi-channel" approach is an inference from the sound-off data (Section 4) and TikTok's "Think sound-on" guidance. It has not been tested as a hook variable.
- Follow TikTok's "every hook needs a payoff" rule. Signal the payoff early ("by the end you'll know why…") and close it explicitly before the end card.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study compares visual-first vs. spoken-first vs. text-first hooks on Reels/TikTok/Shorts, or measures open-loop phrasing against swipe-away rates.
- I found no Instagram-published retention benchmark at 3 s.
- TikTok's 2020 "Value of View" analysis, the source of the "first six seconds" claim, is not publicly available to verify.

## 2. Retention and length: how do 2025–2026 Instagram and TikTok ranking signals treat watch time, completion, rewatches, shares and saves? What length works for education, and is 90 s viable on Reels?

### Takeaway
For Instagram, Mosseri's most-cited guidance (January 2025) names **watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach** as the three most important signals. **Sends (DM shares) matter slightly more for reaching non-followers**, and likes matter slightly more for followers. Instagram made **Reels up to 3 minutes recommendable to non-followers** in January 2025, so 90 s is well inside the recommendable range. Industry data for H1 2026 shows **60–90 s Reels earn about the same median views as 45–60 s Reels**, with roughly a 6% gap, and only Reels over 3 minutes drop sharply. I found no official 2025–2026 TikTok statement that ranks completion against shares against saves. YouTube Shorts allows up to 3 minutes for vertical or square uploads.

### Cited Findings
**Instagram: ranking signals (official/Mosseri)**
- In a January 2025 video, Mosseri named the three factors with the most algorithmic weight. He told creators to watch **average watch time, likes per reach, and sends per reach** in Insights. He said likes are "slightly" more important for connected content (followers) and sends "slightly" more important for unconnected content (reaching new people). **[OFFICIAL, via trade press]** — [Social Media Today, "Instagram shares algorithm insights 2025"](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-insights-2025/738034/)
- Earlier (2024), Mosseri said post share rates are "now a key driver of reach." **[OFFICIAL, via trade press]** — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-chief-says-post-share-rates-are-now-a-key-driver-of-reach/716540/)
- Instagram moved creators toward "Views" as the primary metric. Mosseri later described **Views and Sends per Reach** as the most important metrics to focus on. **[OFFICIAL, via trade press]** — [Social Media Today](https://socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-updates-metrics-to-focus-creators-on-views/723645/)
- Caution: the widely repeated claim that sends are weighted "3–5×" likes is **not** Mosseri's. His wording was "slightly." Claims about a "July 2026 skip-rate ranking signal" have no Instagram or Mosseri source. **[SECONDARY, debunking]** — [Hanami Social](https://hanamisocial.com/en/blog/instagram-reels-algorithm-2026/); [Keywords Everywhere algorithm tracker](https://keywordseverywhere.com/news/instagram-algorithm-updates/)
- Buffer's 2026 algorithm guide still treats Mosseri's three signals as the reference point. **[INDUSTRY]** — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources//instagram-algorithms/)

**Instagram: length**
- Official, Jan 21, 2025: "Starting today, we're letting creators in the United States publish reels up to 3 minutes long in the Reels editor." Previously only reels up to 90 seconds were eligible for recommendation. Reels up to 3 minutes can now be shown to non-followers in the Reels tab and Explore. The same post introduced **Trial reels**, which are shown first to non-followers before being shared to followers. **[OFFICIAL]** — [Instagram Creators blog (Jan 21, 2025)](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/the-latest-with-instagram)
- Mosseri said the old 90 s cap was "too short" for some stories. In July 2024 he had argued that longer videos could dilute Instagram's core identity, so this was a policy reversal. Instagram said it will recommend longer Reels in discovery surfaces. **[OFFICIAL, via trade press]** — [Social Media Today (3-minute expansion)](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-officially-expands-reels-length-to-3-minutes/737766/); [Social Media Today (will recommend longer Reels)](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-will-recommend-longer-3-minute-reels/737913/)
- **Socialinsider, Jan–Jun 2026, ~9.77M organic Reels from brands with 1K–1M followers** (published Jul 14, 2026; updated Oct 20, 2026). **[INDUSTRY DATA]** — [Socialinsider, "Ideal Instagram Reels length"](https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/instagram-reels-length/)
  - Engagement rate (likes + comments ÷ followers): 0–30 s 0.28%; 30–45 s 0.30%; **45–60 s 0.35% (highest)**; **60–90 s 0.30%**; 90–120 s 0.30%; 120–180 s 0.33%; 180 s+ 0.15%.
  - Median views: 1–30 s 4,700; 30–45 s 8,564; **45–60 s 10,374**; **60–90 s 9,790**; 90–120 s 8,000; 120–180 s 9,000; 180 s+ 4,428.
  - Caveats: the engagement-rate formula **excludes saves and sends**. There are internal inconsistencies: tier counts don't sum, and a 100K+ tier appears despite a stated exclusion. The data covers brand accounts, not clinician-educators. No watch-time or completion data is included.
- A separate Socialinsider analysis of 140,000 business Reels (Jan–Jun 2026) reports a 9.78% reach rate for 1K–5K-follower accounts, against 8.80% for carousels and 7% for images. **[INDUSTRY DATA, via search summary; not verified on page]** — [Socialinsider Reels statistics 2026](https://socialinsider.io/blog/instagram-reels-statistics/)

**TikTok**
- TikTok's official SMB playbook says: "TikTok supports video lengths ranging from 15 sec to 3min. Any video length within this range can find success." This is outdated relative to current upload limits, and it is ads-oriented. **[OFFICIAL]** — [TikTok SMB Creative Playbook (PDF)](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/SMB_Creative_Playbook_External.pdf)
- Creator Rewards Program: only original videos **≥1 minute** are eligible. A "qualified view" reportedly requires ≥5 s of continuous viewing from the For You feed. The Creator Fund ended Dec 16, 2023, and the Creativity Program became Creator Rewards on Mar 18, 2024. These details are **third-party, and sources disagree on the exact cutoff (60 vs. 61 s)**. **[SECONDARY]** — [Neal Schaffer](https://nealschaffer.com/tiktok-creator-rewards-program/); [Kompozy](https://kompozy.io/creator-growth/tiktok-creator-rewards-program)
- TikTok data donation: 55% of videos are not watched to completion. **[PREPRINT][OLDER]** — [arXiv 2301.04945](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.04945)
- In a study of more than 1,000 videos from 6 STEM creators, the authors report that, regardless of video length, average watch time does not exceed about **27 seconds**, and the share of viewers who finish declines as length increases. The sample is small and creator-supplied. **[PREPRINT, 2024]** — [Oshinowo et al., "Investigating the dissemination of STEM content on social media with computational tools," arXiv 2404.18944](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.18944)

**YouTube Shorts (official)**
- "Starting on October 15, you can upload Shorts up to 3 minutes long" (2024). **[OFFICIAL]** — [YouTube Official Blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/tall-updates-coming-to-shorts/)
- Any upload of 3 minutes or less with a "square or vertical aspect ratio" is classified as a Short. **Shorts over 1 minute with an active Content ID claim of any type are blocked globally**. Most songs can be used for up to 90 s in a 3-minute Short. **[OFFICIAL]** — [YouTube Help, "Understand three-minute YouTube Shorts"](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877?hl=en)

**Educational-video length research (non-short-form context)**
- Guo, Kim & Rubin analyzed millions of edX viewing sessions and found that "shorter videos are much more engaging." They recommend segmenting into chunks **<6 minutes**. **[PEER-REVIEWED][OLDER, 2014]** — [Guo et al. 2014, L@S](https://www.cs.rochester.edu/hci/pubs/pdfs/edX-MOOC-video-production-and-engagement_LAS-2014.pdf)
- A 2026 Frontiers in Education article on biomedical-engineering teaching argues that Mayer's segmenting, signaling and coherence principles point to brief, focused presentations. It cites engagement peaking around 6 minutes. **[PEER-REVIEWED]** — [Frontiers in Education 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2026.1996663/full)
- In a TikTok study of age-related macular degeneration videos, duration had only a mild **positive** correlation with engagement (r ≈ 0.23–0.30). Longer health videos were not penalized. **[PEER-REVIEWED]** — [PMC12832813](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12832813/)

### Inferences
- **90 s is viable on Reels in 2025–2026.** It is inside the official ≤3-min recommendable window. In H1 2026 industry data, 60–90 s Reels performed close to the best bracket, and the steep penalty only appears above 3 minutes. Expect a lower completion percentage than a 30–45 s cut. Because Mosseri emphasizes **average watch time** (absolute seconds), a well-paced 90 s video can bank more watch-seconds than a 30 s video even with lower completion. This is an inference: Instagram has not published how it normalizes watch time by length.
- Given that average STEM watch time is about 27 s, design the **first ~25–30 s as a self-contained core takeaway**, so viewers who drop early still learn the key idea. The remaining 60 s adds nuance (mechanism, evidence, clinical implication) for viewers who stay.
- For a clinician audience on Instagram, **sends per reach** is the growth lever: residents DM-ing attendings and co-residents. Write a concrete reason to send, such as "send this to whoever's presenting journal club."
- **Hedge with a cut-down.** Produce a 45–60 s variant: the 45–60 s bracket was the top performer in the 2026 Socialinsider data. Test hook variants with Instagram **Trial reels** before publishing to followers.
- For YouTube Shorts cross-posting, use **only royalty-free or original music**. A 90 s Short with any Content ID claim is blocked globally.

### Gaps
- Neither Instagram nor TikTok has published how **rewatches/loops** or **saves** are weighted in 2025–2026. Mosseri's January 2025 statement covers watch time, likes and sends, not saves.
- I found no official 2025–2026 TikTok Creator Academy text on ranking-signal weights. The pages were not retrievable, and third-party summaries could not be verified.
- No source provides **watch-time or completion benchmarks for 60–90 s educational Reels** specifically. A claim attributed to Metricool, that average Reel watch time rose from about 4 s (2025) to 8.5 s (2026), could not be verified at source.
- Socialinsider's full-year 2025 benchmark reportedly found carousels edging Reels on engagement rate, while other studies report the opposite. Format comparisons are sample-dependent.

## 3. Pacing: cut frequency, visual change every N seconds, pattern interrupts, and narration speed (words per minute, comprehension vs. retention)

### Takeaway
There is **no rigorous evidence for a specific "change the visual every N seconds" rule**. Those numbers are creator lore. One 2026 preprint on brand/organizational TikToks finds that **editing pace is associated with engagement**, most strongly with shares and saves, but this is correlational. Narration-rate research is more solid. **Comprehension of recorded instruction holds up to roughly 2× normal speech** (normal ≈ 150 wpm) and declines around 275 wpm. Learners voluntarily speed up slow presenters to about 180–280 wpm. A brisk 160–180 wpm is therefore safe for comprehension. The binding constraint for sound-off viewers is **caption reading speed**, not listening speed.

### Cited Findings
- In an analysis of brand and organizational TikTok videos, "editing pace" was significantly associated with likes, comments, shares and saves, with the strongest associations for **shares (.41) and saves (.39)**, as tabulated. The statistic type is unclear, and the design is correlational. **[PREPRINT, 2026]** — [arXiv 2606.16053](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.16053)
- Practitioner rules such as "first cut within 3 s" and "no static shot longer than ~4–5 s," and claims like "3.3× higher completion from rapid cuts," have **no traceable methodology**. **[VENDOR/CREATOR CLAIM]** — [Komet Media pacing guide](https://www.kometmedia.com/tutorials/pacing-and-cuts-for-high-retention-short-video)
- TikTok's official production tips recommend transitions, text overlays to "emphasize key messages," and "stickers & graphics" to "highlight key information you want your audiences to remember." They give no cut-rate number. For voice-over the guidance is: "Be sure to keep it clear and concise." **[OFFICIAL]** — [TikTok SMB Creative Playbook (PDF)](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/SMB_Creative_Playbook_External.pdf)
- **Speech-rate experiment (UCLA, Murphy et al.):** 231 undergraduates watched 13–15 min lectures at 1×, 1.5×, 2× and 2.5×. Speed "had little effect on both immediate and delayed comprehension until learners exceeded twice the normal speed." Normal speech is about 150 wpm, and comprehension tends to decline as speech approaches about 275 wpm. **[PEER-REVIEWED, 2021–22]** — [UCLA Newsroom](https://newsroom.ucla.edu/releases/learning-while-speed-watching-class-videos); [Murphy et al., Applied Cognitive Psychology (PDF)](https://castel.psych.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/111/2021/11/ACP-Lecture-Speed-Murphy-2021-in-press.pdf)
- A follow-up study in younger and older adults used lecture rates of about **126 wpm (0.75×), 157 wpm (1×) and 314 wpm (2×)**. **[PEER-REVIEWED, 2023]** — [PMC10330257](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10330257/)
- In an analysis of engineering educational videos, viewers sped playback to about **180–280 wpm**, and did so more often when the presenter spoke slowly. Engagement depended on speaking rate alongside length, format and presenter style. **[PEER-REVIEWED]** — [Informit, "Examining the effect of presenter speaking rate on student engagement with educational videos"](https://search.informit.org/doi/abs/10.3316/informit.T2026031700015200447165531)
- Classic compressed-speech research found listening rates of 228 and 278 wpm more efficient for learning than a normal 178 wpm. A separate videotape study found a significant comprehension drop above 275 wpm. **[OLDER, 1970s ERIC reports]** — [ERIC ED048756](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED048756.pdf)
- Guo et al. (edX) flagged instructor **speaking speed and enthusiasm** as engagement factors. Informal talking-head and Khan-style drawing videos beat slide-only videos. **[OLDER, 2014; speaking-speed detail via secondary summary]** — [Guo et al. 2014](https://www.cs.rochester.edu/hci/pubs/pdfs/edX-MOOC-video-production-and-engagement_LAS-2014.pdf); [UBC Video Learning Hub summary](https://blogs.ubc.ca/videolearninghub/?p=569)
- Mayer's **segmenting** principle: "People learn more deeply when a multimedia message is presented in learner-paced segments rather than as a continuous unit". The **signaling** principle states that learning improves when cues highlight the organization of essential material. **[PEER-REVIEWED/TEXTBOOK]** — [Mayer 2020 multimedia principles (PDF)](https://ugc.futurelearn.com/uploads/files/7d/d6/7dd6188d-c343-4311-b064-ac98d2c95abc/Multimedia_Principles._R._E._Mayer__2020.pdf); [Vanderbilt anatomy multimedia design paper](https://www.vumc.org/faculty/sites/default/files/Medical%20Education/Multimedia%20approach%20to%20medical%20edu.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Narration target: about 160–175 wpm** (brisk but not rushed). This is comfortably below the ~275 wpm comprehension cliff. Short-form viewers cannot pause or rewind as easily as MOOC learners can, so stay well under the speed-watching ceiling. For 90 s, minus about 3 s of hook-silence and pauses and about 3–4 s of end card, that gives roughly **220–240 spoken words**.
- **Visual change:** with no evidence base for a specific interval, a defensible design rule is that every narrated clause (about every 2–4 s) gets a corresponding visual state change. Examples are a new animation step, a highlight, a zoom, or a text pop. Make each change **semantically tied to the narration (signaling)**, not decorative motion, which Mayer's coherence principle warns against.
- **Pattern interrupts**: use them at segment boundaries, such as a color/scene change or a number reveal. This gives segmenting cues for learning and also resets attention, at about 20–25 s intervals. With 4 segments in 90 s, each "chapter" is about 20 s.

### Gaps
- No controlled study isolates cut frequency or shot length as a cause of retention on Reels/TikTok/Shorts.
- No study tests optimal wpm **specifically for short-form vertical video**, or the interaction of narration rate with burned-in captions read on a phone.

## 4. Captions and text: sound-off share (current vs. outdated data), burned-in vs. auto-captions, safe zones (pixels), and font size

### Takeaway
The "85% watch muted" statistic is **a 2016 Facebook-publisher figure from an era of muted autoplay**. It should not be applied to Reels or TikTok in 2026. The best-sourced data is still a **2019 Verizon Media/Publicis survey: 69% sound-off in public and 25% in private**, plus 80% more likely to finish a video with captions. TikTok officially describes itself as a sound-on platform. A secondhand claim says over 80% of Reels are viewed with sound on, but I found no primary Meta source. **I found no 2024–2026 primary measurement of sound-off share on Reels/TikTok/Shorts.** Safe zones are not published as pixel specs by any platform for organic content. Meta's ads spec (≈14% top, 35% bottom, 6% sides) and third-party overlays converge on a **combined "all-platform" safe core of about 840 × 960 px** on 1080 × 1920.

### Cited Findings
**Sound-off share**
- The **85%** figure traces to a **May 2016 Digiday report** in which Facebook publishers described their own silent views, when Facebook autoplayed feed video muted. It is not a study of short-form platforms, and its attribution to "Meta" is unverified. **[SECONDARY, debunking]** — [eCommerce Fastlane (2026)](https://ecommercefastlane.com/adding-subtitles-to-product-videos/)
- **Verizon Media + Publicis Media (2019), 5,616 US consumers:** 69% watch video with sound off in public places and 25% in private places. 80% are more likely to watch an entire video when captions are available. **[INDUSTRY DATA][OLDER]** — [Forbes, Jul 31 2019](https://www.forbes.com/sites/tjmccue/2019/07/31/verizon-media-says-69-percent-of-consumers-watching-video-with-sound-off/)
- 2026 caption roundups still rely on the 2019 study, including a "92% of mobile viewers watch with sound off" figure. That is a misreading or overextension of the 2019 data, so flag it. No newer replacement study was found. **[SECONDARY]** — [VidMint caption statistics 2026](https://vidmint.io/blog/caption-statistics-2026.html)
- "80%+ of Reels are viewed with sound on" is attributed to "trade coverage of Meta guidance," with **no primary Meta link**. **[SECONDARY, unverified]** — [Dynamoi](https://dynamoi.com/learn/statistics/instagram-reels-music-discovery-statistics)
- TikTok official: "Think Sound-On… Audio plays a crucial role in content on TikTok" and "To capture attention, videos should always include sound." Voice-over is recommended for "how-to content… or narration." **[OFFICIAL]** — [TikTok SMB Creative Playbook (PDF)](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/SMB_Creative_Playbook_External.pdf)

**Burned-in vs. auto-captions**
- TikTok's CapCut is officially promoted for "auto-caption" and "text overlay: Emphasize key messages and visually engage your customers with text." Instagram's 2025 update added "new fonts, animations and effects" in its text tools. Neither platform publishes evidence comparing burned-in and native auto-captions. **[OFFICIAL]** — [TikTok SMB Creative Playbook (PDF)](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/SMB_Creative_Playbook_External.pdf); [Instagram Creators blog](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/the-latest-with-instagram)

**Safe zones (1080 × 1920)**: none of these are official organic-content specs
- **Meta Reels ads:** keep about **14% top, 35% bottom and 6% each side** clear, or about **269 px top, 672 px bottom and 65 px sides**. Use **40% bottom** if the ad carries a disclaimer. Some guides list 20–35% bottom. Meta's Help Centre reportedly states the rule only in words, so the percentages may be derived from Meta's diagram. **[SECONDARY reporting of OFFICIAL ads spec]** — [AdNabu Meta safe zones](https://blog.adnabu.com/meta-ads/meta-safe-zones/); [Billo, 2026 unified creative updates](https://billo.app/blog/meta-ads-safe-zones); [Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/instagram-reels-safe-zone/)
- **Instagram Reels (organic, third-party):** 270 px top, 670 px bottom, 65 px left, 120 px right. **[VENDOR]** — [revid.ai](https://www.revid.ai/tiktok-safe-zone)
- **TikTok in-feed ads:** 240 px top, 660 px bottom and 120 px sides, plus a **180 px right-hand button column from about y = 840 to 1260**. TikTok organic safe area is about **896 × 1470 px** (third-party). Other guides give 130–140 px top, 400–480 px bottom and 150–180 px right. **[VENDOR, conflicting]** — [TypographyEdit](https://typographyedit.com/tools/safe-zone-checker); [uplads](https://uplads.com/tools/safe-zone-checker); [BranDeal](https://brandeal.ai/en/blog/tiktok-safe-zone-guide); [Faceless](https://faceless.so/tools/safe-zone-checker)
- **Combined "all three apps" overlay:** **288 px top, 672 px bottom, 65 px left, 192 px right**, leaving a safe core of about **840 × 960 px** (x ≈ 65–888, y ≈ 288–1248). A Google vertical overlay uses the same values with 48 px left. **[VENDOR]** — [revid.ai](https://www.revid.ai/tiktok-safe-zone); [TypographyEdit](https://typographyedit.com/tools/safe-zone-checker)
- Multiple sources note that app UIs change often and that these are conservative bounds. Verify on-device before posting. **[VENDOR]** — [revid.ai](https://www.revid.ai/tiktok-safe-zone)

### Inferences
- **Design to the combined safe core (about x 65–888, y 288–1248).** Place captions in the **upper-middle band (about y 1000–1240)**, not at the bottom. On Reels, the bottom ~670 px is covered by the caption, username and audio strip, and the right ~120–190 px by the like/comment/share/send rail. Keep citations and key labels out of the right rail.
- **Burn in captions.** The 2019 sound-off data is dated, but residents and attendings often watch in clinic hallways or between patients, where sound-off viewing is plausible. Burned-in captions also guarantee correct spelling of dental terms ("apical periodontitis," "nocebo"), which auto-captions may garble. Treat native auto-captions as a backup accessibility layer, not the primary text.
- **Font size (inference only; no evidence found):** at 1080 px width, caption text around **54–72 px** (≈5–7% of frame width), bold sans-serif, with a stroke or a semi-opaque box behind it. Body text should not go below about 42 px. Limit captions to **≤2 lines and ≤~32 characters per line**, in 2–5-word chunks synced to speech. These values are practitioner conventions and should be checked on a phone at arm's length.

### Gaps
- No 2024–2026 primary measurement of the sound-off share on Reels, TikTok or Shorts was found. The TikTok-commissioned "88% say sound is vital" survey figure is widely repeated, but the primary report could not be located.
- No study compares burned-in captions with platform auto-captions on retention.
- A Meta ad-data claim that ads with music **and** voice-over draw a 15% higher average positive response than ads without sound appeared in search summaries, but I could not tie it to a verified primary page. It is also ad-specific, so it is not cited above.
- No platform publishes official pixel safe zones for **organic** Reels/TikTok/Shorts. All figures are ad specs or third-party measurements.
- No empirical source was found for minimum legible font size on vertical mobile video.

## 5. Structure: proven templates for educational short-form (hook → payoff → loop-back), series/episodic formats, and sequencing on-screen text with narration

### Takeaway
The only **official** structural template found is TikTok's **Hook (first 3–6 s) → key points/"narrative" → CTA**, with the explicit rule "every hook needs a payoff." Learning science (Mayer's segmenting and signaling) supports **short labelled segments**. Text should **reinforce, not duplicate**, narration: show key terms and numbers, not full sentences. I found no empirical study of loop-backs or episodic series on short-form platforms. Instagram's Trial reels are an official way to A/B test structures with non-followers.

### Cited Findings
- TikTok's official content structure is "The Hook" ("first 3-6 seconds… crucial"), then "Unique Selling Points" ("highlight… key narrative in a clear & memorable way to build trust"), then "Call-to-action" ("Leverage text, voiceover, and/or graphics"). These are framed as a "Narrative Flow." **[OFFICIAL]** — [TikTok SMB Creative Playbook (PDF)](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/SMB_Creative_Playbook_External.pdf)
- TikTok recommends list-style hooks ("3 reasons why…", "5 ways to…", "3 signs you NEED…") and problem/solution framing. **[OFFICIAL]** — [TikTok SMB Creative Playbook (PDF)](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/SMB_Creative_Playbook_External.pdf)
- Instagram: "One of the most effective ways to grow is to post original content consistently." Trial reels let creators "share a reel first with non-followers before deciding whether to share it with followers." **[OFFICIAL]** — [Instagram Creators blog (Jan 2025)](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/the-latest-with-instagram)
- Mayer's segmenting and signaling principles (see Section 3). A 2026 Frontiers article applies them to argue for brief, focused segments in biomedical teaching videos. **[PEER-REVIEWED]** — [Mayer 2020 (PDF)](https://ugc.futurelearn.com/uploads/files/7d/d6/7dd6188d-c343-4311-b064-ac98d2c95abc/Multimedia_Principles._R._E._Mayer__2020.pdf); [Frontiers in Education 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2026.1996663/full)
- Guo et al.: Khan-style tablet drawing ("drawing while explaining") was more engaging than slides. This supports animated build-ups over static slides. **[OLDER, 2014]** — [Guo et al. 2014](https://www.cs.rochester.edu/hci/pubs/pdfs/edX-MOOC-video-production-and-engagement_LAS-2014.pdf)
- Swipes cluster at both the start and the **tail end** of videos. Endings are a drop-off point, so long outros waste watch time. **[PREPRINT][OLDER]** — [Dashlet, arXiv 2204.12954](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.12954)

### Inferences
Suggested 90 s template (beats in seconds):
1. **0–3 s, hook.** Paradox or question with a visual already moving, a 3–6-word on-screen text headline, and narration starting at frame 1.
2. **3–8 s, promise and stakes.** Say what the viewer will know by the end and why it matters clinically, for example how much of "success" in a pain trial is placebo.
3. **8–30 s, core takeaway** (self-contained for early leavers). One clear claim plus one key number, with an on-screen citation.
4. **30–55 s, mechanism.** An animated explanation such as expectation → descending modulation, or nocebo.
5. **55–75 s, evidence and nuance.** Endodontic trial-design implications such as sham controls and regression to the mean. Use a compact "receipt" card.
6. **75–85 s, clinical translation.** One actionable rule, e.g. how pre-op communication framing affects reported pain.
7. **85–90 s, payoff and loop-back.** Restate the opening question with its answer. The final frame visually matches frame 1 for a seamless loop. Use a soft CTA ("Send to your co-resident"), short so it isn't a tail-end exit point.

- **Text–narration sequencing:** on-screen text should appear **at or just before** the spoken keyword (signaling). Show **keywords, numbers and citations**, and caption the narration separately in the caption band. Avoid paragraphs of on-screen text that compete with narration (redundancy and coherence principles).
- **Series format:** number episodes ("Placebo in Endo, 1/4") and use a consistent visual template. This supports "post consistently," gives followers a reason to follow, and lets each episode stand alone. This is an inference; no data was found on series effects.

### Gaps
- No empirical data was found on loop-back endings, rewatch rates, or episodic/series formats on Reels/TikTok/Shorts.
- No study was found on the optimal timing offset between on-screen text and narration in short-form vertical video.

## 6. Science, medical and dental communication on short-form: content quality vs. engagement studies, what top educators do, and what makes clinician audiences save or share

### Takeaway
The peer-reviewed literature, mostly cross-sectional DISCERN/GQS/JAMA-benchmark audits, finds that health content on TikTok and Instagram is **moderate-to-low in quality and rarely cites sources**. Health-professional and specialist creators **score higher on quality but do not reliably get more engagement**. Studies disagree on whether engagement tracks quality: positive in a 2024 coronary disease study, negative in a 2023 gallstone study. **Dental and endodontic-adjacent studies (2025–2026) rate Instagram Reels and TikTok below YouTube for reliability**, and nearly all short-form dental videos fail JAMA benchmarks (authorship, attribution, disclosure, currency). I found **no study on what makes clinician audiences save or share**.

### Cited Findings
**General health on TikTok/Instagram**
- *JMIR Formative Research* 2024, TikTok coronary heart disease videos (DISCERN, JAMA, GQS): cardiologists outperformed non-cardiology clinicians on DISCERN and GQS (P = .02). Engagement metrics (likes, comments, collections/saves, shares) **correlated positively** with quality indicators. **[PEER-REVIEWED]** — [JMIR Form Res 2024;e51513](https://formative.jmir.org/2024/1/e51513/PDF)
- *JMIR Formative Research* 2026, TikTok videos on five rare genetic conditions: only **7 videos (3.8%) cited scientific literature**. Clinician-made videos scored higher on quality but had **lower visibility**. The authors call for more healthcare-professional presence and better visibility of evidence-based content. **[PEER-REVIEWED]** — [JMIR Form Res 2026;e79978](https://formative.jmir.org/2026/1/e79978)
- *JMIR* 2023, TikTok gallstone disease videos: quality was mostly fair to moderate, and **DISCERN scores correlated negatively with likes and shares**. **[PEER-REVIEWED][OLDER]** — [J Med Internet Res 2023;e39162](https://www.jmir.org/2023/1/e39162/XML)
- *JAMA Network Open* (Feb 2025, University of Sydney): of nearly 1,000 Instagram and TikTok posts on five controversial medical tests, the vast majority were "overwhelmingly misleading." About 85% did not mention downsides or risks. Secondary outlets describe the 85% figure differently, so check the paper. **[PEER-REVIEWED, via university release]** — [University of Sydney news](https://www.sydney.edu.au/news-opinion/news/2025/02/27/--influencers-promoting--overwhelmingly--misleading-information-.html); [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1074797)
- TikTok age-related macular degeneration videos: duration correlated mildly and positively with engagement (r ≈ 0.23–0.30). **[PEER-REVIEWED]** — [PMC12832813](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12832813/)
- Orthopaedic content on TikTok and Instagram was of low average quality. **[PEER-REVIEWED][OLDER, 2023]** — [PMC10696526](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10696526/)

**Dental / endodontic**
- *Restorative Dentistry & Endodontics* 2025, 158 English-language videos on pulp therapy across YouTube, TikTok and Instagram: TikTok and Instagram Reels offered "high-to-moderate quality information," but YouTube was more reliable. **73.1% of YouTube, 99% of TikTok and 99% of Instagram videos received low JAMA benchmark scores.** **[PEER-REVIEWED]** — [RDE 2025;50:e26](https://rde.ac/journal/view.php?doi=10.5395%2Frde.2025.50.e26); [PubMed 40904256](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40904256/)
- 2026 rubber-dam technique study with three endodontist raters: YouTube significantly outperformed Instagram Reels on GQS, JAMA and mPEMAT. **[PEER-REVIEWED]** — [PubMed 42432627](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42432627/)
- 2026 biomimetic dentistry study, YouTube and TikTok: uploaders were mostly dental professionals (YouTube 85%, TikTok 100%). Quality was moderate, and **no videos came from academic institutions**. **[PEER-REVIEWED]** — [Acıbadem Univ. Health Sci. J.](http://journal.acibadem.edu.tr/en/pub/article/1976462)
- Facebook endodontic groups have been studied as learning venues for clinicians. Its findings were not reviewed here. **[PEER-REVIEWED]** — [PMC11675520](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11675520/)

**STEM creators**
- In more than 1,000 videos from 6 STEM creators, audiences generated interest signals (likes, bookmarks, comments, shares) that were analyzed against views, and content from newer creators was "disseminated differently." **[PREPRINT, 2024]** — [arXiv 2404.18944](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18944)

### Inferences
- There is a clear **quality gap**: dental and endodontic short-form content almost universally fails JAMA benchmarks, and no academic institutions are producing it. An evidence-cited, specialist-made endodontic explainer is differentiated by default. Professional audiences who can judge quality are the people most likely to **send** it to peers.
- The quality–engagement link is inconsistent, so **evidence alone will not drive reach**. The hook, pacing and "sendability" (Sections 1–5) still matter. Specialist credibility helps quality scores, not reach.
- Clinician-audience save/share drivers (inference, untested): content that is **reusable** (a one-line clinical rule, a figure they can cite at journal club), **status-safe to share** (accurate and cited, so forwarding it doesn't risk embarrassment), and **conversation-starting** (a counterintuitive finding about their own outcomes data).

### Gaps
- No study was found on what drives **saves or shares among clinician or dental-professional audiences** specifically.
- No 2024–2026 study rates **endodontic Reels/TikTok** content for engagement. Existing studies rate quality, mostly for patient-facing content.
- No primary data was found on the practices of top medical or dental educators on Instagram, such as format, length or citation habits. This would require a content audit.

## 7. Credibility signals: on-screen citations, "receipts", and how professional audiences respond

### Takeaway
**No study was found that experimentally tests on-screen citations in short-form video on trust or engagement among professionals.** The indirect evidence is consistent, though. Source citation is **almost absent** in health short-form content (3.8% in one 2026 TikTok study). Nearly all dental short-form videos fail the **JAMA benchmark**, which explicitly scores authorship, attribution/references, disclosure and currency. Visible citations are therefore a cheap way to move from the bottom of the quality distribution to the top.

### Cited Findings
- Only 3.8% of TikTok videos on rare genetic conditions cited scientific literature. Clinician videos were higher quality but less visible. **[PEER-REVIEWED]** — [JMIR Form Res 2026;e79978](https://formative.jmir.org/2026/1/e79978)
- 99% of TikTok and 99% of Instagram pulp-therapy videos had low JAMA benchmark scores. The JAMA benchmark rewards authorship, attribution, disclosure and currency. **[PEER-REVIEWED]** — [RDE 2025](https://rde.ac/journal/view.php?doi=10.5395%2Frde.2025.50.e26)
- Rubber dam videos: Instagram Reels scored lower than YouTube on JAMA and GQS as rated by endodontists. **[PEER-REVIEWED]** — [PubMed 42432627](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42432627/)
- Specialist creators (cardiologists) had higher DISCERN and GQS scores than non-specialist clinicians. **[PEER-REVIEWED]** — [JMIR Form Res 2024;e51513](https://formative.jmir.org/2024/1/e51513/PDF)
- TikTok's official playbook frames the post-hook section as building "trust around your product's benefits." It recommends text, stickers and graphics to "highlight key information you want your audiences to remember." This is ad framing, applicable to evidence cards. **[OFFICIAL]** — [TikTok SMB Creative Playbook (PDF)](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/SMB_Creative_Playbook_External.pdf)
- Health professionals use "debunk-it-yourself" strategies on TikTok when responding to misinformation. Only the reference list was retrieved, so findings were not verified. **[PREPRINT]** — [arXiv 2412.04999](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.04999)

### Inferences
- **On-screen "receipt" spec:** when a claim is spoken, show a compact citation chip, e.g. *"Author et al., J Endod 2023 (RCT, n = …)"*. Place it inside the safe core, upper area (y ≈ 300–450), for **≥2.5–3 s**, so it is readable when paused. Use the same visual style every time so it becomes a recognizable trust marker.
- **Satisfy JAMA benchmark items explicitly.** Name the presenter and credentials (authorship) on screen in the first 5 s or on the end card. Give full references in the post caption or a pinned comment (attribution). Add a one-line disclosure ("No conflicts" or the funding source). Include a date or "evidence as of 2026" (currency).
- Distinguish **evidence levels** on screen (RCT vs. observational vs. meta-analysis). Professional audiences can judge this, and it differentiates the video from the 99%-low-JAMA field.
- Avoid overclaiming. Placebo/nocebo effect sizes in pain trials vary, and the JAMA Netw Open 2025 finding that most posts omit harms or limitations suggests that **stating limitations** is itself a credibility signal.

### Gaps
- No experimental study was found on whether on-screen citations change trust, saves or shares among professional viewers of short-form video.
- A 2025 systematic review (30 studies) reportedly found that young adults judge health influencers by heuristics such as reputation and aesthetics more than by verified expertise. I could only find it secondhand, so it is not cited here as a finding.

## 8. Technical delivery specs: resolution, frame rate, bitrate and loudness (LUFS) for Reels, TikTok and Shorts uploads (2025–2026)

### Takeaway
All three platforms converge on **9:16, 1080 × 1920, MP4 (H.264 + AAC)**. **30 fps** is the safe default, and 60 fps suits fast motion. Platforms do not publish organic upload **bitrate** targets: third-party recommendations range from 3.5 to 16 Mbps. **No platform publishes a loudness (LUFS) target for organic short-form.** The common −14 LUFS recommendation comes from one third-party guide and general streaming practice, not platform documentation. TikTok officially says not to upload below 720p.

### Cited Findings
- TikTok official: "keep videos full screen at a 9:16 vertical aspect ratio" and "videos should not be below 720P." **[OFFICIAL]** — [TikTok SMB Creative Playbook (PDF)](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/SMB_Creative_Playbook_External.pdf)
- YouTube official: uploads ≤3 min with a "square or vertical aspect ratio" are Shorts. To avoid Short classification, use 16:9. **[OFFICIAL]** — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877?hl=en)
- Instagram official: Reels up to 3 minutes in the Reels editor (US first, January 2025). The Edits app exports "without a watermark." **[OFFICIAL]** — [Instagram Creators blog](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/the-latest-with-instagram)
- Third-party consensus: 9:16 at 1080 × 1920 for all three; MP4/H.264/AAC is the working format. Documented codec figures exist only for Reels and TikTok, not Shorts. **[VENDOR]** — [Blck Alpaca specs 2026](https://blckalpaca.at/en/knowledge-base/social-media/social-media-content-creation-formats/instagram-reels-format-shorts-tiktok-specs); [Conbersa export settings](https://www.conbersa.ai/learn/vertical-video-export-settings-by-platform)
- Frame rate: 30 fps is the safest. Reels supports 24/25/30/60 fps, and the 23–60 fps range is accepted on Reels and TikTok. **[VENDOR]** — [ClipSpeed](https://www.clipspeed.ai/blog/video-resolution-guide-shorts-reels-tiktok.html); [Blitzcut](https://blitzcutai.com/blog/social-video-specs-2026)
- Bitrate recommendations conflict: 3.5–5 Mbps for 1080p Reels in one guide; 8–12 Mbps at 30 fps and 12–16 Mbps at 60 fps in another; 10–15 Mbps in a third. One guide notes that no reliable official figure is documented for Reels or Shorts. **[VENDOR, conflicting]** — [ClipSpeed](https://www.clipspeed.ai/blog/video-resolution-guide-shorts-reels-tiktok.html); [Takes](https://gettakes.app/short-video-specs); [Portrait AI cheat sheet](https://portrait.spectatr.ai/blog/video-specs-cheat-sheet)
- File-size caps: Reels is often listed at 4 GB, and TikTok mobile at about 287.6 MB. Sources conflict. **[VENDOR]** — [ChangeThisFile](https://changethisfile.com/blog/video-for-social-media)
- Loudness: one guide recommends "loudness near −14 LUFS" with no clipping. No platform documentation on normalization was found. **[VENDOR]** — [LoopDesk](https://loopdesk.ai/blog/how-to-optimize-video-for-every-platform-2026)
- YouTube Shorts music: up to 90 s of most songs in a 3-min Short. Royalty-free YouTube Audio Library tracks are not claimed. Shorts over 1 min with any active claim are blocked. **[OFFICIAL]** — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877?hl=en)

### Inferences
Recommended export master for the explainer:
- **1080 × 1920, 9:16, progressive, 30 fps** constant frame rate. Animate at 30 fps, or 24 fps conformed to 30. 60 fps only if there is fast camera motion.
- **H.264 High profile, MP4, about 12–16 Mbps VBR** (2-pass if available). The upper range survives platform re-encoding better for flat-color animation and thin text. **Avoid fine gradients and 1-px lines**, which band and shimmer after recompression.
- **AAC-LC, 48 kHz, stereo, ≥256 kbps.**
- **Loudness: about −14 LUFS integrated, true peak ≤ −1 dBTP.** Keep narration clearly above music (music ducked about 15–20 dB under voice). This is an inference from general streaming practice plus one guide; there is no platform spec.
- **Use original or royalty-free music only**, to avoid Content ID blocks on YouTube Shorts over 1 min and rights issues when cross-posting.
- **Upload natively, without the watermark of another platform.** Instagram emphasizes original content and promotes Edits for watermark-free export. Export a clean master and post the same file to each platform.

### Gaps
- No official 2025–2026 upload bitrate or loudness/normalization targets were found for organic Reels, TikTok or Shorts.
- The current TikTok maximum upload length and file size were not verified from an official page in this research.

## 9. Translation: concrete specs and rules for the 90-second, 9:16 endodontic placebo explainer (Instagram-first)

### Takeaway
Ninety seconds is officially recommendable on Reels and performs close to the best length bracket in 2026 industry data. Optimize for **average watch time and sends per reach** (Mosseri). Make the **first ~30 s a complete lesson**. Narrate at about **160–175 wpm (~220–240 words)**, burn in captions inside the **combined safe core (about x 65–888, y 288–1248)**, and show **visible citations**. Near-universal low JAMA-benchmark scores in dental short-form make citations a clear differentiator.

### Cited Findings
- 3-min Reels are recommendable to non-followers (Jan 2025). Trial reels allow testing with non-followers. **[OFFICIAL]** — [Instagram Creators blog](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/the-latest-with-instagram)
- Mosseri's priority signals are watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach, with sends weighted more for non-follower reach. **[OFFICIAL, via trade press]** — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-insights-2025/738034/)
- 60–90 s Reels: median views 9,790 vs. 10,374 for 45–60 s; engagement rate 0.30% vs. 0.35% (H1 2026). **[INDUSTRY DATA]** — [Socialinsider](https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/instagram-reels-length/)
- TikTok official: hook in the first 3–6 s, every hook needs a payoff, sound-on, 9:16, ≥720p. **[OFFICIAL]** — [TikTok SMB Creative Playbook (PDF)](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/SMB_Creative_Playbook_External.pdf)
- Comprehension is robust up to about 2× normal speech (≈275 wpm ceiling); normal speech is about 150 wpm. **[PEER-REVIEWED]** — [Murphy et al. (PDF)](https://castel.psych.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/111/2021/11/ACP-Lecture-Speed-Murphy-2021-in-press.pdf)
- 99% of TikTok and Instagram pulp-therapy videos had low JAMA scores, and only 3.8% of health TikToks in one 2026 sample cited literature. **[PEER-REVIEWED]** — [RDE 2025](https://rde.ac/journal/view.php?doi=10.5395%2Frde.2025.50.e26); [JMIR Form Res 2026](https://formative.jmir.org/2026/1/e79978)

### Inferences
**Spec sheet (rules marked as inferences where not directly evidenced)**

| Parameter | Spec | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Length | 85–90 s master, plus a 45–60 s cut-down for testing | Official ≤3-min recommendability; Socialinsider brackets |
| Hook window | Everything about the hook lands in 0–3 s; payoff promised by ~6 s | TikTok official 3–6 s; swipe clustering at start |
| Hook channels | Moving visual + 3–6-word on-screen headline + narration from frame 1 | Inference (sound-on/off mix) |
| Hook content | The lesson's central paradox, not a side teaser | Inference from 2024 npj curiosity caveat |
| Early-leaver design | Core takeaway complete by ~30 s | STEM preprint (~27 s average watch); 55% non-completion |
| Segments | 4 labelled beats of ~20 s; visual state change every ~2–4 s, tied to narration | Mayer segmenting/signaling; pacing is inference |
| Narration | 160–175 wpm, ~220–240 words; clear, warm, slightly fast; no dead air at start | Murphy et al.; Guo (speed and enthusiasm); TikTok "clear and concise" |
| Captions | Burned in; 2–5-word chunks; ≤2 lines; ~54–72 px bold sans with stroke or box; band y ≈ 1000–1240 | Safe-zone data; font size is inference |
| Safe core | Keep all text and key visuals within x 65–888, y 288–1248 (avoid the right rail and bottom 672 px) | Third-party combined overlays; Meta ads 14/35/6% |
| Citation chips | Author/journal/year/design on screen ≥2.5–3 s whenever a claim is made; full references, date and disclosure in caption | JAMA benchmark gap in dental short-form |
| Presenter ID | Name and credentials within first 5 s or on end card | JAMA authorship criterion |
| Ending | Loop-back frame matching frame 1; ≤3–4 s soft CTA that asks for a send ("Send to your co-resident") | Sends per reach (Mosseri); tail-end swipe clustering |
| Series | "Placebo in Endo 1/N" branding; consistent template | Instagram "post original content consistently"; series effect is inference |
| Testing | Trial reels with 2 hook variants; judge on average watch time, % watched past 3 s, sends per reach | Instagram official tools/metrics |
| Video export | 1080 × 1920, 30 fps CFR, H.264 High, ~12–16 Mbps, MP4 | Vendor consensus; bitrate is inference within the reported range |
| Audio export | AAC 48 kHz ≥256 kbps; about −14 LUFS integrated, ≤ −1 dBTP; music 15–20 dB under voice | Inference (no platform spec) |
| Music | Original or royalty-free only | YouTube 3-min Shorts Content ID block |
| Cross-posting | Same clean master to Reels, TikTok and Shorts; no watermarks | Instagram originality guidance; Edits watermark-free |

**Teaching-goal interactions (inferences):**
- Platform pressure for constant motion conflicts with Mayer's **coherence** principle. Make every visual change explanatory, such as building the mechanism diagram step by step, rather than decorative stock motion.
- The algorithm rewards sends, so give clinicians a **shareable, citable "unit"**: one clinical rule plus one reference. This also matches retrieval-friendly teaching (a single memorable claim).
- Curiosity hooks help memory for the **answer** to the hooked question (Gruber 2014). They may not help for unrelated facts presented alongside (npj 2024). Make sure the most important teaching point **is** the answer to the hook.
- Captions serve both sound-off viewing and dual coding for learners. Keep captions verbatim with narration, keep separate on-screen labels to keywords and numbers only, and avoid paragraph text, to limit redundancy-driven overload.

### Gaps
- Benchmarks for "good" average watch time or completion on a 90 s educational Reel are not published by Instagram, and no independent dataset was found.
- No evidence was found specific to **professional or clinician audiences** on short-form (saves/sends drivers, preferred length, response to citations). The spec above extrapolates from general and patient-facing data.
- Official organic safe-zone pixels, bitrate and LUFS targets remain unpublished. Verify the final export on an actual iPhone/Android in the Reels, TikTok and Shorts players before posting.
