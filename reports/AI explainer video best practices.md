# Code the science, generate only the people

The evidence points one way for a 90-second vertical explainer aimed at endodontists. It should be a tightly segmented narrated animation in which every visual change explains something. Each number should appear as "X of 100" and then hold still for 3–4 seconds, and any on-screen words should be short and sit next to the thing they name. By late 2026, generative video models can make 8–30-second 9:16 shots with native audio, reference-image consistency and conversational re-editing. They still cannot be trusted with the three things this video must get exactly right: tooth anatomy, the opioid receptor, and data. In 2026 benchmarks, the best medical video model passed strict clinical review only 28% of the time, and the best video model still got about one word in four wrong in rendered text. The best image model in a 2026 anatomy study drew structures fully correctly only 37% of the time. The build that works on a CPU-only container is mostly code: programmatic motion graphics (Remotion, GSAP, D3) timed to Kokoro's word timestamps carry every number, caption and chart. A hand-built SVG molar carries the anatomy. Mol* renders of real Protein Data Bank (PDB) structures carry the mechanism: an enkephalin analogue bound to human MOR (8EFQ), then naloxone bound to human MOR (9PXU). ElevenLabs credits go to at most two short, stylized human shots (the fearful patient and the clinician's manner), for roughly $5–25. The larger accuracy risk is not the pixels but statistical overreach in the script: "nothing beat placebo at 48 h" does not mean placebo equals the drugs, an odds ratio of 0.39 does not mean "61% less likely," the 50% pain reduction comes from 44 patients, and Pak & White describe pain in treated patients, not untreated natural history.

## Five design rules survive independent replication, and expertise trims the rest

Independent meta-analyses put the average benefit of multimedia design at **g = 0.38**, about half the medians Mayer reports from his own lab. That benefit concentrates in this video's exact conditions: **system-paced media** (g = 0.41, against 0.27 when learners control the pace) and **complex, high-element-interactivity material** (g = 0.70, against 0.20 for simple material) ([Noetel et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543211052329)). An autoplay Reel that explains opioid-mediated placebo analgesia and six study results is both system-paced and complex. The rules below are the ones that hold up outside Mayer's lab, restated as decisions.

| Rule | Best independent evidence | Decision for this video |
|---|---|---|
| Contiguity | g = 0.74; spatial d = 0.72, temporal d = 0.78 ([Noetel 2022](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543211052329)) | Put labels on the object, never in a legend. A spoken word and its visual land on the same frame |
| Signaling | g = 0.43, consistent for high and low prior knowledge; animated cues d = 0.43 ([Noetel 2022](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543211052329)) | One animated highlight, arrow or underline, synced to each key word |
| Coherence | Removing seductive details g = 0.33; they hurt most when persistently on screen ([Noetel 2022](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543211052329)) | No music bed, stock B-roll, floating molars or persistent logos |
| Modality | d = 0.93 in system-paced presentations; the effect disappears when learners self-pace ([Noetel 2022](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543211052329)) | Narration explains. The screen shows pictures, numbers and labels of 3 words or fewer |
| Segmenting | Retention g = 0.32, transfer g = 0.36 ([Noetel 2022](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543211052329)) | Seven scenes of 10–18 s, each with a visual reset and a ~0.5 s pause |
| Representational animation | Animation vs static d = 0.37; representational 0.40, decorative 0.05 ([Noetel 2022](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543211052329)) | Animate only processes: pain falling, a ligand swap, a pathway firing |
| Redundancy is weak | Mayer 2021 median d = 0.10. Key words added to narration help (g = 0.29). Short text placed beside the graphic helps ([Mayer 2023](https://www.unh.edu/teaching-learning-resource-hub/sites/default/files/media/2023-06/itow-research-based-principles-for-designing-multimedia-instruction-mayer.pdf); [Mayer & Johnson 2008](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.100.2.380)) | Burned-in captions are fine. Don't stack full sentences, labels and narration at once |
| Natural frequencies, absolute numbers | Natural frequencies beat percentages (SMD 0.69). Health professionals are no better than consumers ([Akl et al., Cochrane](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD006776_using-different-statistical-formats-presenting-health-information)) | Every rate as "X of 100," with one denominator throughout. Odds ratios and mean differences go only in the citation chip |
| Static final frames | Animated icon arrays degraded comprehension ([Zikmund-Fisher 2012](https://jmir.org/2012/4/e106)). Small multiples beat animation for comparisons ([Robertson 2008](https://faculty.cc.gatech.edu/~stasko/papers/infovis08-anim.pdf)) | Animate the reveal, then hold a static frame for 3–4 s |

Expertise trims specific supports rather than overturning the rules. In Kalyuga's experiments, narrated walk-throughs of diagrams helped novices but became redundant, and eventually harmful, as learners gained expertise. Animation favours high-knowledge learners (d = 0.49 vs −0.16 for low-knowledge learners) ([Kalyuga 2007](https://www.uky.edu/~gmswan3/EDC608/Kalyuga2007_Article_ExpertiseReversalEffectAndItsI.pdf); [Noetel 2022](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543211052329)). But the pooled meta-analytic data show **no significant moderation by prior knowledge**. And endodontists are experts in pulp biology, not in placebo neurobiology or odds ratios. The calibration that follows is to drop any glossary or pre-training segment, never narrate what the picture already shows (narrate the claim and what it implies instead), keep exactly one inline definition (placebo *response* versus placebo *effect*), and keep signaling and contiguity, which cost nothing.

Presence and voice evidence also points toward drawing rather than faces. An on-screen instructor adds a small retention benefit and no transfer benefit ([Beege et al. 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11423-024-10391-9)). A static speaker image scores d = 0.19 with three negative tests ([Mayer 2023](https://www.unh.edu/teaching-learning-resource-hub/sites/default/files/media/2023-06/itow-research-based-principles-for-designing-multimedia-instruction-mayer.pdf)). Drawing while explaining helps, and once drawing is present, instructor visibility adds nothing ([Fiorella et al. 2019](https://ecrhub.org/publications/instructor-presence-video-lectures-role-dynamic-drawings-eye-contact-and-instructor)). So use "drawn-on" line builds instead of a presenter.

On voice, modern text-to-speech matched human narration on exam scores in a 447-learner RCT, at a small cost in rated learning experience ([Netland et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2024.105164); [Craig & Schroeder 2019](https://doi.org/10.1177/0735633118802877)). The recent studies that still favour human voices used synthetic voices rated far less natural. Kokoro is therefore defensible on two conditions: it must sound warm and moderately brisk, and it must say "naloxone," "periapical" and "NSAIDs" correctly. An expert audience will catch a mispronunciation as a negative social cue.

## Ninety seconds is recommendable, but the first thirty must teach on their own

Instagram made Reels up to 3 minutes recommendable to non-followers in January 2025 ([Instagram Creators](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/the-latest-with-instagram)). Adam Mosseri names three signals as most important: **average watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach**, with sends weighted "slightly" more for reaching new viewers ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-insights-2025/738034/)). First-half-2026 brand data put 60–90-second Reels at 9,790 median views, against 10,374 for the best bracket (45–60 s) ([Socialinsider](https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/instagram-reels-length/)). So length is not the risk. Attention is: across 1,000+ STEM short videos, average watch time did not exceed about **27 seconds** ([Oshinowo et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.18944)). That means a viewer who leaves at 30 s must still have learned the core idea. It also justifies a 45–60-second cut-down, tested through Instagram's Trial reels.

Hook advice is official but untested. TikTok says the first 3–6 seconds are "crucial" and that "every hook needs a payoff" ([TikTok playbook](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/SMB_Creative_Playbook_External.pdf)), but no peer-reviewed study isolates hook design on short-form platforms. The lab evidence carries a warning. Curiosity improves memory for the *answer* to the question that raised it ([Gruber et al. 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4252494)). But a 2024 study found **worse** memory for academic facts presented after high-curiosity trivia ([npj Science of Learning 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41539-024-00234-w.pdf)). The hook must therefore *be* the lesson's central paradox, delivered at 0.0 s through three channels at once: moving visuals, a 3–6-word text headline, and narration.

Captions are mandatory, but for better reasons than the folklore. The "85% watch muted" figure dates from 2016-era Facebook autoplay. The best-sourced data are still a 2019 survey: **69% watch with sound off in public** ([Forbes on Verizon/Publicis](https://www.forbes.com/sites/tjmccue/2019/07/31/verizon-media-says-69-percent-of-consumers-watching-video-with-sound-off/)). Captioning for second-language viewers is also the single largest effect in the multimedia meta-meta-analysis (comprehension g = 0.99) ([Noetel 2022](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543211052329)), and adding on-screen text to narrated video improved second-language comprehension in a direct test ([Lee & Mayer 2018](https://pure.uos.ac.kr/en/publications/fostering-learning-from-instructional-video-in-a-second-language/)). Much of the global endodontic audience watches in a second language.

No platform publishes official safe zones for organic posts. Combined third-party overlays leave a safe core of about **x 65–888, y 288–1248** on a 1080×1920 frame ([revid.ai](https://www.revid.ai/tiktok-safe-zone)); the right-hand button rail and the bottom ~670 px are covered by app UI. No study supports a "cut every N seconds" rule. What is solid is that comprehension holds until speech approaches roughly 275 words per minute ([Murphy et al.](https://castel.psych.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/111/2021/11/ACP-Lecture-Speed-Murphy-2021-in-press.pdf)). So the pacing rule is a meaningful visual change for each narrated clause, roughly every 2–4 seconds, at about 160–170 words per minute.

Credibility is cheap differentiation. **99% of TikTok and Instagram pulp-therapy videos scored low on the JAMA benchmark**, which rewards authorship, attribution, disclosure and currency ([RDE 2025](https://rde.ac/journal/view.php?doi=10.5395%2Frde.2025.50.e26)). Only 3.8% of health TikToks in one 2026 sample cited any literature ([JMIR Form Res 2026](https://formative.jmir.org/2026/1/e79978)). Visible citation chips, an author credit, a disclosure line and an "evidence as of" date move the video to the top of that distribution, though quality alone does not buy reach. Position the video as a supplement that points to the papers or longer CE: adding video to teaching yields g = 0.80, against 0.28 for replacing teaching with it ([AERA on Noetel 2021](https://www.aera.net/Newsroom/Video-Improves-Learning-in-Higher-Education-A-Systematic-Review)).

Two conclusions follow. First, generators can now show anything *plausible*, but nothing *verifiably correct*. Experts call the output "sexy but wrong" ([Ziman et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.14494v2)). A Duke instructor found that AI video added unprompted molecules and morphing text to an aspirin animation ([Duke DDMC](https://sites.duke.edu/ddmc/2026/02/10/molecular-mess/)). No credible, accurate, mainly generative science explainer was found, and the working pattern is hybrid: generated plates under human-authored vector graphics ([Advids](https://advids.co/case-studies/ringcentral-advids-it-services-ai-generated-video-for-demonstrating-conversation-intelligence-platform)).

Second, the AI that gets dental anatomy right is segmentation, not generation. AI-derived CBCT models raised postgraduates' canal detection from 88% to 99% ([Clin Oral Investig 2026](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00784-026-06897-6)).

On this container, everything except Cycles renders in minutes. Remotion warns that headless Chromium without a GPU slows WebGL and CSS blur, shadow and gradient effects, so those must be precomputed ([Remotion GPU](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/gpu); [Remotion performance](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/performance)).

## Seven scenes, one deterministic engine: rank techniques by what must be exactly right

Rank each technique by the cost of being wrong. Anything that carries a number, a structure or a word must be deterministic and traceable to a source file. Generation is allowed only where the content is a human situation that the studies themselves describe.

The scene plan assumes about 210 words at roughly 165 words per minute, with pauses at scene boundaries and after every number. The core takeaway is complete by 31 s.

| **7. Takeaways** (78–90 s · ~26) | Three-line card plus a "say this" line; the final frame mirrors frame 1 so the loop works; soft "send to your co-resident" | "Most feel much better within a day; about 9 in 10 are pain-free by a week" | Each line must be literally supported by Pak & White (60 of 100 pain-free at 24 h, 89 of 100 at 1 week) |

The guards come from the source records retrieved from PubMed for this report. In Zanjir's network meta-analysis, NSAIDs beat placebo by **21 points on a 0–100 scale at 6–8 h (95% credible interval −34 to −7.6)**, only NSAIDs remained effective at 12 and 24 h, and "at 48 hours, no treatment resulted in significant pain reduction," with very low to moderate confidence in the estimates ([Zanjir et al. 2020, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.joen.2020.07.002)). Pak & White's 81/40/11 prevalences are means across 72 studies of patients receiving root canal treatment, with SDs of 28, 24 and 14 points ([Pak & White 2011, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.joen.2010.12.016)). Nixdorf's figure is the odds of persistent pain, which affected 10% of patients at 6 months: **OR 0.39 (95% CI 0.22–0.67)** for optimism about the procedure ([Nixdorf et al. 2016, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1097/j.pain.0000000000000343)). In Liu's practice-network cohort, anesthesia failed in 16% of patients, and "pre-treatment fear was the only significant psychosocial predictor" ([Liu et al. 2026, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/iej.70171)). Daniali's 50% reduction disappeared when the same information was given without the treatment team's knowledge, and the authors attribute it to the dentist's positive nonverbal behaviour ([Daniali et al. 2024, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/08870446.2024.2392820)), which makes that behaviour the clinician-effect scene's actual teaching point. Levine's naloxone raised pain only in placebo responders after impacted third-molar extraction ([Levine, Gordon & Fields 1978, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0140-6736%2878%2992762-9)).

## Conclusion

For accuracy-critical explainers, the frontier that matters is agent-written deterministic code with a visual critic in the loop, not generated pixels. Remotion's skills and planner-coder-critic research make "AI-made" practical without surrendering correctness. Generators have become a scenery department. The scarce input is verified source data: the six papers' actual tables, PDB coordinates, and a CC BY micro-CT or reference drawing. Anatomy and mechanism should never be prompted into existence.

Endodontics also hands this video an unusually strong, sendable story. Placebo analgesia's opioid component was demonstrated in dental surgery patients, the pain trajectory after treatment explains much of what placebo arms "achieve," and Daniali's open-versus-hidden result turns "placebo" from a sugar pill into a clinician behaviour. None of these design rules has been tested on endodontists. Running the Trial-reel A/B, with residents' comprehension of the "X of 100" claims as the outcome, would produce the first evidence of its kind.
